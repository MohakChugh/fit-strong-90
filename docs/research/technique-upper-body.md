# Technique research: upper body (pull, press, shoulders, arms)

**Scope.** Technique, setup, tempo, breathing, safety and coaching cues for 24 upper-body strength exercises. This feeds the voice coach and the 3D "correct form" and "how not to do it" animations. Lower body, core, stretching and cardio are covered in separate docs. General educational content, not individual medical advice. Researched 2026-10-05. ExRx.net blocks automated fetchers, so the ExRx links were checked against Internet Archive snapshots. PubMed IDs were checked through Europe PMC.

## 1. Conventions

- **Tempo** is `C-H-E-P` in seconds: concentric (lift, pull or press), hold at the end of it, eccentric (lower or return), then pause in the start or stretched position. `2-1-3-0` = lift 2 s, hold 1 s, lower 3 s, no pause.
  - Entries marked *starts at top* (push-ups, dumbbell presses, dips, lying and overhead triceps extensions) begin with the lowering, so the coach counts E first.
- **Mistake angles and distances** are approximate deviations from the correct position, written for animators. 0° means correct or neutral. A phrase like "elbows ~90° from the torso" is an absolute joint angle. Figures assume an adult about 170-180 cm tall. They are not clinical thresholds. Each mistake reads Mistake → Risk → Fix.
- **Neutral spine** means the natural low-back curve is kept and the ribs stay stacked over the pelvis, with no rounding or arching under load. DB = dumbbell.
- **Back & sciatica labels** (same scheme as the mobility/cardio doc):
  - **OK**: suitable as written.
  - **Modify**: use the stated variant, and always use it on irritable days.
  - **Avoid when irritable**: swap in the named alternative until symptoms calm.
  - **Irritable** means leg symptoms that are easily provoked, present at rest, or slow (more than 30 min) to settle.
- **G1-G6** point to the global rules below. Each entry adds only exercise-specific detail.

## 2. Global rules (apply to every exercise)

- **G1. Breathe and brace; never hold your breath.**
  - Exhale through pursed lips during the hard part of the rep, and inhale during the lowering or return.
  - To brace, tighten the whole abdominal wall evenly, as if expecting a light poke, and keep breathing. Bracing stiffens the spine more than drawing the navel in ([Grenier & McGill 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17207676/)).
  - During 1 s holds, keep the air moving with small breaths.
- **G2. End every set 2-3 reps short of failure.**
  - A brief breath-hold becomes almost unavoidable with loads above about 80% of maximum or with sets to failure ([Hackett & Chow 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23222073/)).
  - Even single-arm curls taken to failure drove mean peak arterial pressure to 255/190 mmHg ([MacDougall et al. 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3980383/)).
  - With hypertension, avoid the Valsalva manoeuvre. With severe non-proliferative or unstable proliferative retinopathy, avoid breath-holding, heavy straining and head-down positions ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)).
  - Suggested app behaviour: cue "breathe out" on every lifting phase, and never prescribe sets to failure.
- **G3. Back and sciatica stop rules.**
  - Stop and switch to the named regression if pain spreads into the buttock or leg or goes below the knee, or if tingling, numbness or weakness appears or worsens ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sciatica/)).
  - Seek urgent care and do not train for any of these: sciatica in both legs, severe or worsening weakness or numbness in both legs, numbness around the genitals or anus, or new difficulty controlling the bladder or bowels ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sciatica/)).
  - Common upper-body triggers:
    - loaded or sustained forward bending, such as bent-over rows and flies or floor pick-ups. Unsupported bent-over rows had the highest lumbar load of the rows tested ([Fenwick et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19197209/)).
    - sitting with straight knees while reaching forward, which resembles the slump test used to provoke nerve symptoms ([Majlesi et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18391677/))
    - loaded low-back arching
    - twisting while holding a weight
    - swinging reps
  - Default to supported, seated, machine or cable versions.
- **G4. Handling weights and getting up.**
  - Take and return DBs and bars at thigh height (from a rack or bench). From the floor, hinge with a neutral spine and keep one hand on a bench.
  - Never twist while holding a weight.
  - Never drop DBs from lockout. Bring them back to the thighs.
  - After lying or reclined exercises, sit a few seconds before standing. Some blood-pressure medicines cause exercise-related hypotension ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)).
- **G5. Diabetes.**
  - Follow the user's glucose plan. Beta-blockers can mask hypoglycaemia ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)).
  - With neuropathy or poor balance, use the seated or supported variant of standing exercises.
- **G6. Shoulders.**
  - Up to 36% of resistance-training injuries involve the shoulder, and poor technique is a recurring factor ([Kolber et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20508476/)).
  - A pinch or sharp pain at the front or top of the shoulder means shorten the range or switch variant.
  - Otherwise use the full pain-free range: complete range of motion improves strength gains ([ACSM 2026 position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)).

## 4. Back (pulls)

### `lat-pulldown` — Lat pulldown
- **Muscles:** Primary: latissimus dorsi, teres major. Secondary: biceps, rhomboids, middle/lower trapezius, rear deltoid.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a tall cable station with a long bar on a high pulley and a seat with knee rollers.
  - Set: feet flat, knees ~90°, thigh pad snug on the thighs ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/158/seated-lat-pulldown/)). Overhand grip slightly wider than the shoulders. A neutral handle is the most shoulder-friendly ([NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/the-biomechanics-of-the-lat-pulldown-muscles-grip-and-form)). Stand to grip, then sit.
- **Execution:**
  1. Sit tall, thighs locked under the pad.
  2. Lean back slightly and brace.
  3. Shoulders down, then drive your elbows to your ribs.
  4. Bring the bar to the top of your chest.
  5. Let it rise slowly to straight arms.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (pull 2 s, hold 1 s, return 3 s). From straight arms to the bar at the upper chest. Stop when the elbows start moving back instead of down (ACE).
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you pull, inhale on the return (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: lats (under the armpits), mid-back, biceps. Not: shoulder pinch, neck strain, low-back ache, hand tingling.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Torso swings ~25-35° back past the set lean, lumbar arching → momentum and low-back extension load → fix the lean at ~10-20° (ACE max 30°), lighter stack.
  - Bar behind the neck: neck flexes ~25-30°, shoulders at end range → shoulder and neck stress, no benefit ([Sperandei et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19855327/)) → pull in front of the face.
  - Shoulders shrugged ~3-5 cm throughout → upper-trap and neck tension → shoulders down first.
  - Bar dragged to the belly, elbows ~15° behind the torso, shoulders rolling forward → front-shoulder stress → stop at the upper chest.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** The pad-locked seat protects the spine; just avoid swinging and arching. If sitting provokes symptoms, use a light standing staggered-stance cable pulldown (an ACE variation).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: lighter stack, neutral handle, single-arm cable pulldown. Harder: 4 s return, then assisted-pull-up.
- **Narration cues:** "Shoulders down, then pull." · "Elbows to your ribs." · "Bar to your collarbone." · "Chest tall, no swinging."
- **YouTube search:** `lat pulldown proper form physical therapist common mistakes`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Seated Lat Pulldown](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/158/seated-lat-pulldown/) · [NASM: Lat pulldown biomechanics](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/the-biomechanics-of-the-lat-pulldown-muscles-grip-and-form) · [Sperandei et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19855327/)

### `assisted-pull-up` — Assisted pull-up (machine)
- **Muscles:** Primary: latissimus dorsi. Secondary: teres major, biceps, brachialis, rhomboids, lower trapezius.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a tall tower with steps, top handles, a moving knee pad (or foot bar) and a stack, often labelled "assisted chin/dip".
  - More counterweight means more help, the opposite of other machines. Pick enough for 8-12 smooth reps (G2).
  - Mounting: climb, grip, then kneel one knee at a time and let the pad sink slowly ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/LatissimusDorsi/AsPullupOpenKneeling)). Grip overhand slightly wider than the shoulders, or neutral. Dismount from the top, one foot at a time.
- **Execution:**
  1. Hang with straight arms, ribs down.
  2. Set your shoulders down and back.
  3. Pull your elbows down to your sides.
  4. Rise until your chin is level with your hands.
  5. Lower slowly to straight arms.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (pull 2 s, hold 1 s, lower 3 s). From straight arms with the shoulders set to the chin level with the bar ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/191/pull-ups/)).
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down. Needing to hold your breath means add counterweight (G1, G2).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: lats, mid-back, biceps. Not: shoulder pinch, elbow pain, neck strain, low-back arching, leg tingling.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Kipping: hips flex ~30-40° then snap open, body swings ±15° → jerky shoulder load, lumbar flexion and extension → more assistance, slower reps.
  - Arched reach: ~15-25° lumbar extension, chin jutting ~5-8 cm to clear the bar → back and neck strain → ribs down, stop at hand height.
  - Half reps: elbows stay bent ~60-90° at the bottom → less range and strength → straight arms every rep.
  - Shrugged hang: shoulders ~5 cm up toward the ears, head forward → passive shoulder hanging, neck tension → shoulders gently down.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** The spine stays unloaded if the ribs stay down. Mount and dismount without twisting. If hanging or kneeling provokes leg symptoms, use lat-pulldown.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: more counterweight, lat-pulldown. Harder: less counterweight each week, 4 s lowering, bodyweight pull-up.
- **Narration cues:** "More weight means more help." · "Shoulders down, elbows to ribs." · "Chin to your hands." · "Slow on the way down."
- **YouTube search:** `assisted pull up machine how to use physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Machine-assisted Pull-up (kneeling)](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/LatissimusDorsi/AsPullupOpenKneeling) · [ACE: Pull-ups](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/191/pull-ups/)

### `seated-cable-row` — Seated cable row (straight-back)
- **Muscles:** Primary: latissimus dorsi, middle trapezius, rhomboids. Secondary: rear deltoid, biceps, brachialis.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a long low bench facing a low pulley with an angled foot plate and a V-shaped close-grip handle.
  - Set: sit forward with your feet on the plate. Bend your knees more to reach the handle with a neutral spine, then slide your hips back until your arms are straight and the stack lifts ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/CBStraightBackSeatedRow)). Keep the knees bent ≥20-30°.
- **Execution:**
  1. Sit tall, knees soft, chest up.
  2. Brace and keep your torso still.
  3. Pull the handle to your upper belly.
  4. Squeeze your shoulder blades for one second.
  5. Straighten your arms slowly; only your shoulder blades slide forward.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (pull 2 s, squeeze 1 s, return 3 s). From straight arms with the torso upright (±10°) until the handle touches the upper abdomen ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/48/seated-row/)).
- **Breathing:** Exhale on the pull, inhale on the return (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: between the shoulder blades, lats, rear shoulders. Not: low-back ache, pain or tingling down a leg (stop, G3), shoulder pinch.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Rounded reach: trunk tips ~30° forward with ~20-30° lumbar flexion at the return → loaded flexion; with straight knees it copies the slump position → hinge no more than 10°, move only the shoulder blades.
  - Rowing with the back: torso ~20-30° behind vertical at the finish, lumbar arching → momentum and extension load → torso still, lighter stack.
  - Locked knees: pelvis tucks under and the low back rounds → more hamstring and sciatic-nerve tension → knees bent.
  - Shrug and flare: shoulders up ~3-5 cm, elbows out to ~70-90° → neck strain, less lat work → elbows ~15-30° from the ribs.
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable**; use chest-supported-row. ExRx's standard "cable seated row" deliberately flexes and extends the low back ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/CBSeatedRow)), so use only the straight-back version.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: chest-supported-row, single-arm. Harder: heavier with the same posture, 2 s squeeze.
- **Narration cues:** "Sit tall, ribs over hips." · "Elbows back past your ribs." · "Squeeze, then reach long." · "Torso stays still."
- **YouTube search:** `seated cable row form physiotherapist neutral spine`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Seated Row (cable)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/48/seated-row/) · [ExRx: Cable Straight Back Seated Row](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/CBStraightBackSeatedRow) · [ExRx: Cable Seated Row (flexing version, for contrast)](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/CBSeatedRow)

### `one-arm-dumbbell-row` — One-arm dumbbell row (bench-supported)
- **Muscles:** Primary: latissimus dorsi, middle trapezius, rhomboids. Secondary: rear deltoid, biceps. The obliques resist rotation.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A flat bench and one DB, placed on the bench end rather than the floor.
  - Set: support knee and hand on the bench, hand under the shoulder and knee under the hip ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/126/single-arm-row/)). Working foot on the floor slightly back and out. Torso near horizontal and level across the shoulders ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/DBBentOverRow)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Hand under your shoulder, knee under your hip, back flat.
  2. Let the dumbbell hang straight down.
  3. Pull it toward your hip pocket.
  4. Stop when your elbow passes your torso, chest square.
  5. Lower slowly to a straight arm.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (pull 2 s, hold 1 s, lower 3 s). From a straight arm until the upper arm is just past horizontal (ExRx). Stop before the trunk has to rotate (ACE).
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you row, inhale as you lower (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: working-side lat and mid-back, rear shoulder. Not: low-back pain, buttock or leg symptoms, shoulder pinch, neck strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Torso twist: chest opens ~20-40° toward the working side → spinal rotation under load → shoulders level, lighter DB.
  - Spine collapse: lumbar rounding ~15-20° (or sagging) with the head craned ~30° → disc and neck load → flat back, eyes on the floor.
  - Rowing to the ear: elbow flares ~70-90°, shoulder shrugs ~3-5 cm → neck strain, less lat work → pull to the hip.
  - Leg heave: hips and knees dip and drive to jerk the weight → spinal jolt → lighter DB, 2 s pull.
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable**; use chest-supported-row. The bench helps, but the hinged posture and floor pick-ups can provoke sciatica (G3, G4). Unsupported bent-over rows carry the highest lumbar load ([Fenwick et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19197209/)).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: chest-supported-row, hand on an incline bench. Harder: heavier DB, 1 s pause.
- **Narration cues:** "Flat back, like a tabletop." · "Pull to your hip pocket." · "Chest stays square." · "Long neck, eyes down."
- **YouTube search:** `single arm dumbbell row bench form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Single-arm Row](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/126/single-arm-row/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Bent-over Row](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/DBBentOverRow) · [Fenwick et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19197209/)

### `chest-supported-row` — Chest-supported row (machine or incline-bench DBs)
- **Muscles:** Primary: middle trapezius, rhomboids, latissimus dorsi. Secondary: rear deltoid, biceps.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a seated row machine (upright chest pad facing two handles), a plate-loaded chest-supported T-bar (lie face-down on an angled pad), or an adjustable bench with DBs.
  - Machine: seat so the handles are about shoulder level. The chest pad lightly touches the chest while straight arms still let the shoulders reach forward ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/168/seated-row/), [ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/LVSeatedRow)).
  - DBs: bench at ~30-45°, top of the breastbone at the pad's edge, DBs hanging clear of the floor ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/DBLyingRow)). Place them beside the bench first.
- **Execution:**
  1. Chest on the pad, feet planted, neck long.
  2. Arms straight, shoulder blades spread.
  3. Pull your elbows back past your ribs.
  4. Squeeze your shoulder blades for one second.
  5. Reach back to straight arms.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (pull 2 s, squeeze 1 s, return 3 s). From straight arms with the blades forward until the elbows are just behind the torso, lower chest still on the pad (ExRx).
- **Breathing:** Exhale on the pull, inhale on the return. The pad restricts the chest, so keep breaths small but continuous (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: between the shoulder blades, rear shoulders. Not: low-back strain (expect almost none), neck strain, shoulder pinch, pad pressing on the belly.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Chest peels ~5-10 cm off the pad with ~15° of lumbar arch to heave → support lost → lighter load, "chest glued to the pad".
  - Neck crane: neck extends ~20-30° to look forward → neck strain → chin slightly tucked.
  - Shrug: shoulders up ~3-5 cm at the finish → upper-trap dominance → shoulders down and back.
  - Pad set too far: torso rounds ~20° forward to reach the handles → loaded flexion → move the pad closer.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** The default row for back pain or sciatica, because the pad carries the torso. A steeper bench or a towel eases belly pressure. Get on and off without twisting (G4).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: lighter, single-arm, seated band row. Harder: heavier, 2 s squeeze.
- **Narration cues:** "Chest stays on the pad." · "Elbows back, squeeze." · "Shoulders away from your ears." · "Reach long on the way back."
- **YouTube search:** `chest supported row incline bench dumbbell form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Seated Row (machine)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/168/seated-row/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Lying Row](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/DBLyingRow) · [ExRx: Lever Seated Row](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/BackGeneral/LVSeatedRow)

### `face-pull` — Face pull (cable, rope)
- **Muscles:** Primary: rear deltoid, middle trapezius, rhomboids. Secondary: infraspinatus, teres minor, lower trapezius.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: any adjustable cable tower (a pulley on a pin-locked track) with the rope attachment.
  - Set: pulley at about eye level ([NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/face-pull)). Overhand grip, thumbs toward you. Step back until the cable is taut. Use a staggered stance, or sit on a bench facing the pulley (safer for the back).
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand tall, arms straight, ribs down.
  2. Pull the rope toward your eyes.
  3. Keep your elbows high, level with your shoulders.
  4. Pull the rope apart; hands finish beside your ears.
  5. Pause, then straighten your arms slowly.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (pull 2 s, hold 1 s, return 3 s). From straight arms to hands beside the ears, with the elbows at shoulder height and the upper arms at right angles to the trunk ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidPosterior/CBStandingRearDeltRowRope)).
- **Breathing:** Exhale on the pull, inhale on the return (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: rear shoulders, between the shoulder blades. Not: neck strain, shoulder pinch, low-back arching.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Body-weight lean: torso ~20-30° back with lumbar arching to drag the stack → low-back strain → lighter load, upright torso, staggered stance.
  - Dropped elbows: elbows ~45° from the trunk, rope to the chin → becomes a lat-dominant row (ExRx) → elbows level with the shoulders.
  - Shrug: shoulders up ~3-5 cm → neck tension → shoulders down.
  - Head poke: chin ~5 cm forward to meet the rope → neck strain → bring the rope to your face.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Loads are light; the main back risk is leaning back. Stand staggered or sit.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: band face pull, seated. Harder: half-kneeling, slower return.
- **Narration cues:** "Rope to your eyes." · "Elbows high." · "Pull the rope apart." · "Shoulders down, ribs down."
- **YouTube search:** `face pull proper form physical therapist rope cable`
- **Sources:** [NASM: Face Pull](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/face-pull) · [ExRx: Cable Rear Delt Row (rope)](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidPosterior/CBStandingRearDeltRowRope)

## 5. Chest (presses, flies, push-ups, dips)

### `machine-chest-press` — Machine chest press
- **Muscles:** Primary: pectoralis major. Secondary: front deltoid, triceps, serratus anterior.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a seated machine with an upright backrest and two handles at chest height on lever arms. Some have a foot lever.
  - Seat: handles at mid-chest, about nipple level ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/188/seated-chest-press/), [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/chest-press-machine)).
  - Start position: handles no further back than the front of the chest. If there is a foot lever, use it to bring the handles forward and to park them ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/LVChestPress)).
  - Grip: neutral handles with the elbows ~45° from the body are friendliest.
- **Execution:**
  1. Back on the pad, feet flat.
  2. Shoulder blades gently back and down.
  3. Press until your arms are almost straight.
  4. Keep your shoulder blades on the pad.
  5. Return slowly to chest level.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press 2 s, return 3 s). From the handles at chest level to the elbows straight but not snapped, shoulder blades still on the pad (ACE).
- **Breathing:** Exhale on the press, inhale on the return (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: chest, front shoulders, triceps. Not: front-shoulder pain, low-back arching, elbow pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Seat too low: handles ~8-10 cm above the nipple line, elbows ~90° out, shoulders shrugged → impingement-type stress → raise the seat.
  - Start too deep: elbows ~10-15 cm behind the plane of the back → front-shoulder and pec strain → handles no deeper than the chest.
  - Arching: low back ~15-25° off the pad, hips up → lumbar extension load → glutes and upper back on the pad, lighter.
  - Shoulder blades lift ~3-5 cm off the pad at lockout → less stable shoulder (ACE) → stop as the blades start to move.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** The fixed path and full support make this the default press for back pain. If the stack forces an arch, it is too heavy.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: lighter, single-arm, incline push-up. Harder: 4 s return, then dumbbell-bench-press.
- **Narration cues:** "Handles at mid-chest." · "Shoulder blades stay back." · "Soft elbows at the end." · "Slow return."
- **YouTube search:** `chest press machine seat height setup proper form`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Seated Chest Press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/188/seated-chest-press/) · [NASM: Chest Press Machine](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/chest-press-machine) · [ExRx: Lever Chest Press](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/LVChestPress)

### `dumbbell-bench-press` — Dumbbell bench press (flat)
- **Muscles:** Primary: pectoralis major. Secondary: front deltoid, triceps.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A flat bench and a pair of DBs.
  - Set: feet flat on the floor, or on a low step if needed to keep the back neutral. Head, shoulders and buttocks stay on the bench ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/19/chest-press/)).
  - Getting into position: sit with the DBs on your thighs, then lie back keeping them tight to the chest, using the knees to help ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/DBBenchPress)).
  - Grip: palms toward the feet, or turned ~45° inward, which is kinder to the shoulders.
- **Execution:**
  1. Lie back; shoulder blades down and back.
  2. Start with straight arms over your shoulders.
  3. Lower the dumbbells to the sides of your chest.
  4. Keep your elbows about 45 degrees from your body.
  5. Press up and slightly in.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press 2 s, lower 3 s, no bounce). *Starts at top.* Down until the DBs are beside the chest with a mild stretch (ExRx), back up to straight arms.
- **Breathing:** Inhale as you lower, exhale as you press, never hold at the bottom (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: chest, front shoulders, triceps. Not: front-shoulder pain, low-back pain, wrist pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - T-arms: elbows ~90° from the torso, DBs over the neck → front-shoulder stress → elbows ~45°: "arrow, not T".
  - Big arch: low back ~5-8 cm off the bench, hips rising → lumbar compression → feet on the floor or a step, ribs down.
  - Over-stretch: DBs ~5-10 cm below the chest → pec and shoulder strain → stop at chest level.
  - Dumping: dropping the DBs to the sides from lockout or twisting to the floor → shoulder and back strain → chest, then thighs, then rock up (G4).
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Getting into and out of position is the riskiest part, so use loads you can bring to the thighs easily. If the rock-up provokes symptoms, use machine-chest-press.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: machine-chest-press, neutral grip. Harder: heavier DBs, single-arm press.
- **Narration cues:** "Shoulder blades down and back." · "Elbows at forty-five." · "Lower slowly to your chest." · "Press up and together."
- **YouTube search:** `dumbbell bench press form how to get dumbbells into position safely`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Chest Press (dumbbells)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/19/chest-press/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Bench Press](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/DBBenchPress)

### `incline-dumbbell-press` — Incline dumbbell press
- **Muscles:** Primary: pectoralis major, clavicular (upper) head. Secondary: front deltoid, triceps.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - An adjustable bench with numbered notches. Back pad at ~30°, no more than 45°.
  - 30° gave the most upper-chest EMG activity, and angles above 45° shifted work to the front deltoid ([Rodríguez-Ridao et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505/)). ACE lists 45-60°.
  - Raise the seat pad one notch to stop sliding. Mount from the thighs as you lean back ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralClavicular/DBInclineBenchPress)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Lean back; shoulder blades down and back.
  2. Dumbbells beside your upper chest, elbows about 45 degrees.
  3. Press up until your arms are straight over your shoulders.
  4. Lower slowly to your upper chest.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press 2 s, lower 3 s). *Starts at top.* Down to the sides of the upper chest, back up to straight arms over the shoulders (not over the face).
- **Breathing:** Inhale down, exhale up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: upper chest, front shoulders, triceps. Not: front-shoulder pain, low-back arching, neck strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Bench at 45-60°: torso nearly upright, so it becomes a shoulder press → front-deltoid dominance → ~30°.
  - Elbows ~90° out with the DBs lowered toward the neck → shoulder stress → elbows ~45°, lower to the upper chest.
  - Slide and arch: hips ~5-10 cm forward, low back arched ~15-20° → lumbar stress → raise the seat notch, buttocks back.
  - Chin pokes ~5 cm forward, or the head drives into the pad → neck strain → head neutral.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** The pad supports the spine; watch for sliding and arching. If mounting hurts, use machine-chest-press.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: machine-chest-press, incline push-up. Harder: heavier DBs, single-arm.
- **Narration cues:** "Bench low, about thirty degrees." · "Elbows at forty-five." · "Press up over your shoulders." · "Slow to the upper chest."
- **YouTube search:** `incline dumbbell press bench angle form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Rodríguez-Ridao et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505/) · [ACE: Incline Chest Press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/25/incline-chest-press/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Incline Bench Press](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralClavicular/DBInclineBenchPress)

### `cable-fly` — Standing cable fly
- **Muscles:** Primary: pectoralis major. Secondary: front deltoid, pectoralis minor, serratus anterior.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a cable crossover, which is two tall towers with adjustable pulleys joined by an overhead bar.
  - Set: pulleys at shoulder height or slightly above, with D-handles ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/160/standing-chest-fly/), [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/cable-crossover)). Take one handle, then the other, and step forward into a staggered stance. Slight hip hinge (~10-15°), elbows bent ~15-20° and fixed.
- **Execution:**
  1. Staggered stance, chest proud.
  2. Arms open, elbows softly bent.
  3. Hug the handles together in front of your chest.
  4. Squeeze for one second; hands meet, not cross.
  5. Open slowly to chest level.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (close 2 s, squeeze 1 s, open 3 s). From hands level with the chest, never behind the armpit line ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/163/standing-incline-cable-flyes/)), to hands together in front of the lower chest.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as your hands come together, inhale as you open (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: chest, front shoulders. Not: pulling pain at the front of the shoulder when open, low-back strain, elbow pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Pressing, not flying: elbows bend from ~20° to ~90° and back → becomes a press → lock the elbow angle.
  - Over-stretch: hands ~20-30 cm behind the body line → front-shoulder and pec strain → stop at chest level.
  - Hunch: trunk flexed ~30-45° with a rounded back → more shoulder stress (ACE) and spinal flexion → upright torso.
  - Shrug at the squeeze: shoulders up ~3 cm and rolling forward → neck tension → shoulders down.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** The cables pull you backward; the staggered stance prevents arching. Handle the cables without twisting. When irritable, use the pec deck (seated, back supported).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: pec deck, single-arm fly. Harder: high-to-low or low-to-high angles, 4 s opening.
- **Narration cues:** "Hug a big tree." · "Elbows soft and fixed." · "Squeeze your chest." · "Open only to chest level."
- **YouTube search:** `cable chest fly form shoulder safe common mistakes`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Standing Chest Fly](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/160/standing-chest-fly/) · [ACE: Standing Incline Cable Flyes](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/163/standing-incline-cable-flyes/) · [NASM: Cable Crossover](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/cable-crossover)

### `push-ups` — Push-up
- **Muscles:** Primary: pectoralis major. Secondary: front deltoid, triceps, serratus anterior. The abdominals and glutes hold the plank.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Floor or mat. Hands slightly wider than the shoulders and level with the chest ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/BWPushup)). Body straight from head to heels.
  - Sore wrists: grip DB handles ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/)).
  - Incline version: hands on a wall, bench or racked bar. The higher the hands, the easier.
- **Execution:**
  1. Hands just wider than your shoulders.
  2. Brace your belly and squeeze your glutes.
  3. Lower your chest toward the floor, elbows about 45 degrees.
  4. Stop just above the floor, body still straight.
  5. Push the floor away.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (push 2 s, lower 3 s). *Starts at top.* Down until the chest is ~2-5 cm off the floor, back up to straight arms.
- **Breathing:** Inhale down, exhale up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: chest, triceps, front shoulders, abs. Not: low-back ache from sagging, wrist pain, shoulder pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Sag: pelvis ~5-10 cm below the head-heel line with ~15-25° lumbar extension → low-back extension load → brace and squeeze the glutes, or raise the hands.
  - Pike: hips ~10-15 cm above the line → shortened range → straight line.
  - T-arms: elbows ~90° out, hands level with the face → shoulder stress → elbows ~45°.
  - Head drop: neck flexed ~30°, nose reaching the floor first → neck strain and short range → chest leads.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.**
  - Ordinary push-ups load the spine modestly; ballistic or clapping versions load it more ([Freeman et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16540847/)), so avoid those.
  - Use the incline height that keeps the body straight.
  - Feet-elevated push-ups are head-down, so avoid them with severe retinopathy (G2).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: wall, then bench, then knee push-up (check for sagging). Harder: floor push-up, 4 s lowering.
- **Narration cues:** "One straight line, head to heels." · "Elbows at forty-five." · "Chest to the floor, not your chin." · "Push the floor away."
- **YouTube search:** `push up proper form physical therapist incline progression`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Push-up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/) · [ExRx: Push-up](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/BWPushup) · [Freeman et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16540847/)

### `assisted-dips` — Assisted dip (machine)
- **Muscles:** Primary: triceps with an upright torso, or pectoralis major with a forward lean. Secondary: front deltoid, pectoralis minor.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: usually the same tower as the assisted pull-up, with dip handles at waist height, a knee pad and a counterweight. More weight means more help.
  - Mounting: step up, grip the neutral handles about shoulder width apart, straighten your arms, then kneel ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/AsChestDip)).
  - Torso: upright for triceps, or leaning ~20-30° forward for chest ([E3 Rehab](https://e3rehab.com/how-to-perform-dips/)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Arms straight, shoulders down, chest proud.
  2. Lean forward slightly.
  3. Bend your elbows to lower yourself, with your elbows travelling back.
  4. Stop when your upper arms are about parallel to the floor.
  5. Press back up to straight arms.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press 2 s, lower 3 s). *Starts at top.* Down until the elbows are at ~90° and the upper arms about parallel to the floor (E3 Rehab), or higher if the shoulder complains.
- **Breathing:** Inhale down, exhale up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: triceps, lower chest, front shoulders. Not: sharp front-shoulder pain, breastbone or rib pain (the two most common dip complaints, per E3 Rehab), elbow pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Too deep: shoulders sink below the elbows and roll forward → shoulder extension near its limit. Bench dips reached ~88°, the participants' full tested range, and bar dips used ~88% of it ([McKenzie et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9603242/)) → stop at parallel.
  - Shrug: shoulders up ~4-6 cm at the bottom → shoulder and neck strain → long neck.
  - Elbow flare: elbows ~45-60° out to the sides → elbow and shoulder stress → elbows travel back.
  - Kneeling arch: ~15-20° lumbar extension → back strain → ribs down, glutes on.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Spine load is minimal if the ribs stay down; mount and dismount without twisting. Don't substitute bench dips, which demand the most shoulder extension.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: more counterweight, shorter range, rope-pushdown. Harder: less counterweight.
- **Narration cues:** "Shoulders down, long neck." · "Elbows back, not out." · "Stop at parallel." · "Press tall."
- **YouTube search:** `assisted dip machine form shoulder safe depth physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Machine-assisted Chest Dip](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/PectoralSternal/AsChestDip) · [E3 Rehab: How to perform dips](https://e3rehab.com/how-to-perform-dips/) · [McKenzie et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9603242/)

## 6. Shoulders

### `machine-shoulder-press` — Machine shoulder press
- **Muscles:** Primary: front deltoid. Secondary: side deltoid, triceps, upper pectoralis, trapezius.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: an upright seat with a tall backrest and two handles beside the head on lever arms, often with horizontal and neutral grips.
  - Set: seat height so the handles start level with or just above the shoulders ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/186/seated-shoulder-press/)). Back flat on the pad. Neutral handles if available, elbows slightly in front of the body ("4 and 8 o'clock"). Avoid an over-wide grip ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidAnterior/LVShoulderPress)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Back and head on the pad.
  2. Hands at shoulder height, elbows slightly forward.
  3. Press up until your arms are almost straight.
  4. Ribs down, back stays on the pad.
  5. Pull the handles down slowly to shoulder height.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press 2 s, lower 3 s). From the shoulders to arms extended but not locked. On the way down, pull with the back muscles rather than letting the weight drop (ACE).
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: front and side shoulders, triceps. Not: pain at the top or front of the shoulder, neck pain, low-back arching.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Arching off the pad: ~15-25° lumbar extension, ribs flaring, upper back ~5 cm off the pad → lumbar compression → back on the pad, lighter.
  - Seat too low: handles ~10 cm below the shoulders, elbows dropping below the handles → forced deep range → raise the seat.
  - Elbows flared straight out with a wide grip → impingement-type stress → elbows slightly forward, neutral handles.
  - Shrug and head poke: shoulders up ~3-5 cm, chin ~5 cm forward at lockout → neck strain → head on the pad.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** The backrest makes this the default overhead press. Pressing without the backrest (an ACE progression) is only for users without back symptoms.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: close neutral grip ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/187/seated-machine-close-grip-shoulder-press/)), landmine-press. Harder: dumbbell-shoulder-press.
- **Narration cues:** "Back flat on the pad." · "Elbows slightly forward." · "Press up, ribs down." · "Pull it down slowly."
- **YouTube search:** `machine shoulder press seat height setup proper form`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Seated Shoulder Press (machine)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/186/seated-shoulder-press/) · [ACE: Close-grip Machine Shoulder Press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/187/seated-machine-close-grip-shoulder-press/) · [ExRx: Lever Shoulder Press](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidAnterior/LVShoulderPress)

### `dumbbell-shoulder-press` — Seated dumbbell shoulder press
- **Muscles:** Primary: front deltoid. Secondary: side deltoid, triceps, upper pectoralis, trapezius.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Set: an adjustable bench with the back pad upright (~80-90°). Head, shoulders and buttocks stay on the pad ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/45/seated-overhead-press/)). Feet flat and wider than the hips.
  - Lift the DBs from the thighs to the shoulders one at a time.
  - Start: wrists stacked over the elbows ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidAnterior/DBShoulderPress)), elbows slightly forward. A neutral grip is kinder to the shoulders.
- **Execution:**
  1. Back on the pad, feet wide.
  2. Dumbbells at shoulder height, wrists over elbows.
  3. Press up until your arms are straight.
  4. Keep your lower back against the pad.
  5. Lower slowly to shoulder height.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press 2 s, lower 3 s). From shoulder or ear level to straight arms with the DBs above the shoulders.
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: shoulders, triceps. Not: low-back pressure, a pinch at the top of the shoulder, neck pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Lean-back press: hips slide ~5-10 cm forward, trunk reclines ~15-25° → lumbar extension load → back on the pad, lighter DBs.
  - Elbows behind the body: elbows ~10 cm behind the ear line at the bottom → front-shoulder stress → elbows ~30° forward.
  - Drifting DBs: wrists ~10-15 cm outside the elbows → shoulder strain → wrists over elbows.
  - Bottom bounce: DBs drop ~10 cm below the shoulders and rebound → shoulder strain → stop at shoulder level.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Seated with back support only. Standing raises deltoid demand but also trunk stability demand ([Saeterbakken & Fimland 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23096062/)). When irritable, use machine-shoulder-press.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: machine-shoulder-press, landmine-press. Harder: heavier DBs, single-arm. Standing only without back symptoms.
- **Narration cues:** "Back on the pad." · "Wrists over elbows." · "Press up, ribs down." · "Control to your shoulders."
- **YouTube search:** `seated dumbbell shoulder press form back support physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Seated Overhead Press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/45/seated-overhead-press/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Shoulder Press](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidAnterior/DBShoulderPress) · [Saeterbakken & Fimland 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23096062/)

### `landmine-press` — Half-kneeling single-arm landmine press
- **Muscles:** Primary: front deltoid, upper pectoralis. Secondary: triceps, serratus anterior. The obliques resist rotation and the glute of the down leg stabilises.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a barbell with one end in a floor swivel sleeve, or wedged into a corner on a towel ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/september-2025/8948/the-ace-workout-builder-for-landmine-training/)). Use small plates so they don't block the press ([strength coach](https://ericcressey.com/strength-exercise-of-the-week-half-kneeling-1-arm-landmine-press/)).
  - Set: half-kneel facing the bar, with the down knee on a pad on the pressing side, the front knee at ~90° and the hips over the down knee. Bar end at the front of the shoulder, elbow under the hand. Lift it there from a box, not the floor (G4).
- **Execution:**
  1. Kneel tall; squeeze the glute of your down leg.
  2. Bar at your shoulder, ribs down.
  3. Press up and forward until your arm is straight.
  4. Let your shoulder blade reach at the top.
  5. Lower slowly back to your shoulder.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press 2 s, lower 3 s). From the bar at the front of the shoulder to a straight arm along the bar's arc, hand in line with the shoulder.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you press, inhale as you lower (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: front shoulder, upper chest, triceps, obliques, glute. Not: a pinch at the top of the shoulder, low-back arching, pain in the kneeling knee.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Arching: low back extends ~15-20°, ribs flare → lumbar extension load → ribs down, glute on.
  - Twisting: trunk rotates ~20-30° to drive the bar → spinal torsion → square hips and shoulders.
  - Pressing across the body: hand ~10-15 cm past the midline → poorer shoulder mechanics → press straight out (strength coach).
  - Elbow drifts behind the body on the way down, shoulder tipping forward → front-shoulder stress → stop at the shoulder.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** The arcing path presses up and forward without full overhead range and with less joint stress than a vertical press (ACE). The shoulder never reaches end range ([Prehab Guys](https://theprehabguys.com/blog/landmine-exercises/)). Half-kneeling removes leg drive and discourages arching. If kneeling hurts, use tall-kneeling or a standing split stance.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: empty bar, two-handed press. Harder: standing split stance, heavier, then dumbbell-shoulder-press.
- **Narration cues:** "Squeeze the down-side glute." · "Ribs down." · "Press up and out, not across." · "Chin tucked."
- **YouTube search:** `half kneeling landmine press form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Landmine training](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/september-2025/8948/the-ace-workout-builder-for-landmine-training/) · [Strength coach: Half-kneeling 1-arm landmine press](https://ericcressey.com/strength-exercise-of-the-week-half-kneeling-1-arm-landmine-press/) · [Prehab Guys (physical therapists): Landmine exercises](https://theprehabguys.com/blog/landmine-exercises/)

### `lateral-raises` — Dumbbell lateral raise
- **Muscles:** Primary: side deltoid. Secondary: front deltoid, supraspinatus, middle/lower trapezius.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Light DBs. Stand hip-width or split, or sit on a bench with back support (safer for the back).
  - Start: palms in, elbows bent ~10-30°. A ~10° hip hinge is fine ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidLateral/DBLateralRaise)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand or sit tall, elbows soft.
  2. Shoulders down, away from your ears.
  3. Raise your arms out to the sides, slightly forward.
  4. Stop at shoulder height, elbows level with or above your wrists.
  5. Lower slowly.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (raise 2 s, hold 1 s, lower 3 s). From the arms at the sides to the upper arms about parallel to the floor, ~30° in front of the body line. Past ~60-70°, tip the front of the DB slightly up. That slight outward rotation may reduce impingement ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/26/lateral-raise/)).
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: the side of the shoulders. Not: a pinch at the top of the shoulder, neck or upper-trap strain, the low back working.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Swing: hips hinge ~15° then snap upright, trunk ~10-15° back at the top → momentum and lumbar extension → lighter, or seated.
  - Shrug: shoulders up ~3-5 cm → upper-trap dominance → "reach for the walls".
  - Jug-pour: arms past ~100° with the thumbs down → impingement risk, and internal rotation raises upper-trapezius activity ([Coratella et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7503819/)) → stop at shoulder height, thumbs neutral.
  - Wrists lead: hands ~10 cm above the elbows → the front deltoid takes over (ExRx) → lead with the elbows.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Keep it light and don't swing. Sit with back support when irritable.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: seated, one arm at a time. Harder: cable lateral raise, 4 s lowering.
- **Narration cues:** "Reach out, not up." · "Lead with your elbows." · "Shoulders down." · "Stop at shoulder height."
- **YouTube search:** `dumbbell lateral raise form physical therapist shoulder impingement`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Lateral Raise](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/26/lateral-raise/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Lateral Raise](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidLateral/DBLateralRaise) · [Coratella et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7503819/)

### `rear-delt-fly` — Rear-delt fly (chest-supported)
- **Muscles:** Primary: rear deltoid. Secondary: middle trapezius, rhomboids, infraspinatus, teres minor.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Default: an adjustable bench at 45-60°. Sit facing the back pad with your chest on it. Let the DBs hang with palms facing each other and elbows soft ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/34/incline-reverse-fly/)).
  - Machine alternative: the reverse pec deck (a pec deck with the handles set behind). Sit facing the pad, seat set so the handles are at shoulder height, thumbs-up grip ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidPosterior/LVRearLateralRaise)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Chest on the pad, arms hanging, elbows soft.
  2. Shoulders down, away from your ears.
  3. Open your arms wide to the sides.
  4. Stop at shoulder height, hands in line with your ears.
  5. Lower slowly.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (open 2 s, hold 1 s, lower 3 s). From the arms hanging under the shoulders to near shoulder level, with the DBs in line with or just in front of the ears (ACE).
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you open, inhale as you lower (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: back of the shoulders, upper back. Not: neck strain, a shoulder pinch, any low-back work.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Chest lifts ~5-10 cm off the pad with ~15° of lumbar arch → support lost → chest on the pad, lighter.
  - Elbows sweep ~45° toward the hips, turning it into a row → the lats take over (ExRx) → keep the upper arms at right angles to the torso.
  - Arms travel behind the ear line → emphasis shifts to the lats (ACE) → stop in line with the ears.
  - Elbows progressively bend from ~20° to ~90° → it turns into a row → fix the elbow angle.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Never use the unsupported bent-over version with back-pain users. It holds a near-horizontal hinge for the whole set, and the back rounds easily with tight hamstrings ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidPosterior/DBRearLateralRaise)).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: reverse pec deck, band pull-apart. Harder: heavier, 2 s hold.
- **Narration cues:** "Chest stays on the pad." · "Open wide like wings." · "Shoulders away from your ears." · "Stop at shoulder height."
- **YouTube search:** `chest supported rear delt fly incline bench form`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Incline Reverse Fly](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/34/incline-reverse-fly/) · [ExRx: Seated Reverse Fly machine](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidPosterior/LVRearLateralRaise) · [ExRx: Bent-over Rear Lateral Raise (for contrast)](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidPosterior/DBRearLateralRaise)

### `arnold-press` — Seated Arnold press
- **Muscles:** Primary: front deltoid. Secondary: side deltoid, triceps, supraspinatus, trapezius.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Set: the same bench as dumbbell-shoulder-press, back pad upright (~80-90°), head, shoulders and buttocks on the pad ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/45/seated-overhead-press/)).
  - Start: DBs in front of the shoulders at chin height, palms facing you, elbows under the wrists ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidAnterior/DBArnoldPress)). Use lighter DBs than your normal press.
- **Execution:**
  1. Back on the pad, dumbbells at chin height, palms facing you.
  2. Open your elbows to the sides as you start to press.
  3. Turn your palms forward as the dumbbells rise.
  4. Finish with your arms straight overhead.
  5. Reverse on the way down.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (press and turn 2 s, lower and turn back 3 s). From the DBs in front of the chin with palms toward the face to straight arms with palms forward.
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: front and side shoulders, triceps. Not: catching or pinching in the shoulder during the turn, wrist pain, low-back strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Lean-back arch: trunk ~15-25° off the pad → lumbar extension load → back on the pad, lighter.
  - Wrist-only twist: forearms rotate while the elbows stay forward and low → misses the shoulder-abduction element that is the point of the lift (ExRx) → open the elbows first.
  - Shrug and head poke: shoulders up ~3-5 cm, chin ~5 cm forward → neck strain → long neck.
  - Jerky rotation with heavy DBs → shoulder irritation → light load, smooth 2 s press.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Seated with back support only. For painful shoulders, use the neutral-grip dumbbell-shoulder-press or landmine-press. This lift adds loaded shoulder rotation, and the shoulder is one of the most commonly injured regions in resistance training ([Kolber et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20508476/)).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: neutral-grip dumbbell-shoulder-press, machine-shoulder-press. Harder: heavier DBs.
- **Narration cues:** "Elbows open, palms turn." · "Back on the pad." · "Press up, ribs down." · "Reverse it on the way down."
- **YouTube search:** `arnold press proper form seated physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Dumbbell Arnold Press](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/DeltoidAnterior/DBArnoldPress) · [ACE: Seated Overhead Press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/45/seated-overhead-press/) · [Kolber et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20508476/)

## 7. Arms

### `barbell-curl` — Barbell / EZ-bar curl
- **Muscles:** Primary: biceps brachii. Secondary: brachialis, brachioradialis.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A straight bar or EZ-bar (the zig-zag bar, easier on the wrists), taken from a rack at hip height, not the floor (G4).
  - Set: underhand grip about shoulder width ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/70/bicep-curl/), [ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Biceps/BBCurl)). Feet hip-width, knees soft. With back pain, stand with your back against a wall.
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand tall, bar at your thighs, elbows by your sides.
  2. Brace and keep your chest still.
  3. Curl until your forearms are vertical.
  4. Squeeze, elbows still by your sides.
  5. Lower slowly to straight arms.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (curl 2 s, squeeze 1 s, lower 3 s). From straight arms to vertical forearms. The elbows may move forward only slightly (ExRx).
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down. Curls to failure push blood pressure very high ([MacDougall et al. 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3980383/)), so stop 2-3 reps short (G2).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: front of the upper arm, forearms. Not: low-back strain, front-shoulder pain, elbow or wrist pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Body swing: hips hinge ~15-20°, then thrust forward with the trunk ~15-20° back at the top → lumbar extension and momentum → lighter bar, back against a wall.
  - Elbows forward: upper arms flex ~30-45°, bar to the chin → the front deltoid takes over → elbows at the sides.
  - Wrist curl: wrists flex ~30° at the top → wrist strain → straight wrists, or an EZ-bar.
  - Dropped lowering: the bar falls in under 1 s and the elbows snap straight → elbow strain → 3 s lowering.
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable.** The load sits in front of the body and invites swinging. For back-pain users prefer a seated DB curl with back support or a cable curl; hammer and incline curls already cover the biceps.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: seated DB curl, cable curl. Harder: heavier bar, 4 s lowering.
- **Narration cues:** "Elbows pinned to your sides." · "Chest still, no swing." · "Curl, squeeze, lower slowly." · "Breathe out on the way up."
- **YouTube search:** `barbell curl proper form no swinging physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Bicep Curl (barbell)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/70/bicep-curl/) · [ExRx: Barbell Curl](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Biceps/BBCurl) · [MacDougall et al. 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3980383/)

### `incline-dumbbell-curl` — Incline dumbbell curl
- **Muscles:** Primary: biceps brachii, with the long head lengthened because the arm hangs behind the body. Secondary: brachialis, brachioradialis.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Set: an adjustable bench at 45-60° ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Biceps/DBInclineCurl)). Head, shoulders and buttocks on the pad, feet flat. Arms hang straight down, palms in or forward.
  - Pick up the DBs one at a time before leaning back.
- **Execution:**
  1. Lean back, arms hanging straight down.
  2. Keep your shoulders against the bench.
  3. Curl up, turning your palms to face your shoulders.
  4. Keep your elbows pointing down.
  5. Lower slowly to straight arms.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (curl 2 s, squeeze 1 s, lower 3 s). From straight hanging arms to vertical forearms with the palms toward the shoulders.
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: biceps, with a stretch at the bottom. Not: pain at the front of the shoulder (the biceps tendon; if so, raise the bench or switch to a seated curl), elbow pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Elbows swing forward ~30-45° → the stretch is lost and the front deltoid helps → keep the elbows pointing down.
  - Shoulders lift ~3-5 cm off the pad and the chin juts → neck strain → shoulders and head back.
  - Bench too flat (≤30°): arms hang far behind the torso → front-shoulder strain → 45-60°.
  - Bottom bounce: elbows snap straight under the DBs' momentum → elbow and tendon stress → 3 s lowering.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Seated and supported. Biceps activation follows a similar pattern to a standard curl through the whole range ([Oliveira et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3737788/)), so an upright seated curl is a fair swap if the shoulder objects.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: seated upright DB curl. Harder: heavier, alternating arms.
- **Narration cues:** "Arms hang straight down." · "Elbows stay back." · "Curl and turn." · "Slow to a full stretch."
- **YouTube search:** `incline dumbbell curl proper form bench angle`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Dumbbell Incline Curl](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Biceps/DBInclineCurl) · [Oliveira et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3737788/)

### `hammer-curl` — Hammer curl
- **Muscles:** Primary: brachioradialis, brachialis. Secondary: biceps brachii.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - DBs held with a neutral grip (palms facing the body, thumbs up).
  - Use a split stance ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/10/hammer-curl/)), or sit with back support (safer for the back).
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand or sit tall, palms facing your body.
  2. Elbows by your sides, shoulders down.
  3. Curl up, thumbs leading toward your shoulders.
  4. Stop when the dumbbell nears the front of your shoulder.
  5. Lower slowly.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (curl 2 s, squeeze 1 s, lower 3 s). From straight arms to a vertical forearm with the thumb toward the shoulder ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Brachioradialis/DBHammerCurl)).
- **Breathing:** Exhale up, inhale down (G1, G2).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: the thumb side of the forearm, the front of the upper arm. Not: outer-elbow pain, wrist pain, low-back strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Body swing: trunk ~15° back as the hips thrust → lumbar extension → lighter DBs, split stance or seated.
  - Elbows drift forward or out ~30° → the shoulder takes over → elbows pinned.
  - Bent wrists: wrists tilt ~20° toward the thumb or little finger → wrist strain → thumb in line with the forearm.
  - Shrug: shoulders up ~3 cm → neck tension → shoulders down.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Sit with back support when irritable, and alternate arms to halve the load.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: seated, alternating, cable rope hammer curl. Harder: heavier DBs.
- **Narration cues:** "Thumbs up, like holding hammers." · "Elbows by your sides." · "Lift without leaning." · "Slow on the way down."
- **YouTube search:** `hammer curl proper form common mistakes physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Hammer Curl](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/10/hammer-curl/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Hammer Curl](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Brachioradialis/DBHammerCurl)

### `rope-pushdown` — Rope triceps pushdown
- **Muscles:** Primary: triceps brachii. Secondary: anconeus. The lats and abdominals stabilise.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Set: a cable tower with the pulley at its highest setting and the rope attached ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/3/triceps-pressdown/)). Stand close to the cable ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Triceps/CBPushdown)), feet hip-width or staggered, torso tall.
  - Grip the rope thumbs-up, elbows at your sides beside the mid-trunk ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/185/triceps-pushdowns/)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand tall, elbows at your sides.
  2. Start with your hands at chest height.
  3. Push the rope down until your arms are straight.
  4. Spread the rope ends apart at the bottom.
  5. Let your hands rise slowly, elbows pinned.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (push 2 s, hold 1 s, return 3 s). From the hands at upper-chest height (just before the elbows would drift forward, ACE) to straight arms with the hands beside the thighs.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you push, inhale on the return (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: the back of the upper arms. Not: elbow pain, front-shoulder pain, low-back strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Leaning over the rope: trunk flexed ~30-45° with a rounded back → lumbar flexion load → stand tall, lighter stack.
  - Elbows drift ~30-40° forward and up at the top → the lats join in and the triceps do less (ACE) → elbows pinned.
  - Elbow flare: elbows ~30-45° out to the sides → elbow and shoulder stress → elbows in.
  - Half lockout: elbows stop ~20-30° short of straight → less triceps work → finish straight without slamming.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Spine load is low when you stand tall. If standing provokes symptoms, use a seated triceps machine.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: band pushdown, lighter. Harder: heavier, single-arm, overhead-tricep-extension.
- **Narration cues:** "Elbows glued to your sides." · "Push down and spread the rope." · "Stand tall." · "Slow on the way up."
- **YouTube search:** `tricep rope pushdown proper form elbows physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Triceps Pressdown (rope)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/3/triceps-pressdown/) · [ACE: Triceps Pushdowns](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/185/triceps-pushdowns/) · [ExRx: Cable Pushdown](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Triceps/CBPushdown)

### `overhead-tricep-extension` — Seated overhead triceps extension (dumbbell)
- **Muscles:** Primary: triceps brachii, with the long head lengthened. Secondary: anconeus.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Set: one DB held in both hands under the top plate ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Triceps/DBTriExt)). Sit on a bench with an upright back pad whose top is just below the shoulders, so it doesn't block the DB (ExRx). Feet flat, ribs down.
  - Cable alternative: rope on a low pulley, facing away, staggered stance.
- **Execution:**
  1. Sit tall, back on the pad, dumbbell straight overhead.
  2. Point your elbows forward, close to your head.
  3. Lower the dumbbell behind your head.
  4. Stop at about a right angle.
  5. Straighten your arms back overhead.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (extend 2 s, lower 3 s). *Starts at top.* Down to elbows at ~90°, or until the upper arms start moving back ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/74/triceps-extension/)), back up to straight arms.
- **Breathing:** Inhale down, exhale up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: a long stretch and work in the back of the upper arm. Not: low-back arching or pain, elbow pain, a shoulder pinch, neck strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Rib flare and arch: ~15-25° lumbar extension with the ribs popping forward, typical with limited shoulder flexion (ExRx) → lumbar extension load → back supported, ribs down, less depth.
  - Elbow flare: elbows ~30-45° out, ~15-20 cm wider than the shoulders → elbow and shoulder strain → elbows forward at shoulder width (ACE).
  - Upper arms rock ~20-30° forward and back → load shifts off the triceps → upper arms still.
  - Head bowed: neck flexed ~20-30° to make room, DB near the back of the head → neck strain and impact risk → head neutral, stop at 90°.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Standing versions invite arching, so use the seated, back-supported version or the light cable version. Worth keeping: overhead elbow extension produced ~1.4× more triceps growth than a neutral-arm cable extension ([Maeo et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)). With limited shoulder mobility, use rope-pushdown.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: rope-pushdown, lighter DB. Harder: heavier, single-arm.
- **Narration cues:** "Ribs down, back on the pad." · "Elbows forward, close to your head." · "Lower behind your head slowly." · "Press to the ceiling."
- **YouTube search:** `seated overhead tricep extension dumbbell form elbows`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Triceps Extension](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/74/triceps-extension/) · [ExRx: Dumbbell Triceps Extension](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Triceps/DBTriExt) · [Maeo et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)

### `skull-crushers` — Lying triceps extension ("skull crusher")
- **Muscles:** Primary: triceps brachii. Secondary: anconeus.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A flat bench with an EZ-bar (easier on the wrists) or two DBs (neutral grip, easier to control near the face).
  - Set: feet flat, or on a step to keep the back neutral. Narrow overhand grip about shoulder width ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Triceps/BBLyingTriExtSC)).
  - Getting into position: take the bar from a rack or the bench end, rest it on your thighs, lie back with it on your chest, then press it up (G4).
- **Execution:**
  1. Lie back, bar over your shoulders, arms straight.
  2. Keep your upper arms still and shoulder-width apart.
  3. Bend your elbows to lower the bar toward your forehead.
  4. Slow down near your head, and stop just above.
  5. Straighten your arms back to the start.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (extend 2 s, lower 3 s). *Starts at top.* Down to just above the forehead or slightly behind the head, stopping when the upper arms start to drift back ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/36/lying-barbell-triceps-extensions/)).
- **Breathing:** Inhale down, exhale up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel: the back of the upper arms. Not: elbow, wrist or shoulder pain, or low-back strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Elbow flare: elbows ~15-20 cm wider than the grip → elbow and shoulder stress → elbows at shoulder width.
  - Arm drift: upper arms swing ~30-45° toward the head and back, turning it into a pullover and press → less triceps work → upper arms still.
  - Uncontrolled drop: the bar falls the last ~10 cm toward the face → impact injury → slow near the forehead (ExRx), or use DBs.
  - Back arch: ~15-20° lumbar extension with the hips lifting → lumbar compression → feet planted or on a step, ribs down.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Lying on your back with your feet planted is easy on the spine. The risks are the setup and getting up, so sit for a moment before standing (G4).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: DB lying extension, rope-pushdown. Harder: heavier load, 4 s lowering.
- **Narration cues:** "Upper arms still." · "Elbows in, shoulder-width." · "Slow near your forehead." · "Squeeze straight."
- **YouTube search:** `skull crusher proper form elbow friendly ez bar dumbbell`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Lying Barbell Triceps Extensions](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/36/lying-barbell-triceps-extensions/) · [ExRx: Barbell Lying Triceps Extension ("Skull Crusher")](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Triceps/BBLyingTriExtSC)
