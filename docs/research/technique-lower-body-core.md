# Technique research: lower body and core (hinges, squats, legs, carries, core)

**Scope.** Technique, setup, tempo, breathing, safety and coaching cues for 28 lower-body and core exercises. This feeds the voice coach and the 3D "correct form" and "how not to do it" animations. Upper body, stretching/mobility and cardio are covered in separate docs. General educational content, not individual medical advice. Researched 2026-10-05. ExRx.net blocks automated fetchers, so ExRx links were checked against Internet Archive snapshots; only pages that were free to view in their latest snapshot are cited (ExRx has moved some pages, such as its trap-bar deadlift, behind a paywall). PubMed and PMC records were checked through NCBI E-utilities.

## 1. Conventions

- **Tempo** is `C-H-E-P` in seconds, the same as the upper-body doc: concentric (lift, stand, press or curl), hold at the end of it, eccentric (lower or return), then pause in the start or stretched position. `2-0-3-1` = up 2 s, no hold, down 3 s, 1 s pause at the bottom.
  - Each entry also gives the tempo in words and says where the rep starts. For exercises that start standing or with the legs extended (squats, RDLs, leg press), the coach counts the lowering first.
  - Holds (planks, curl-ups, bird dogs) are given as seconds per hold × reps.
- **Mistake angles and distances** are approximate deviations from the correct position, written for animators. 0° means correct or neutral. Figures assume an adult about 170-180 cm tall. They are not clinical thresholds. Each mistake reads Mistake → risk → fix.
- **Neutral spine under load.** Skilled lifters flatten the lower-lumbar curve by about 20° (upper lumbar about 13°) between standing and the bottom of a deadlift ([Bengtsson 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528690/)). The correct animation may show that slight flattening. A "rounded back" fault must go clearly beyond it, with the low back visibly convex.
- **Back & sciatica labels** (same scheme as the other technique docs):
  - **OK**: suitable as written.
  - **Modify**: use the stated variant, and always use it on irritable days.
  - **Avoid when irritable**: swap in the named alternative until symptoms calm.
  - **Irritable** means leg symptoms that are easily provoked, present at rest, or slow (more than 30 min) to settle.
- **G1-G6** point to the global rules below. Each entry adds only exercise-specific detail.

## 2. Global rules (apply to every exercise)

- **G1. Brace and breathe; never hold your breath.**
  - To brace, breathe in through the nose and tighten the whole trunk evenly (front, sides and back, "360°"), as if expecting a light poke. Bracing stiffens the spine more than drawing the navel in ([Grenier & McGill 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17207676/)).
  - Keep the brace and keep breathing. Exhale slowly through pursed lips ("tsss") through the hardest part of the rep, and inhale during the lowering or at the easy end.
  - During holds, breathe continuously and count breaths aloud. Prefer repeated ~10 s holds to long ones.
- **G2. End every set 2-3 reps short of failure; no maximal lifts.**
  - A brief breath-hold becomes almost unavoidable above about 80% of maximum or near failure ([Hackett & Chow 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23222073/)). The double-leg press taken to failure produced mean peak pressures of 320/250 mmHg in experienced lifters ([MacDougall 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3980383/)).
  - With hypertension, avoid the Valsalva manoeuvre during resistance training ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)). Resistance work supplements aerobic training at moderate intensity ([ACSM 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15076798/)). Stop for headache, dizziness, chest pain or visual change.
- **G3. Back and sciatica stop rules.**
  - Green: muscle effort, burn or mild stretch.
  - Amber: local low-back ache up to about 3/10 that settles within 30 min and is no worse the next morning. Keep the load; don't progress.
  - Red: pain, tingling or numbness spreading into the buttock or below the knee, sharp or catching pain, or symptoms worse the next day. Stop that exercise and use the named regression.
  - Seek urgent care and do not train if any of these appear: sciatica on both sides, severe or worsening weakness or numbness in both legs, numbness around the genitals or anus, or new difficulty controlling the bladder or bowels ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sciatica/)).
- **G4. Know which direction hurts.**
  - **Flexion-sensitive** backs are worse with sitting and bending, which is common with disc-related sciatica. Avoid loaded end-range rounding: limit depth on squats and the leg press, and drop loaded crunching. Repeated flexion under modest compression produced disc herniation in a porcine model ([Callaghan & McGill 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11114441/)).
  - **Extension-sensitive** backs are worse with standing, walking or arching (for example stenosis or spondylolisthesis). Avoid arching at lockout (hip thrust, back extension, deadlift lean-back) and sagging planks.
  - **Nerve tension** rises when hip flexion is combined with a straight knee and a dorsiflexed ankle, which is the straight-leg-raise position ([Coppieters 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16838375/)). While the leg is symptomatic, keep more knee bend and a shorter range in hinges and the seated leg curl.
  - Heavy hinging is not banned after back pain. Deadlift-based training reduced pain about as much as motor-control training in recurrent mechanical low-back pain, although motor-control training did better for activity and movement control ([Aasa 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25641309/)). It has to be earned, though, and not while leg symptoms are active.
- **G5. Diabetes.**
  - With peripheral neuropathy, foot care and appropriate footwear are needed, and more non-weight-bearing work is advised if gait is altered ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)). Check the feet after carries, lunges, step-ups and calf work, and use the supported variant if balance is poor.
  - With severe non-proliferative or unstable proliferative retinopathy, avoid breath-holding, heavy straining and head-down positions such as the bottom of the 45° back extension (ADA 2016).
- **G6. Getting weights into position.**
  - Take bars and dumbbells from a rack or bench at thigh height where possible. If a weight starts on the floor, squat or hinge to it with a neutral spine. Never side-bend or round to grab it.
  - Set safety pins, straps or stops on racks and leg presses before the first set.

## 4. Hinge and glute

### `deadlift` — Conventional deadlift (barbell)
- **Muscles:** Primary: gluteus maximus, hamstrings, adductor magnus, quadriceps (off the floor). Secondary: spinal erectors (isometric), latissimus dorsi, trapezius, forearm flexors (grip).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a straight 2.2 m Olympic bar loaded on the floor, usually on a lifting platform.
  - Full-size 45 cm plates put the bar about 22 cm off the floor. With smaller plates, raise the bar on blocks to that height.
  - Feet under the hips, toes 0-15° out, bar over mid-foot (about 2-3 cm from the shins). Double-overhand grip just outside the shins.
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand with the bar over the middle of your feet.
  2. Hinge down and grip just outside your legs.
  3. Bend your knees until your shins touch the bar. Chest up, back long.
  4. Breathe in, brace, and pull the slack out of the bar.
  5. Push the floor away, keeping the bar against your legs.
  6. Stand tall: hips through, ribs down.
  7. Push your hips back and slide the bar down your legs to the floor.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-2-1 (lift 2 s, stand tall 1 s, lower 2 s, 1 s dead stop). Each rep starts and ends with the plates resting on the floor. No bouncing.
- **Breathing:** Brace on the floor. Exhale through pursed lips as the bar passes the knees, then re-breathe at the top or on the floor (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the glutes, hamstrings, grip and upper back. You should not feel sharp or one-sided low-back pain, buttock pain, or tingling or pain below the knee.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Rounded low back:** the lumbar spine flexes a further ~25-35° beyond the start posture, so the low back turns convex and the pelvis tucks → disc and ligament strain → lighter load or bar on blocks; "long spine, chest proud".
  - **Bar drifts forward:** bar 8-15 cm in front of the shins or thighs → much bigger lever on the low back → "lats tight, drag the bar up your legs".
  - **Hips shoot up:** knees straighten ~20-30° before the bar leaves the floor and the torso tips ~15° toward horizontal → the back does the lifting → "hips and shoulders rise together".
  - **Leaning back at lockout:** lumbar spine extends ~10-20° past neutral, with the shoulders ~10 cm behind the hips → facet-joint compression → "squeeze glutes, ribs over hips".
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable.** When calm, start from blocks (bar at mid-shin) and keep 2-3 reps in reserve. The safer default is trap-bar-deadlift from the high handles. If that still provokes symptoms, use hip-thrust or glute-bridge.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: dowel hip-hinge drill, then a kettlebell deadlift from a step, then the trap bar. Harder: add load first, then lower the start height (blocks, then floor).
- **Narration cues:** "Bar on your legs." · "Push the floor away." · "Long spine." · "Hips through, ribs down."
- **YouTube search:** `how to deadlift physical therapist explains form`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Barbell Deadlift](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/GluteusMaximus/BBDeadlift) · [Stronger by Science: How to Deadlift](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/how-to-deadlift/) · [Bengtsson 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528690/)

### `trap-bar-deadlift` — Trap-bar (hex-bar) deadlift
- **Muscles:** Primary: quadriceps, gluteus maximus, adductors. Secondary: hamstrings, spinal erectors (isometric), trapezius, grip. Compared with a straight bar it gives lower peak lumbar and hip moments and a higher knee moment ([Swinton 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21659894/)), and less hamstring activity ([Andersen 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28151780/)).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a hexagon-shaped bar you stand inside, with plates on short sleeves at each side. Most have high and low handles; you flip the bar to switch.
  - Use the high handles for anyone with a back history. They start the lift about 5-10 cm higher, depending on the model.
  - Stand centred, with your ankle bones in line with the middle of the handles. Feet hip-width, toes slightly out. Grip the centre of each handle so the bar balances.
- **Execution:**
  1. Step into the centre, ankles in line with the handles.
  2. Push your hips back, bend your knees and grip the middle of the handles.
  3. Shoulders just over the handles, back long.
  4. Breathe in, brace and take the slack out.
  5. Drive the floor away and stand tall.
  6. Sit your hips back and lower the bar softly to the floor.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-2-1 (lift 2 s, stand tall 1 s, lower 2 s, 1 s dead stop). Each rep goes from the plates on the floor, or on 5-10 cm blocks, to standing tall.
- **Breathing:** Brace on the floor. Exhale from mid-shin to lockout, then re-breathe on the floor (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the quads, glutes and grip. You should not feel low-back pain, or pain or tingling below the knee.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Off-centre stance:** feet 5-10 cm too far forward or back, so the bar tilts → the back works to balance it → ankles level with the handle centres.
  - **Rounded low back at the bottom:** lumbar spine flexes ~20-30° beyond the start, most often from the low handles → disc strain → high handles, hips back, chest proud.
  - **Shrugging and bent arms:** shoulders rise 3-5 cm toward the ears and the elbows bend ~20° → neck and biceps strain → "long arms, shoulders down".
  - **Leaning back at lockout:** lumbar spine extends ~10-20°, with the shoulders behind the hips → facet compression → stand tall and squeeze the glutes.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** This is the preferred heavy hinge for users with a back history: high handles, moderate loads with 3 reps in reserve, and stop if leg symptoms appear (G3). If it still irritates, use glute-bridge or cable-pull-through.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: high handles from blocks, then glute-bridge. Harder: add load, then move to the low handles.
- **Narration cues:** "Ankles under the handles." · "Long arms." · "Push the floor away." · "Stand tall, don't lean."
- **YouTube search:** `trap bar deadlift technique physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Swinton 2011: straight vs hexagonal bar](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21659894/) · [Andersen 2018: barbell vs hex bar vs hip thrust EMG](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28151780/)

### `rack-pull` — Rack pull
- **Muscles:** Primary: gluteus maximus, hamstrings, spinal erectors (isometric), upper trapezius. Secondary: latissimus dorsi, rhomboids, forearm flexors.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: done in a power rack, a steel cage with four uprights, numbered holes and horizontal safety pins or straps.
  - Set the pins so the bar starts just below the kneecaps. A "block pull" (plates raised on blocks) is kinder to bar and rack; 10-15 cm blocks under full-size plates put the bar at about mid-shin.
  - Feet hip-width, bar touching the shins, double-overhand grip just outside the legs.
- **Execution:**
  1. Set the bar just below your knees.
  2. Hinge and grip just outside your legs, shins close to the bar.
  3. Brace and pull the slack out.
  4. Drive your hips forward, sliding the bar up your thighs.
  5. Stand tall with your shoulders down. No leaning back.
  6. Lower under control to the pins.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-2-1 (lift 2 s, stand tall 1 s, lower 2 s, 1 s on the pins). Each rep goes from the pins to full hip and knee extension. Touch the pins softly; don't bounce.
- **Breathing:** Brace at the pins and exhale through the lockout (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the glutes, hamstrings, upper back and grip. You should not feel low-back pain or pain down the leg.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Overloading and hitching:** heavier-than-deadlift loads make the lumbar spine round ~15-25°, and the knees re-bend ~15-20° to rest the bar on the thighs → spinal overload → deadlift-range loads, one smooth pull.
  - **Leaning back at lockout:** torso ~15-20° behind vertical with the low back arched → facet compression → finish with the glutes, ribs over hips.
  - **Bouncing off the pins:** bar dropped and rebounded → lost brace and a jolt to the spine → 1 s dead stop.
  - **Rolling the shoulders:** shoulders shrug and roll at the top → neck strain for no benefit → "shoulders down and back".
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable.** When calm, set the pins at or above the knee and use moderate loads; the short range tempts lifters into very heavy weights. Safer swaps are trap-bar-deadlift from high handles or cable-pull-through. For an upper-back focus, chest-supported-row trains the same muscles without spinal load.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: higher pins (above the knee), light load, then the trap bar. Harder: lower pins, then the full deadlift.
- **Narration cues:** "Bar on your thighs." · "Hips forward." · "Tall, not back." · "Smooth."
- **YouTube search:** `rack pull technique strength coach`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Barbell Rack Pull](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/GluteusMaximus/BBRackPull) · [Stronger by Science: How to Deadlift](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/how-to-deadlift/)

### `romanian-deadlift` — Romanian deadlift (RDL)
- **Muscles:** Primary: hamstrings, gluteus maximus, adductor magnus. Secondary: spinal erectors (isometric), latissimus dorsi, grip.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Barbell taken from a rack set at mid-thigh height, so there is no floor pick-up (G6). Two dumbbells also work.
  - Feet hip-width, toes forward. Double-overhand grip just outside the thighs. Knees unlocked by about 15-20°.
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand tall, bar at your thighs, knees soft.
  2. Push your hips back and slide the bar down your thighs.
  3. Keep your shins nearly vertical and your back long.
  4. Stop when your hips stop moving back, usually just below the knees.
  5. Drive your hips forward to stand and squeeze your glutes.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-3-0 (lower 3 s, rise 2 s, stand tall 1 s). The rep starts and ends standing, so the coach counts the lowering first. The bottom is wherever the hips stop travelling back without the back rounding: usually the bar just below the knees to mid-shin, with the torso about 45-70° forward.
- **Breathing:** Inhale and brace at the top. Exhale as you rise past the knees (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel a hamstring stretch and the glutes. You should not feel low-back pain, or burning or tingling behind the knee or in the calf, which is nerve tension rather than stretch.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Reaching for the floor:** bar goes below mid-shin and the lumbar spine flexes ~20-30° once the hamstrings run out of length → disc strain → stop where the hips stop moving back.
  - **Squatting it:** knees bend more than 40°, shins tilt ~20° forward and the hips drop → hamstrings unloaded, it turns into a deadlift → "hips back, shins vertical".
  - **Bar drifts away:** bar 10-20 cm in front of the thighs → bigger low-back moment → "shave your legs with the bar".
  - **Locked knees:** 0° knee bend at the bottom of the hinge → extra hamstring and sciatic-nerve tension → keep a 15-20° bend.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** With sciatica, take the bar no lower than just above the knee, bend the knees about 25-30° and keep the chin neutral (G4). Dumbbells or cable-pull-through are gentler options. Stop if symptoms go below the knee.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: dowel hip-hinge drill, then a kettlebell RDL, then cable-pull-through. Harder: barbell, slower lowering, single-leg RDL.
- **Narration cues:** "Hips back." · "Bar on your thighs." · "Long spine." · "Squeeze to stand."
- **YouTube search:** `Romanian deadlift form physical therapist hip hinge`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Romanian Deadlift](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/OlympicLifts/RomanianDeadlift) · [ACE: Romanian Deadlift](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/317/romanian-deadlift/) · [ACE: Hip Hinge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/33/hip-hinge/)

### `cable-pull-through` — Cable pull-through
- **Muscles:** Primary: gluteus maximus, hamstrings. Secondary: adductors, grip, spinal erectors. Erector demand is lower than in the RDL: longissimus activation was 11% lower and multifidus 14% lower ([Dicus 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10124728/)).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a cable station, which is a tall column with a weight stack and a pulley you slide up or down and lock with a pop-pin.
  - Set the pulley to the lowest position and clip on a rope. Face away, take the rope between your legs, and walk out 2-3 steps (about 1-1.5 m) until the stack lifts.
  - Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out, knees soft.
- **Execution:**
  1. Face away from the cable with the rope between your legs.
  2. Soften your knees, push your hips back and let the rope pull your hands through.
  3. Keep your back long. Stop when your hips stop moving back.
  4. Drive your hips forward to stand tall.
  5. Squeeze your glutes. Your arms just hold the rope.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-1-2-1 (drive forward 1 s, squeeze 1 s, hips back 2 s, 1 s in the stretch). The rep starts standing and goes to the torso about 45° forward.
- **Breathing:** Inhale as the hips go back; exhale as you drive forward (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the glutes at the top and the hamstrings in the stretch. You should not feel the low back working or the shoulders being yanked.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Squatting instead of hinging:** knees flex more than 60° and the hips drop toward the heels → glutes and hamstrings unloaded → "hips back, like shutting a car door".
  - **Rounding at the bottom:** hands pulled deep between the legs and the lumbar spine flexes ~15-25° → disc loading → shorter range.
  - **Pulling with the arms:** elbows bend ~30-60° → shoulders and biceps take over from the hips → "arms are ropes".
  - **Leaning back at lockout:** lumbar spine extends ~10-15° → facet compression → stand tall and squeeze.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** A back-friendly hinge: the resistance is horizontal and erector demand is lower. While irritable, shorten the range and lighten the load, and stop if leg symptoms appear.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: band pull-through, dowel hip hinge. Harder: more load, a pause in the stretch, then the RDL.
- **Narration cues:** "Hips back." · "Arms long." · "Hips through." · "Squeeze."
- **YouTube search:** `cable pull through technique physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Dicus 2023: RDL vs cable pull-through vs reverse hyper EMG](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10124728/) · [ACE: Hip Hinge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/33/hip-hinge/)

### `back-extension-45` — 45° back extension (hip-hinge style)
- **Muscles:** Primary: gluteus maximus, hamstrings, spinal erectors (isometric). Secondary: adductor magnus.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: the 45° hyperextension bench (often called a Roman chair). It is an angled frame with thigh pads and a footplate with ankle rollers.
  - Set the pad so its top edge sits just below the hip crease. Your belly should not press into the pad when you lower ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Hamstrings/BW45HyperextensionHips)).
  - Heels against the footplate, rollers behind the lower calves. Hands behind the hips is easiest; arms crossed on the chest is harder.
- **Execution:**
  1. Set the pad just below your hip crease.
  2. Brace so your body is straight from head to heels.
  3. Hinge at the hips and lower until you feel a hamstring stretch.
  4. Keep your back long. Don't curl over the pad.
  5. Squeeze your glutes to rise.
  6. Stop at a straight line. No arching.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-1-2-0 (lower 2 s, rise 2 s, 1 s hold at the straight line). The rep starts at the straight line, so the coach counts the lowering first. The bottom is a mild stretch with the torso about 70-90° to the legs.
- **Breathing:** Inhale as you lower; exhale as you rise (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the glutes and hamstrings. You should not feel low-back pain or pressure in the head at the bottom.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Arching at the top:** torso rises ~15-25° above the line of the legs with the low back arched → facet and pars compression → stop at the straight line.
  - **Curling at the bottom:** lumbar spine flexes ~30-40° over the pad → repeated loaded flexion → hinge at the hips only and limit the depth.
  - **Pad too high:** pad sits on the belly, so the hips can't hinge → the spine bends instead → lower the pad.
  - **Bouncing:** fast drop and rebound → spikes in spine load → 2 s down.
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable.** Also avoid it with extension-sensitive pain or active sciatica. When calm, use the hip-hinge style only, with no arching. Of the extensor exercises tested, active trunk extension produced the highest spine loads and single-leg extension (bird-dog style) the lowest ([Callaghan 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9442191/)). Regress to bird-dog, glute-bridge or cable-pull-through. The bottom position is head-down (G5).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: hands behind the hips, partial range, bird-dog. Harder: arms crossed, then a plate held at the chest.
- **Narration cues:** "Hinge at the hips." · "Long spine." · "Squeeze up." · "Stop at the line."
- **YouTube search:** `45 degree back extension glute focus physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: 45° Hyperextension (hands behind hips)](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Hamstrings/BW45HyperextensionHips) · [Callaghan 1998: spine load during extensor exercises](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9442191/)

### `hip-thrust` — Barbell hip thrust
- **Muscles:** Primary: gluteus maximus. Secondary: hamstrings, quadriceps, adductors. It produces more glute EMG than the back squat ([Contreras 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26214739/)).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a flat bench 40-46 cm high, braced against a wall or set on a non-slip mat so it can't slide ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/GluteusMaximus/BBHipThrust)). Hip-thrust machines have a back pad at shoulder-blade height and a padded lever or belt over the hips.
  - Bench edge just below the shoulder blades. Padded bar across the hip crease, not the stomach.
  - Feet flat, hip- to shoulder-width, placed so the shins are vertical at the top (knees about 90°).
- **Execution:**
  1. Shoulder blades on the bench, bar over your hip crease.
  2. Feet flat, so your shins are vertical at the top.
  3. Tuck your chin, ribs down, brace.
  4. Drive through your heels until your body is flat like a table.
  5. Squeeze your glutes for two seconds. No arching.
  6. Lower until your hips are just above the floor.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-2-2-0 (drive up 1 s, squeeze 2 s, lower 2 s). The rep starts with the hips about 10 cm above the floor and ends at full hip extension, torso parallel to the floor.
- **Breathing:** Inhale at the bottom; exhale while driving up and through the squeeze (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the glutes, strongest at the top. You should not feel low-back pain, a pinch at the front of the hips, or hamstring cramp.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Arching at the top:** ribs flare, the lumbar spine extends ~15-20° and the pelvis tips forward ~10° → facet compression, and the glutes do less → chin tucked, ribs down, tuck the tailbone.
  - **Feet too far out:** knees open beyond 110° at the top → hamstrings cramp and take over → shins vertical.
  - **Knees cave:** ~10-15° valgus → knee stress, less glute → "knees over toes".
  - **Short lockout:** hips stop ~15-20° short of full extension → less glute work → "finish flat".
- **Back safety:** **OK.** No axial spinal load, so it is a good deadlift substitute. If extension-sensitive, emphasise the tailbone tuck and stop just short of full lockout. Regress to glute-bridge.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: bodyweight hip thrust, glute-bridge. Harder: barbell load, single-leg hip thrust, 3 s holds.
- **Narration cues:** "Chin tucked." · "Ribs down." · "Drive through your heels." · "Squeeze, don't arch."
- **YouTube search:** `barbell hip thrust setup physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Barbell Hip Thrust](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/GluteusMaximus/BBHipThrust) · [Contreras 2015: hip thrust vs back squat EMG](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26214739/)

### `glute-bridge` — Glute bridge
- **Muscles:** Primary: gluteus maximus. Secondary: hamstrings, deep abdominals.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Mat. Lie on your back with knees bent about 90°, feet flat and hip-width, heels about 25-30 cm from the buttocks, arms by your sides.
  - Optional: a dumbbell or plate resting on the hips.
- **Execution:**
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width.
  2. Ribs down; gently tighten your abs.
  3. Press through your heels and lift your hips.
  4. Stop when your shoulders, hips and knees line up.
  5. Squeeze your glutes for two seconds.
  6. Lower slowly.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-2-2-0 (lift 1 s, squeeze 2 s, lower 2 s). The rep starts with the buttocks on the floor and ends at a straight shoulder-hip-knee line.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you lift; inhale as you lower ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/49/glute-bridge/)).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the glutes. You should not feel the low back working or a hamstring cramp.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Over-arching:** hips pushed too high, so the lumbar spine extends ~15° and the ribs flare → facet compression → stop at the straight line, ribs down.
  - **Feet too far away:** knees open to ~120-130° → hamstring cramp → bring the heels in to about 90° knees.
  - **Pushing through the toes:** heels lift 2-3 cm → quads take over → "heels down".
  - **Hip drop:** one side sits 3-5 cm lower → uneven loading → level hips, slower reps.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** First-line glute work during flares. If extension-sensitive, lift to about three-quarters height.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: smaller range, pelvic tilts. Harder: band above the knees, single-leg bridge, hip-thrust.
- **Narration cues:** "Heels down." · "Ribs down." · "Squeeze." · "Lower slowly."
- **YouTube search:** `glute bridge proper form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Glute Bridge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/49/glute-bridge/) · [ACE: Single-leg glute bridge progression](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/145/glute-bridge-single-leg-progression/)

## 5. Squat, lunge and leg press

### `leg-press` — Leg press (45° sled or seated)
- **Muscles:** Primary: quadriceps, gluteus maximus. Secondary: adductors, hamstrings, calves.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: either a 45° leg press (a plate-loaded sled on diagonal rails, with a reclined seat and safety-lock handles beside the hips) or a seated/horizontal leg press (a pin-loaded stack with a seat that slides on a rail).
  - If the back pad adjusts, pick a more reclined setting. It gives the hips more room before the pelvis tucks. Keep the tailbone and low back flat on the pad.
  - Feet mid-plate, hip- to shoulder-width, toes out 10-30°, whole foot flat.
    - Feet higher on the plate work the glutes more but make the pelvis tuck earlier.
    - Feet lower on the plate push the knees further forward and make the heels lift.
  - Set the safety stops at your depth limit. On the seated version, set the seat so the knees start at about 90° ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/154/seated-leg-press/)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Sit back with your tailbone and low back on the pad.
  2. Feet mid-plate, shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
  3. Press the plate away and release the safety handles.
  4. Lower under control until your knees reach about 90 degrees, and stop before your tailbone lifts.
  5. Press through your whole foot to just short of straight.
  6. Lock the safety handles after the last rep.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (lower 3 s, press 2 s). The rep starts with the legs extended (knees about 5-10° short of lockout), so the coach counts the lowering first. The bottom is about 90° of knee bend, or shallower if the pelvis starts to tuck first.
- **Breathing:** Inhale on the way down and exhale steadily as you press. Never hold the breath at the bottom: this machine produced the highest blood pressures in the MacDougall study (G2).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the quads and glutes. You should not feel the low back peeling off the pad, sharp knee pain or leg tingling.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Pelvis tucks at the bottom ("butt wink"):** sacrum lifts 2-5 cm off the pad and the lumbar spine flexes ~15-25° → loaded flexion of the lumbar discs → shallower depth, safeties set, feet slightly wider with toes out.
  - **Locking the knees:** knees snap straight or hyperextend ~5° → knee-joint stress → stop with a slight bend.
  - **Knees cave:** ~10-15° valgus → knee stress → "knees over toes".
  - **Heels lift:** heels 2-4 cm off the plate → knee load and an unstable foot → feet slightly higher, press through the whole foot ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/GluteusMaximus/SL45LegPress)).
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Usually well tolerated with a depth limit: stop before the pelvis tucks, use the safety stops, and try lighter single-leg sets. If seated flexion provokes sciatica, shorten the range or swap to step-up or a goblet squat to a box.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: shorter range, lighter load. Harder: more load, a 1 s pause at the depth limit, single-leg press.
- **Narration cues:** "Tailbone down." · "Knees over toes." · "Whole foot." · "Soft knees at the top."
- **YouTube search:** `leg press form physical therapist lower back rounding`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Sled 45° Leg Press](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/GluteusMaximus/SL45LegPress) · [ACE: Seated Leg Press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/154/seated-leg-press/) · [Escamilla 2001: squat vs leg press technique variations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11528346/)

### `goblet-squat` — Goblet squat
- **Muscles:** Primary: quadriceps, gluteus maximus. Secondary: adductors, trunk (front-loaded), upper back.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - One dumbbell held vertically and cupped under the top bell at breastbone height, elbows tucked. A kettlebell held by the horns also works.
  - Feet shoulder-width or slightly wider, toes out 15-30°.
  - Optional: a box or bench behind you, about 45 cm high, as a depth target.
- **Execution:**
  1. Hold the weight at your chest, elbows down.
  2. Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
  3. Brace, then sit down between your heels.
  4. Knees track over your toes; chest stays tall.
  5. Go as low as your back stays long, about thighs parallel.
  6. Drive up through your whole foot.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-1 (lower 3 s, pause 1 s, stand 2 s). The rep starts standing and goes to thighs about parallel, or to the box.
- **Breathing:** Inhale and brace at the top. Exhale through the sticking point on the way up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the quads, glutes and upper back holding the weight. You should not feel low-back pain, a knee pinch or leg symptoms.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Butt wink:** the pelvis tucks and the lumbar spine flexes ~15-25° at the bottom → disc loading → stop just above that depth, or use a box.
  - **Knees cave:** ~10-15° valgus on the way up → knee stress → "spread the floor".
  - **Heels lift:** heels rise 1-3 cm with the weight on the toes → knee stress, poor balance → whole-foot pressure, slightly wider stance.
  - **Chest drops:** torso tips past 45° and the weight drifts 10-15 cm off the chest → back strain → "elbows down, chest tall".
- **Back safety:** **OK.** The preferred loaded squat for a back history: light, front-loaded and upright. On irritable days, squat to a 45-50 cm box.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: bodyweight box squat. Harder: heavier weight, pauses, slower lowering.
- **Narration cues:** "Elbows down." · "Knees out." · "Chest tall." · "Push the floor."
- **YouTube search:** `goblet squat technique physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Goblet Squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/362/goblet-squat/) · [ExRx: Kettlebell Goblet Squat](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Kettlebell/KBGobletSquat)

### `barbell-squat` — Barbell back squat
- **Muscles:** Primary: quadriceps, gluteus maximus, adductors. Secondary: hamstrings, spinal erectors, calves.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: done in a power rack or squat stand with adjustable J-hooks and safety pins or straps.
  - Set the J-hooks at mid-chest to armpit height, so you unrack with a slight knee bend. Set the safeties a few cm below the bar's lowest point.
  - High-bar position: bar on the upper trapezius, not the neck, hands just outside the shoulders. Feet shoulder-width, toes out 15-30°.
- **Execution:**
  1. Bar on your upper back, hands just outside your shoulders.
  2. Stand it up and take two steps back.
  3. Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
  4. Breathe in, brace, and sit down between your heels.
  5. Lower until your thighs are about parallel, back long.
  6. Drive up, with chest and hips rising together.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-0 (lower 3 s, stand 2 s); the paused variant is 2-0-3-1. The rep starts standing and goes to thighs about parallel.
- **Breathing:** Inhale and brace at the top. Exhale through pursed lips from the sticking point, and re-breathe at the top (G1, G2).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the quads and glutes. You should not feel low-back pain, knee pain or leg symptoms.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Butt wink:** the pelvis tucks and the lumbar spine flexes ~20-30° at the bottom → disc stress under axial load → limit the depth or squat to a box.
  - **Knees cave:** ~10-15° valgus → knee stress → "knees out".
  - **Good-morning squat:** the hips rise first and the torso tips ~15-20° further forward → the low back takes the load → "chest and hips together".
  - **Heels lift and the bar drifts forward:** heels rise 1-3 cm and the bar moves ahead of mid-foot → back and knee strain → whole foot down, lighter load.
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable**, because the spine is axially loaded. When calm, use a high bar, a box at parallel and moderate loads. Parallel squats at 80% of 1RM reduced lumbar lordosis and stressed the L4-S1 discs more than half squats did ([Yanagisawa 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33038028/)). Safer options: goblet-squat, or leg-press with a depth limit.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: goblet-squat, then a box squat. Harder: add load first, then depth.
- **Narration cues:** "Brace." · "Knees out." · "Chest up." · "Up together." · "Breathe out."
- **YouTube search:** `back squat technique physical therapist common mistakes`
- **Sources:** [Stronger by Science: How to Squat](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/how-to-squat/) · [Myer 2014: back-squat assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4262933/) · [Yanagisawa 2021: squat depth and lumbar discs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33038028/)

### `bulgarian-split-squat` — Bulgarian split squat (rear foot elevated)
- **Muscles:** Primary: quadriceps and gluteus maximus of the front leg. Secondary: adductors, hamstrings, the rear leg's hip flexors (stretched), trunk. It is hip-dominant and loads the knee less than the back squat ([Mackey & Riemann 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8136570/)).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A flat bench or box about knee height (40-50 cm; [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/366/bulgarian-split-squat/)). Go lower (about 30 cm) with tight hip flexors or a sensitive back.
  - Rear foot laces-down on the bench. Front foot one long stride ahead (about 60-90 cm from the bench), so the front shin is roughly vertical at the bottom.
  - Feet hip-width apart, not in one line. Dumbbells at the sides, or one held goblet-style.
- **Execution:**
  1. Back foot laces-down on the bench.
  2. Front foot one long stride ahead, hip-width out to the side.
  3. Brace, ribs down, lean slightly forward.
  4. Lower straight down until your back knee is just above the floor.
  5. Drive through your front foot to stand.
  6. Finish the set, then switch legs.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-3-1 (lower 3 s, pause 1 s, stand 2 s). The rep starts standing and goes to the front thigh about parallel, with the rear knee 3-5 cm above the floor.
- **Breathing:** Inhale on the way down; exhale on the way up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the quad and glute of the front leg and a stretch at the front of the rear hip. You should not feel a pinch in the low back or knee pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Front knee caves:** ~10-15° valgus → knee stress → "knee over your middle toes".
  - **Arched back:** the pelvis tips forward ~10-15° and the lumbar spine extends as the rear hip stretches → facet compression → lower bench, ribs down, squeeze the rear glute.
  - **Stance too short:** the front heel lifts 2-3 cm and the knee travels 10 cm or more past the toes → knee load → step further out.
  - **Pushing off the back foot:** the rear leg drives and the torso bounces → the front leg is under-trained → "the back foot is just a kickstand".
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Spinal load is low. If extension-sensitive or balance is poor, use a lower bench, hold a rack, or do split squats with the rear foot on the floor (G5).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: split squat with the rear foot on the floor, hand support. Harder: more load, a small platform under the front foot.
- **Narration cues:** "Straight down." · "Knee over toes." · "Ribs down." · "Drive through the front heel."
- **YouTube search:** `Bulgarian split squat setup physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Bulgarian Split Squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/366/bulgarian-split-squat/) · [Mackey & Riemann 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8136570/)

### `step-up` — Dumbbell step-up
- **Muscles:** Primary: quadriceps, gluteus maximus. Secondary: hamstrings, adductors, gluteus medius, calves.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A stable box, bench or step that cannot slide.
  - Start at mid-shin height (20-30 cm). Progress to knee height at most, which puts the knee at about 90° with the foot on the box.
  - Whole foot on the box. Dumbbells at the sides; don't use a barbell.
- **Execution:**
  1. Place your whole foot on the box.
  2. Lean slightly forward over that foot.
  3. Drive through the heel of the top foot to stand up.
  4. Don't push off with the bottom foot.
  5. Step down slowly with the same trailing leg.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-0-2-0 (step up 2 s, step down 2 s). The rep starts with both feet on the floor and ends standing tall on the box.
- **Breathing:** Exhale on the way up; inhale on the way down (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the quad and glute of the working leg. You should not feel knee or back pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Pushing off the floor leg:** the trailing ankle springs and the body bounces up → the working leg is skipped → "tap the toes only".
  - **Knee caves:** ~10-15° valgus → knee stress → "knee over your middle toes".
  - **Box too high:** the thigh sits above parallel when the foot is placed (hip flexion past 100°) → pelvic tuck and knee strain → lower the box.
  - **Dropping down:** the descent takes under 1 s → knee impact → 2 s down.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Spinal load is low. Hold a rail for balance if needed, which is also useful with diabetic neuropathy (G5).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: lower box, bodyweight only, hand support. Harder: higher box (up to knee height), more load, lateral step-up.
- **Narration cues:** "Whole foot on the box." · "Drive through the heel." · "Tall at the top." · "Slow down."
- **YouTube search:** `dumbbell step up technique physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Dumbbell Step-up](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Quadriceps/DBStepUp)

### `walking-lunges` — Dumbbell walking lunge
- **Muscles:** Primary: quadriceps, gluteus maximus. Secondary: adductors, hamstrings, gluteus medius, calves, trunk.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A clear 10-15 m lane. Dumbbells at the sides.
  - Take a long stride (about 70-90 cm heel to heel for most adults) so both knees bend to about 90°.
  - Keep the feet hip-width apart side to side, like train tracks, not on a tightrope.
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand tall with the weights at your sides.
  2. Step forward, landing heel first, then forefoot.
  3. Lower until your back knee is just above the floor.
  4. Front knee over your middle toes, torso tall.
  5. Push through your front foot and step through into the next lunge.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-0-2-0 per step (lower 2 s, rise 1 s). Each step starts standing and goes down until the rear knee is 3-5 cm above the floor.
- **Breathing:** Inhale as you lower; exhale as you rise (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the quads and glutes. You should not feel knee pain or low-back pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Tightrope stance:** feet placed in one line → the pelvis twists and balance is lost → hip-width tracks.
  - **Front knee caves:** ~10-15° valgus → knee stress → "knee over toes".
  - **Back knee slams down:** the knee strikes the floor → bruised kneecap → stop 3-5 cm above.
  - **Torso pitches or arches:** the trunk leans ~30° forward or the low back arches → back strain → tall chest, ribs down.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Shorten the stride if long steps provoke back or leg symptoms. Switch to static split squats with support if balance is poor (G5).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: static split squat, reverse lunge. Harder: more load, longer distance.
- **Narration cues:** "Hip-width tracks." · "Drop the back knee." · "Tall chest." · "Push through the front heel."
- **YouTube search:** `walking lunge technique physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Dumbbell Walking Lunge](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Quadriceps/DBWalkingLunge) · [ACE: Lunge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/363/lunge/)

## 6. Hamstrings and calves

### `hamstring-curl` — Hamstring curl (seated or lying machine)
- **Muscles:** Primary: hamstrings. Secondary: gastrocnemius, sartorius, gracilis.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise the seated leg curl: an upright seat with a back pad, a thigh hold-down pad and a roller under the lower legs. Slide the back pad until the knee joint lines up with the machine's pivot, often marked with a coloured dot. Put the roller on the lower calf just above the ankles, and the thigh pad snug just above the knees ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Hamstrings/LVSeatedLegCurl)).
  - Recognise the lying leg curl: a face-down bench with a hump under the hips and a roller at the ankle end. Knees just off the bench edge, in line with the pivot, and the roller just above the heels ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/153/lying-hamstrings-curl/)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Line your knees up with the machine's pivot.
  2. Roller just above your ankles, thigh pad snug.
  3. Brace and keep your hips down.
  4. Curl your heels toward your seat.
  5. Pause and squeeze.
  6. Lower slowly until your knees are almost straight.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-1-3-0 (curl 1 s, squeeze 1 s, lower 3 s). The rep starts with the knees almost straight and ends fully curled (90° or more).
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you curl; inhale as you lower (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the backs of the thighs. You should not feel the low back arching (lying version), cramp behind the knee or tingling down the leg.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Hips lift (lying):** hips rise 5-10 cm off the pad and the lumbar spine extends ~10-20° → low-back extension load → lighter load, hips pinned.
  - **Swinging:** the weight is jerked up with momentum → hamstring strain → 3 s lowering.
  - **Knee off the pivot:** about 5 cm out of line → knee shear and a sliding pad → adjust the back pad.
  - **Partial range:** curling only ~45° → less hamstring work → full curl.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** The seated version builds more hamstring ([Maeo 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179/)) and avoids prone arching. But its start position (hips bent, knees straight) tensions the sciatic nerve (G4). With sciatica, use the range limiter so the knees start bent about 30° and recline the back pad, or use the lying curl with the hips pinned.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: lighter load, limited range. Harder: more load, 4 s lowering, single-leg.
- **Narration cues:** "Hips down." · "Curl." · "Squeeze." · "Slow down."
- **YouTube search:** `seated leg curl machine setup physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Lever Seated Leg Curl](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Hamstrings/LVSeatedLegCurl) · [ACE: Lying Hamstrings Curl](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/153/lying-hamstrings-curl/) · [Maeo 2021: seated vs prone leg curl](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179/)

### `calf-raises` — Standing calf raise
- **Muscles:** Primary: gastrocnemius. Secondary: soleus.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise the standing calf machine: shoulder pads on a lever or weight stack above a raised foot block. It loads the spine through the shoulders.
  - Set the pad height so the pads sit just below your shoulders with the heels fully lowered; then stand up into them with straight, soft knees ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Gastrocnemius/LVStandingCalfRaise)).
  - Spine-free options: single-leg bodyweight raises on a step holding a rail, or a calf press with the legs straight out in front, on a calf-press or leg-press machine ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/294/calf-raise/)).
  - In every version, the balls of the feet go on the edge with the heels free.
- **Execution:**
  1. Balls of your feet on the edge, heels hanging.
  2. Knees straight but soft. Stand tall.
  3. Lower your heels slowly into a stretch.
  4. Pause for two seconds.
  5. Rise as high as you can onto your big toes.
  6. Squeeze, then lower slowly.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-1-2-2 (rise 1 s, squeeze 1 s, lower 2 s, 2 s pause in the stretch). The rep goes from the heels below the step (full stretch) to a full rise.
- **Breathing:** Steady breathing; exhale on the way up (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the calves. You should not feel Achilles pain, foot cramp, or compression through the spine on the machine.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Bouncing:** no pause, rebounding out of the stretch → Achilles stress and less muscle work → 2 s pause.
  - **Half reps:** the heels never drop below the step, or rise only halfway → less range → full stretch, full rise.
  - **Rolling out:** the ankle inverts ~10-15° and the weight moves onto the little toes → ankle strain → press through the big toe.
  - **Bending the knees:** the knees flex ~20-30° to bounce → it becomes a mini-squat → soft but fixed knees.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** Skip the shoulder-loaded machine (and barbell versions) while the back is irritable. Use single-leg bodyweight raises, a dumbbell in one hand, the leg-press calf press or seated-calf-raise. With neuropathy, keep shoes on, hold a support and check the feet afterwards (G5).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: both legs, holding a support. Harder: single leg, a dumbbell, slower lowering.
- **Narration cues:** "Pause in the stretch." · "Up onto the big toe." · "Squeeze." · "Slow down."
- **YouTube search:** `calf raise proper form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Lever Standing Calf Raise](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Gastrocnemius/LVStandingCalfRaise) · [ACE: Calf Raise (machine)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/294/calf-raise/)

### `seated-calf-raise` — Seated calf raise
- **Muscles:** Primary: soleus. Secondary: gastrocnemius, which works less because the knee is bent ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Soleus/LVSeatedCalfRaise)).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a low seat with a padded lever resting on the lower thighs, a foot platform, a plate horn and a safety lever.
  - Adjust the pad so it sits snugly on the lower thighs, just above the knees, with the balls of the feet on the platform edge and the heels free.
  - Raise the heels slightly, then push the safety lever away to release it. Pull it back to lock after the last rep.
  - No machine: sit on a bench with dumbbells on the knees and the balls of the feet on a plate.
- **Execution:**
  1. Balls of your feet on the edge, pad on your lower thighs.
  2. Lift slightly and release the safety lever.
  3. Lower your heels into a deep stretch.
  4. Pause for two seconds.
  5. Push up as high as you can and squeeze.
  6. Lock the lever after the last rep.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-1-2-2 (rise 1 s, squeeze 1 s, lower 2 s, 2 s pause in the stretch). The rep goes from full stretch to a full rise.
- **Breathing:** Steady breathing; exhale on the push (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the lower calves. You should not feel Achilles pain or the pad pressing on the kneecaps.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Bouncing:** rebounding out of the bottom → Achilles strain → 2 s pause.
  - **Half reps:** the heels stay level with the platform → less range → full stretch.
  - **Rolling out:** the ankle inverts ~10-15° → ankle strain → press through the big toe.
  - **Pushing with the hands:** the arms press or pull the pad → the calves are under-loaded → hands rest lightly.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** No spinal load, so this is the preferred calf exercise while the back is irritable.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: bodyweight seated heel raises. Harder: more load, longer pauses.
- **Narration cues:** "Deep stretch." · "Pause." · "All the way up." · "Slow down."
- **YouTube search:** `seated calf raise machine proper form`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Lever Seated Calf Raise](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/Soleus/LVSeatedCalfRaise)

## 7. Core: stability

### `plank` — Front plank (forearms)
- **Muscles:** Primary: rectus abdominis, transversus abdominis, obliques. Secondary: gluteus maximus, quadriceps, serratus anterior, shoulders.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Mat. Forearms on the floor shoulder-width apart, elbows under the shoulders, palms down.
  - Feet hip-width, toes tucked under ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Elbows under your shoulders, forearms on the mat.
  2. Step your feet back, toes tucked.
  3. Squeeze your glutes and thighs; ribs down.
  4. Lift into a straight line from head to heels.
  5. Breathe and hold.
  6. Lower your knees to rest.
- **Tempo & ROM:** Holds of 10 s × 5-6 with about 5 s rest, or 20-30 s once form is solid. End the hold when the line breaks.
- **Breathing:** Keep breathing, with short exhales through pursed lips. Count breaths aloud (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the abs, the fronts of the shoulders and the glutes. You should not feel low-back ache or shoulder pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Hips sag:** the pelvis drops 5-10 cm below the head-heel line and the lumbar spine extends ~15-20° → facet compression → squeeze the glutes, tuck the tailbone, shorter holds.
  - **Hips pike:** the hips rise 10-20 cm above the line → the abs are offloaded → lower the hips to the line.
  - **Head drops or cranes:** the neck flexes or extends ~20-30° → neck strain → eyes on the floor just ahead.
  - **Shoulders collapse:** the chest sinks between the shoulder blades → shoulder strain → push the floor away.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** If the hips sag or the back is extension-sensitive, regress to an incline plank (forearms on a bench) or a knee plank.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: incline plank, knee plank. Harder: more 10 s reps, or the long-lever posterior-tilt plank, which activates the abdominals more than the standard plank ([Schoenfeld 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25325773/)).
- **Narration cues:** "Straight line." · "Squeeze your glutes." · "Ribs down." · "Breathe."
- **YouTube search:** `plank form common mistakes physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Front Plank](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/) · [ExRx: Front Plank](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/RectusAbdominis/BWFrontPlank) · [Schoenfeld 2014: plank variations EMG](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25325773/)

### `side-plank` — Side plank (side bridge)
- **Muscles:** Primary: obliques, quadratus lumborum, transversus abdominis. Secondary: gluteus medius, shoulder stabilisers. Among the isometric abdominal exercises tested, the side bridge best combined high abdominal-wall activation with low psoas activity ([Juker 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9502361/)).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Mat. Elbow directly under the shoulder, forearm on the floor in front of you.
  - Knees bent 90° for the easier version ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/100/side-plank-modified/)), or legs straight with the top foot in front of the bottom foot.
  - Top hand on the hip.
- **Execution:**
  1. Lie on your side with your elbow under your shoulder.
  2. Knees bent, or legs straight with the top foot in front.
  3. Brace and lift your hips off the floor.
  4. Make a straight line from your head to your knees or feet.
  5. Breathe and hold.
  6. Lower, then switch sides.
- **Tempo & ROM:** Holds of 10 s × 3-5 per side.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you lift (ACE), then breathe steadily through the hold (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the side of the trunk and hip on the bottom side. You should not feel shoulder pain or a pinch in the low back.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Hips sag:** the bottom hip drops 5-10 cm and the trunk side-bends ~10-15° → compression on the low side of the spine → hips high, shorter holds.
  - **Rolling forward or back:** the pelvis rotates ~15-20° → twisting, and the obliques miss the target → stack hip over hip.
  - **Elbow out of place:** the elbow sits 5-10 cm beyond or in front of the shoulder → shoulder strain → elbow under the shoulder.
  - **Shoulder shrug:** the shoulder rises toward the ear → neck strain → push the floor away.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** One of the McGill "big 3". Start from the knees.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: knees bent, or a standing side lean against a wall. Harder: straight legs, top leg raised, or the rolling side plank (side to side through a front plank).
- **Narration cues:** "Elbow under your shoulder." · "Hips high." · "Stack your hips." · "Breathe."
- **YouTube search:** `McGill side bridge progression`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Side Plank (modified)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/100/side-plank-modified/) · [McGill & Karpowicz 2009: side-bridge forms and progressions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154838/) · [Juker 1998: psoas and abdominal-wall EMG](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9502361/)

### `mcgill-curl-up` — McGill curl-up
- **Muscles:** Primary: rectus abdominis. Secondary: obliques, which work harder when the trunk is braced. Psoas activity stays below 10% of maximum, compared with 15-35% for sit-ups ([Juker 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9502361/)).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Mat. Lie on your back with one knee bent (foot flat) and the other leg straight.
  - Slide both hands, palms down, under the natural arch of the low back to keep it neutral. Don't flatten it.
  - Elbows on the floor is easier; elbows lifted is harder.
- **Execution:**
  1. Lie on your back, one knee bent, one leg straight.
  2. Slide your hands under your lower back.
  3. Brace your abs gently.
  4. Lift your head and shoulders just off the floor, a few centimetres.
  5. Keep your head and neck still, as one unit.
  6. Hold for 8-10 seconds, breathing.
  7. Lower. Switch the bent leg halfway through the set.
- **Tempo & ROM:** Holds of 8-10 s in a descending pyramid (for example 6, then 4, then 2 reps) with 20-30 s between rounds. The shoulder blades only just clear the floor, and the low-back curve stays the same throughout.
- **Breathing:** Breathe continuously during every hold (G1). Breathing during these holds changes which muscles work hardest ([McGill & Karpowicz 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154838/)).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the upper abs. You should not feel neck strain or low-back pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Flattening the back:** the pelvis tucks and the lumbar spine flexes ~10-15° onto the hands → the neutral spine the drill trains is lost → keep the arch over your hands.
  - **Chin to chest:** the neck flexes ~30-45° → neck strain → "head and neck as one unit, eyes to the ceiling".
  - **Sitting up too high:** the trunk rises more than 20-30°, into a sit-up → lumbar flexion and psoas pull → the shoulder blades just clear the floor.
  - **Holding the breath:** breathing stops for the whole hold, often with a reddening face → blood-pressure spike (G2) → breathe through the hold.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Designed for low-back rehabilitation. Partial curl-ups gave the best abdominal challenge for the spine compression they caused ([Axler & McGill 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9219209/)).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: head lift only, elbows down. Harder: elbows lifted, deep breaths during the hold, a longer pyramid.
- **Narration cues:** "Head and neck still." · "Just off the floor." · "Breathe." · "Hold."
- **YouTube search:** `McGill curl up big 3 technique`
- **Sources:** [McGill & Karpowicz 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154838/) · [Axler & McGill 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9219209/) · [Juker 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9502361/)

### `bird-dog` — Bird dog
- **Muscles:** Primary: erector spinae and multifidus (isometric), gluteus maximus. Secondary: obliques, deltoid, serratus anterior.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Mat. Hands under the shoulders, knees under the hips, spine neutral ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/14/bird-dog/)).
  - A dowel or light bar laid across the low back shows any hip rotation.
- **Execution:**
  1. Hands under your shoulders, knees under your hips.
  2. Brace, back flat like a table.
  3. Reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back.
  4. Stop at shoulder and hip height, no higher.
  5. Make a fist and push the heel back. Hold for 8-10 seconds.
  6. Return, then switch sides.
- **Tempo & ROM:** Holds of 8-10 s, 5-6 per side. The arm and leg travel from the floor to horizontal.
- **Breathing:** Breathe steadily during the hold (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the glute of the raised leg, the back muscles and the shoulder. You should not feel a pinch in the low back.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Leg too high:** the foot rises ~15-20° above hip height and the low back arches ~10-15° → extension load → stop at hip height (ACE).
  - **Hip rotation:** the pelvis rotates ~10-20° as the raised hip opens → twisting the spine → keep the hips level.
  - **Weight shift or sag:** the trunk shifts 5-10 cm sideways or sags → the brace is lost → brace before lifting.
  - **Head up:** the neck extends ~20-30° → neck strain → look at the floor between your hands.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** Single-leg extension tasks put low load on the spine ([Callaghan 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9442191/)). Regress to leg only or arm only.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: arm only, or leg only. Harder: longer holds, or drawing small squares with the hand and foot, which raises muscle activation ([McGill & Karpowicz 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154838/)).
- **Narration cues:** "Back flat." · "Reach long." · "Hips level." · "Hold and breathe."
- **YouTube search:** `McGill bird dog technique`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Bird Dog](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/14/bird-dog/) · [McGill & Karpowicz 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154838/) · [Callaghan 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9442191/)

### `dead-bug` — Dead bug
- **Muscles:** Primary: transversus abdominis, rectus abdominis, obliques. Secondary: hip flexors, shoulder stabilisers.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Mat. Lie on your back with your arms pointing at the ceiling over your shoulders.
  - Hips and knees bent 90°, knees over the hips. Low back resting gently on the floor.
- **Execution:**
  1. Lie on your back, arms up, knees over your hips.
  2. Ribs down; low back resting on the floor.
  3. Slowly reach one arm overhead and lower the opposite heel toward the floor.
  4. Keep your back still. No arching.
  5. Bring them back, then switch sides.
- **Tempo & ROM:** Limbs go out in 3 s, hold 1 s, and come back in 3 s, for 6-8 per side. The heel and hand stop just above the floor.
- **Breathing:** Inhale as you lower and exhale as you return ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/147/supine-dead-bug/)). Never hold the breath. Use a gentle 360° brace rather than only drawing in the navel (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the deep abs. You should not feel the low back lifting or aching, or a pinch in the hip flexors.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Back arches:** the low back lifts 2-4 cm off the floor (lordosis up ~10-15°) → extension load, and the abs switch off → shorten the reach.
  - **Ribs flare:** the rib cage lifts as the arm goes overhead → the brace is lost → "ribs down".
  - **Rushing:** the limbs drop in under 1 s → momentum replaces control → 3 s each way.
  - **Straight leg too soon:** a fully straight leg is lowered → the long lever pulls the back into an arch → bent-knee heel taps.
- **Back safety:** **OK.**
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: bent-knee heel taps, arms only or legs only. Harder: straighter legs, a light weight in the hands, band resistance.
- **Narration cues:** "Ribs down." · "Back still." · "Slow." · "Breathe."
- **YouTube search:** `dead bug exercise physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Supine Dead Bug](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/147/supine-dead-bug/)

### `pallof-press` — Pallof press (anti-rotation press)
- **Muscles:** Primary: obliques, transversus abdominis, rectus abdominis, all resisting rotation. Secondary: gluteus medius, shoulders, chest.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a cable column (or a resistance band) with a D-handle.
  - Set the pulley at chest height and stand side-on to it, about 1-1.5 m away, so the cable is taut with the handle at the breastbone ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/332/standing-anti-rotation-press/)).
  - Feet hip- to shoulder-width, knees soft, both hands on the handle.
- **Execution:**
  1. Stand side-on to the cable, handle at your breastbone.
  2. Feet hip-width, knees soft. Brace.
  3. Press the handle straight out from your chest.
  4. Don't let it turn you. Hold for 2-3 seconds.
  5. Bring it back to your chest.
  6. Finish the set, then turn around for the other side.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 2-3-2-0 (press out 2 s, hold 3 s, return 2 s). The hands travel from the breastbone to full reach.
- **Breathing:** Exhale slowly as you press and through the hold; inhale as you return (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the obliques and deep abs. You should not feel the low back twisting or shoulder pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Rotating toward the stack:** the trunk turns ~10-20° and the hands drift off the midline → the spine twists and the abs switch off → lighter load, hands on the midline.
  - **Hip shift or lean:** the hips slide 5-10 cm or the trunk side-bends ~10° → neutral is lost → ribs stacked over the pelvis.
  - **Shrugging and bent arms:** the shoulders lift and the elbows stay bent → shoulder and neck strain → shoulders down, press straight.
  - **Standing too far out or loading too heavy:** the hands can't stay on the midline for the full hold → the faults above creep in → step in or lighten the load.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** It trains the trunk without spinal motion, and is the recommended replacement for russian-twist.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: tall- or half-kneeling, which was the most stable variation tested ([Juan-Recio 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11857476/)). Harder: feet together, split or tandem stance, longer holds.
- **Narration cues:** "Press straight out." · "Don't let it turn you." · "Ribs down." · "Breathe."
- **YouTube search:** `Pallof press technique physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Standing Anti-Rotation Press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/332/standing-anti-rotation-press/) · [Juan-Recio 2025: Pallof press variations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11857476/)

## 8. Loaded carries

### `suitcase-carry` — Suitcase carry (one hand)
- **Muscles:** Primary: obliques and quadratus lumborum (mainly on the side opposite the weight), gluteus medius. Secondary: grip, trapezius, spinal erectors.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - One dumbbell or kettlebell. Start at about 10-15% of body weight.
  - Pick it up from a bench, or squat or hinge down to it. Never side-bend to grab it (G6).
  - A clear 15-20 m lane.
- **Execution:**
  1. Pick up one weight with a squat, not a side bend.
  2. Stand tall with your shoulders level.
  3. Brace, and don't lean toward the weight.
  4. Walk with short, smooth steps.
  5. Set it down with a squat, then switch hands.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 20-40 m or 30-45 s per side, with 30-60 s rest.
- **Breathing:** Breathe steadily and exhale every two or three steps (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the side of the trunk opposite the weight, the grip and the hips. You should not feel low-back pain or pain down the leg.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Leaning toward the weight:** the trunk side-bends ~10-15° and the loaded shoulder drops 3-5 cm → lopsided spine compression → lighter load; "shoulders level".
  - **Over-correcting away:** leaning ~10° away from the weight → still asymmetric → stand stacked.
  - **Shrugging the loaded shoulder:** the shoulder hikes toward the ear → neck and trapezius strain → "shoulder down".
  - **Twisting with each step:** the trunk rotates ~10-15° and the weight swings → twisting load → slower steps, weight still.
- **Back safety:** **Modify.** A one-hand load raises spine compression: 30 kg in one hand exceeded 2,800 N, against 1,570 N with the same load split between both hands ([McGill 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23384188/)). While the back is sensitive, keep the load moderate (cap about 20-25% of body weight). Heavier loads belong in farmer-carry.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: stationary suitcase hold, lighter weight. Harder: heavier weight, longer distance.
- **Narration cues:** "Stand tall." · "Shoulders level." · "Don't lean." · "Breathe."
- **YouTube search:** `suitcase carry technique physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [McGill 2013: one- vs two-hand carrying](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23384188/) · [McGill 2009: strongman events including the suitcase carry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19528856/) · [Ellestad 2024: loaded carry EMG](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042841/)

### `farmer-carry` — Farmer's carry (two hands)
- **Muscles:** Primary: grip and forearms, upper trapezius, trunk stabilisers on both sides. Secondary: glutes, quadriceps, calves.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Two equal dumbbells or kettlebells. Start at about 25-35% of body weight in total.
  - Lift them from a bench or rack, or deadlift them with a flat back (G6).
  - A clear 15-20 m lane. Tight grip, arms by your sides ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/359/farmer-s-carry/)).
- **Execution:**
  1. Pick up both weights with a flat back.
  2. Stand tall, shoulders down and back.
  3. Brace and walk with short, quick steps.
  4. Keep the weights still at your sides.
  5. Set them down with a squat or a hinge.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 20-40 m or 30-60 s, with about 60 s rest.
- **Breathing:** Breathe steadily; don't hold the breath (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the grip, upper trapezius and whole trunk. You should not feel low-back pain or pain down the leg.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Rounding to pick up or set down:** the lumbar spine flexes ~20-30° to reach the floor → loaded flexion → squat or hinge, or start from a bench.
  - **Shoulders rolled forward and shrugged:** the shoulders protract and rise 3-5 cm → neck strain → "shoulders down and back".
  - **Leaning forward or back:** the trunk sits ~10-15° off vertical → back-muscle overload → stand stacked.
  - **Weights swinging:** the bells hit the thighs and the trunk twists → twisting load → shorter steps.
- **Back safety:** **OK.** A balanced load produces less spine compression than the same load in one hand ([McGill 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23384188/)). While sensitive, lift the weights from bench height. With diabetes, wear supportive shoes and check the feet afterwards (G5).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: stationary farmer hold, lighter weights. Harder: heavier weights, longer distance.
- **Narration cues:** "Tall." · "Shoulders down." · "Short steps." · "Breathe."
- **YouTube search:** `farmers carry technique strength coach`
- **Sources:** [ACE: Farmer's Carry](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/359/farmer-s-carry/) · [McGill 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23384188/) · [Ellestad 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042841/)

## 9. Core: flexion and rotation

### `cable-crunch` — Kneeling cable crunch
- **Muscles:** Primary: rectus abdominis. Secondary: obliques.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Recognise it: a cable column with a rope on the high pulley.
  - Kneel facing the stack about 30-60 cm away. Hold the rope ends beside your head, wrists against the head ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/RectusAbdominis/CBKneelingCrunch)).
  - The hips stay at a fixed height throughout.
- **Execution:**
  1. Kneel facing the stack with the rope beside your head.
  2. Keep your hips still and brace.
  3. Curl your ribs toward your hips.
  4. Bring your elbows toward your thighs and breathe out.
  5. Uncurl slowly without moving your hips.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-1-2-0 (crunch 1 s, hold 1 s, uncurl 2 s). The spine moves from upright to curled while the hips stay fixed.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you crunch; inhale as you uncurl (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the abs. You should not feel low-back pain, the hip flexors pulling, or neck strain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Hinging at the hips:** the hips sit back 20-30 cm, so the hips flex instead of the spine → the hip flexors do the work → hips still, "ribs to hips".
  - **Pulling with the arms:** the arms drag the rope down → the abs are unloaded → hands fixed at the head.
  - **Jerking a heavy stack:** fast, ballistic flexion → disc stress → lighter, controlled reps.
  - **Neck cranked:** the chin is jammed onto the chest → neck strain → keep space between chin and chest (ExRx).
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable.** It is repeated loaded lumbar flexion, the pattern that produced disc herniation in a porcine model ([Callaghan & McGill 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11114441/)). Swap to mcgill-curl-up, dead-bug or pallof-press. When calm, use light loads and a small range.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: mcgill-curl-up. Harder: more load, only for users with no back history.
- **Narration cues:** "Hips still." · "Ribs to hips." · "Breathe out." · "Slow on the way up."
- **YouTube search:** `kneeling cable crunch proper form`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Cable Kneeling Crunch](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/RectusAbdominis/CBKneelingCrunch) · [Callaghan & McGill 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11114441/)

### `hanging-knee-raise` — Hanging knee raise
- **Muscles:** Primary: hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris). Secondary: rectus abdominis and obliques, which only work dynamically if the pelvis curls up ([ExRx](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/HipFlexors/BWHangingLegRaise)); grip, latissimus dorsi.
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - A pull-up bar with an overhand grip slightly wider than the shoulders.
  - Or a captain's chair: a tall tower with a back pad and padded forearm rests with handles. The back is supported and grip isn't the limit, so it is preferred for a back history.
- **Execution:**
  1. Hang with straight arms and pull your shoulders down.
  2. Brace and keep your legs still.
  3. Raise your knees to hip height.
  4. Curl your pelvis up slightly.
  5. Lower slowly. No swinging.
- **Tempo & ROM:** 1-1-2-1 (raise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s, 1 s still hang). The rep starts with the legs hanging and ends with the thighs horizontal plus a small pelvic curl.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as the knees rise; inhale as they lower (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the lower abs, hip flexors and grip. You should not feel low-back pain or shoulder pain.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Swinging:** the body swings ~20-30° forward and back → momentum and shoulder stress → a still hang at the bottom, or use the captain's chair.
  - **Arching at the bottom:** the lumbar spine extends ~10-15° as the legs drop → the psoas pulls the low back into loaded extension → bent knees, slight tuck.
  - **Dropping the legs:** they lower in under 1 s → uncontrolled spinal loading → 2 s down.
  - **Passive hang:** the shoulders creep up by the ears → shoulder strain → "shoulders down".
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable.** When calm, use bent knees, no swing and the captain's chair. Hanging straight-leg raises produced about 3,000 N of spine compression ([McGill 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111163/)). Swap to dead-bug, or a reverse crunch with a small range.
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier: captain's-chair knee raise, dead-bug. Harder: knees to the chest, then straight legs, only for calm backs.
- **Narration cues:** "Shoulders down." · "Knees up." · "Curl the pelvis." · "Slow down."
- **YouTube search:** `hanging knee raise proper form no swinging`
- **Sources:** [ExRx: Hanging Leg Raise (knee raise)](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/HipFlexors/BWHangingLegRaise) · [McGill 2015: hanging leg raise spine load](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111163/)

### `russian-twist` — Russian twist
- **Muscles:** Primary: obliques. Secondary: rectus abdominis, hip flexors (holding the lean).
- **Equipment & setup:**
  - Mat. Sit with knees bent about 90° and heels on the floor.
  - Lean the torso back about 45° with a long spine. Hold a light medicine ball or plate (2-5 kg) at the chest.
- **Execution:**
  1. Sit tall, knees bent, heels down.
  2. Lean back slightly, keeping a long spine.
  3. Brace and turn your chest to one side.
  4. Move slowly, so the ribs and the weight turn together.
  5. Turn to the other side.
- **Tempo & ROM:** About 2 s to each side with a 1 s pause at the centre, 6-10 per side. The chest rotates about 30-45° each way from the centre.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you turn; inhale through the centre (G1).
- **Feel / don't feel:** Feel the obliques. You should not feel low-back pain or leg symptoms.
- **Mistakes:**
  - **Rounded back while twisting:** the lumbar spine flexes ~20-30° while rotating under load → disc injury; twisting combined with repeated flexion caused annular delamination in porcine discs ([Marshall & McGill 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19815318/)) → long spine, or swap.
  - **Arms only:** the arms swing ~45° while the chest stays square → no trunk work → turn the chest.
  - **Fast, ballistic twisting:** the trunk whips ~45° each way in under half a second → torque spikes → 2 s per side.
  - **Feet lifted with a load:** heels 10-15 cm off the floor → more hip-flexor pull and lumbar flexion → heels down.
- **Back safety:** **Avoid when irritable**, and replace by default for anyone with a back or sciatica history, because it combines flexion and rotation. Use pallof-press instead. Other alternatives are side-plank, suitcase-carry, or a ball-supported twist with a neutral spine ([ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/65/russian-twist/)).
- **Progressions / regressions:** Easier, and the replacement: half-kneeling pallof-press. Harder (no back history only): heavier ball, feet up.
- **Narration cues:** "Long spine." · "Turn the chest." · "Slow." · "Breathe."
