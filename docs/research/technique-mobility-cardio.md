# Technique research: mobility, stretching and cardio

**Scope.** This doc covers technique, dosage, breathing, safety and coaching cues for two parts of the voice-guided session (15 min stretching/mobility → strength → cardio): the stretching/mobility block and the cardio block. It feeds the narration and the 3D "correct form" and "common mistake" animations. Strength technique and the overall back-pain evidence are in separate research docs. This is general educational content, not individual medical advice. Researched 2026-10-05.

## 1. Conventions

- **Back & sciatica labels.**
  - **OK**: suitable for most people with low back pain or one-sided sciatica, as long as the global stop rules are followed.
  - **Modify**: use the stated modification or regression, and always do so on irritable days.
  - **Avoid when irritable**: leave it out on irritable days (on the symptomatic side), then bring it back through the regression once symptoms are calm.
- **Irritable** means leg symptoms that are easily provoked, present at rest, or slow (more than 30 min) to settle after activity.
- **Mistake angles** are approximate deviations from the correct position, written for animators (0° = correct or neutral). Each mistake reads Mistake → Risk → Fix.
- **Breath counts** assume a calm breath of about 5-6 s, so a 30 s hold is about 5 breaths. No item cues a breath hold by default. Box breathing's classic holds are opt-in and are never offered with hypertension or diabetic eye disease.
- **Dosage** lines are for the pre-strength block. Longer work is listed under "Deep-stretch progression" and belongs after the strength block or on separate days.

## 2. Global rules (apply to every item)

1. **How hard.** Stretch to gentle tension (about 3-4/10), never sharp pain. Pain during exercise of up to 5/10 is acceptable only if it settles quickly and is no worse the next morning. Above that, reduce the reps, speed or range ([NHS inform 0-10 guide](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/neck-and-back-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-back-pain/)).
2. **Nerve rule.** Ease off at once and use the regression if you get tingling, numbness, burning or electric pain, or any symptom that spreads further down the arm or leg (peripheralisation). Symptoms that retreat toward the spine (centralisation) are a good sign. Centralisation and directional preference are positive prognostic factors in low back pain ([May et al. 2018 systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30273918/)).
3. **Red flags: stop and seek urgent care** ([NHS sciatica](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sciatica/)):
   - sciatica in both legs
   - severe or worsening weakness or numbness in both legs
   - numbness around the genitals or anus
   - new difficulty controlling the bladder or bowels
4. **Pre-strength dose.** Keep static holds to 20-30 s and no more than 60 s total per muscle, and end the block with dynamic drills.
   - Holding a static stretch for 60 s or more per muscle just before lifting costs about 4.6% performance, against about 1.1% for under 60 s. Dynamic activity afterwards offsets the loss ([Behm et al. 2016 systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26642915/)).
   - General targets: about 60 s per stretch per muscle group on 2 or more days a week ([ACSM 2011 position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556/)); 10-30 s holds × 2-4 reps ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)); 20-30 s × 2-3 sets ([NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/neck-and-back-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-back-pain/)).
5. **Building real flexibility.** Range gains track weekly time under stretch. Aim for at least 5 min per muscle group per week, spread over 5 or more days ([Thomas et al. 2018 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29506306/)).
   - Increase hold time first (30 → 60 → 90-120 s), then range.
   - Light contract-relax (≤30-50% effort, keep breathing) and loaded end-range strength work are alternatives. Strength training improves range about as much as stretching ([Afonso et al. 2021 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8067745/)).
6. **Hypertension.** No breath-holding or straining. Valsalva-style effort can drive arterial pressure to extreme levels: a group mean of 320/250 mmHg was recorded during a double-leg press ([MacDougall et al. 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3980383/)).
   - Exhale while deepening or pushing, and use light grips and light bands.
   - Rise slowly from the floor. Blood pressure stays lower for up to 22 h after endurance exercise ([ACSM 2004 position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15076798/)), and some blood-pressure medications can cause exercise-related hypotension ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)).
7. **Heart-rate caveat.** Beta-blockers lower heart rate at rest and during exercise, so heart-rate zones under-read effort. Use the talk test and RPE instead ([Eston & Connolly 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8776008/); [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)).
8. **Diabetes.** Limited joint mobility is common (glycation stiffens tissues), so regular stretching is recommended. Stretching does not lower glucose on its own ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)).
   - With peripheral neuropathy: wear supportive shoes for standing drills and cardio, check the feet daily, and stretch gently, because overstretch may not be felt.
   - With severe non-proliferative or unstable proliferative retinopathy: avoid vigorous work, jumping or jarring, head-down positions and breath-holding. This is relevant to child's pose, box-breathing holds and hard intervals.

## 3. Sciatica and safety quick reference

**Recommendations for the back/sciatica track.**

- **Avoid when irritable** (leave out on irritable days, at least on the symptomatic side):
  - `childs-pose`: sustained end-range lumbar flexion.
  - `knee-to-opposite-shoulder`: close to the FAIR sciatic-provocation position.
  - `supine-hamstring-stretch-strap` on the symptomatic leg: it is the straight-leg-raise nerve test position.
  - `rowing-machine`: repeated loaded lumbar flexion at the catch.
- **Consider excluding from the default library:**
  - `knee-to-opposite-shoulder`: `supine-figure-4` and `seated-piriformis-stretch` cover the same muscles with less nerve provocation.
  - `rowing-machine`: walking, bike and elliptical cover the cardio need.
- **Auto-swap on irritable days:**
  - `wall-calf-stretch` → `soleus-stretch`
  - `supine-hamstring-stretch-strap` → `sciatic-nerve-glide-supine`
  - `childs-pose` → hands-on-chair hip hinge
  - `knee-to-chest` → single knee, partial range
  - `incline-treadmill-walk` → 0-3% incline
- **Direction-specific items** (`prone-press-up`, `knee-to-chest`, `childs-pose`, `cat-cow`) should follow each person's response:
  - Flexion-sensitive (typical disc) backs tend to dislike end-range flexion.
  - Extension-sensitive (stenosis-type) backs tend to dislike end-range extension.
  - Continue only when symptoms stay the same or centralise.
- **Hypertension and retinopathy:**
  - `box-breathing` defaults to the no-hold variant.
  - `childs-pose`: keep the head supported and rise slowly.
  - Interval "fast" segments are capped at RPE 7.

## 4. Neck

### `chin-tuck` — Chin tuck (cervical retraction)
- **Region / targets:** Neck — deep neck flexors (longus colli/capitis); light suboccipital lengthening.
- **Setup:** Firm chair, sitting tall; or lying on your back, head on a folded towel.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit tall, shoulders relaxed.
  2. Look straight ahead, eyes level.
  3. Glide your head straight back into a small double chin.
  4. Hold gently — no nodding, no tipping up.
  5. Slide forward to neutral.
- **Dosage:** 5 s hold × 10 reps.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you glide back; one slow breath per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: light work deep in the front of the neck. Stop if: dizziness, visual change, headache, or arm pain/tingling.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — no lumbar load. Regression: lying version, pressing the head gently into the towel.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Lying nods for endurance: tongue on palate, nod 2 cm, hold 10 s × 10. Add range in 2 cm steps, then a small head lift.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Chin drops into a nod (~20-30° upper-neck flexion) → no retraction, neck strain → Eyes level; glide horizontally.
  - Head tips back (~10-15° extension) → upper-neck joint compression → Gaze on the horizon.
  - Shoulders shrug 2-3 cm, jaw clenched → trapezius and jaw take over → Shoulders heavy, jaw soft.
- **Cues:** "Eyes level." · "Slide back, like closing a drawer." · "Small double chin." · "Breathe out."
- **YouTube:** `chin tuck exercise physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [HSS — Seated stretches](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/seated-stretches); [Cleveland Clinic — Posture exercises](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/posture-exercises); [NHS Ayrshire & Arran — Neck pain exercises](https://www.nhsaaa.net/musculoskeletal-msk-service-patient-portal/neck-msk-patient-portal/neck-pain-exercises-msk-patient-portal/)

### `upper-trap-stretch` — Upper trapezius stretch
- **Region / targets:** Neck/shoulder — upper trapezius; secondary scalenes.
- **Setup:** Firm chair, sitting tall; hold the seat edge with the hand on the side being stretched.
- **Steps:**
  1. Hold the seat with your left hand; let that shoulder sink.
  2. Keep your nose pointing forward.
  3. Tip your right ear toward your right shoulder.
  4. Stop at the first gentle stretch; optionally rest your right hand on your head — weight only.
  5. Come up slowly; switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 20-30 s × 2 per side.
- **Breathing:** Slow nasal inhale, long exhale as the shoulder drops; ~5 breaths per 30 s.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: stretch from neck to shoulder top. Stop if: arm tingling, numbness or burning (ease off), dizziness, sharp pain.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — seated, no lumbar load. Regression: smaller tilt, no hand on the head.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s × 2-3. Light contract-relax: press the head 20-30% into your hand for 5 s, keep breathing, then ease further.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Head rotates 20-30° (nose turns down or up) → load shifts to neck joints → Nose forward, ear straight to shoulder.
  - Stretched shoulder hikes 2-3 cm → slack muscle, no stretch → Hold the seat; shoulder down first.
  - Hand yanks toward full side-bend (~45°) or bounces → joint or nerve irritation → Hand rests; static hold.
- **Cues:** "Shoulder heavy." · "Ear to shoulder, nose forward." · "Hand rests, never pulls." · "Breathe out, soften."
- **YouTube:** `upper trapezius stretch physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [HSS — Stretches before bed](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/stretches-before-bed); [Cleveland Clinic — Posture exercises](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/posture-exercises); [NHS — Flexibility exercises](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-and-flexibility-exercises/flexibility-exercises/)

### `levator-scapulae-stretch` — Levator scapulae stretch
- **Region / targets:** Neck/shoulder blade — levator scapulae.
- **Setup:** Front of a firm chair, sitting tall. For the left side, the left hand holds the seat behind you.
- **Steps:**
  1. Anchor your left hand on the seat behind you.
  2. Turn your head about 45° to the right.
  3. Nod down toward your right hip pocket.
  4. Rest your right hand on your head — weight only, never pull.
  5. Lift your head, turn to centre; switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 20-30 s × 2 per side.
- **Breathing:** Slow nasal inhale, long exhale as the shoulder blade sinks; ~5 breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: stretch from the back of the neck to the top of the shoulder blade. Stop if: arm tingling, dizziness, visual change, headache.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — seated, no lumbar load. Regression: smaller nod, no hand weight.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s × 2-3. Light contract-relax: lift the head 20-30% into your hand for 5 s, keep breathing, then nod slightly deeper.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Head turned toward the stretched side, or not turned → stretches upper trapezius instead → Turn ~45° away, then nod.
  - Hand cranks the head 10-15° past comfort, or bouncing → neck strain, nerve irritation → Hand weight only.
  - Anchor released or upper back slumps 15-20° → stretch lost → Hold the seat, sit tall, then nod.
- **Cues:** "Turn, then nod." · "Nose toward the opposite pocket." · "Hand rests, never pulls."
- **YouTube:** `levator scapulae stretch physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [HSS — Seated stretches](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/seated-stretches); [Physiopedia — Levator scapulae](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Levator_Scapulae); [Phys Ther Sport 2017 — levator stretching positions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28578252/)

## 5. Upper back / thoracic

### `cat-cow` — Cat-cow (McGill-style cat-camel)
- **Region / targets:** Whole spine — gentle flexion-extension mobility; a motion drill, not an end-range stretch.
- **Setup:** Mat, all fours: hands under shoulders, knees under hips, knees padded.
- **Steps:**
  1. Start with a neutral spine, gaze down.
  2. Exhale and round your back up, head relaxing.
  3. Inhale and let your chest and belly sink gently, gaze slightly forward.
  4. Stop short of either end range; keep the neck long.
  5. Flow smoothly, about 3 s each way.
- **Dosage:** 6-8 slow cycles, no holds (motion, not a stretch).
- **Breathing:** Exhale to round, inhale to arch; one breath per phase.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: easy spinal motion. Stop if: pain spreads into the buttock or leg, tingling below the knee, or a sharp catch.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — end-range rounding can irritate disc-type sciatica; end-range arching can irritate stenosis-type symptoms. Regression: half range in the direction that eases symptoms, or standing with hands on a chair seat.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Keep it a mobility drill: 10 slower, segment-by-segment cycles. With osteoporosis, keep the rounding partial.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Deep lumbar sag (~20-30° extra arch), head thrown back ~30° → joint compression, neck strain → Arch half-way, eyes on the floor ahead.
  - Cat forced to full flexion, hips rocking back 10-15 cm → end-range disc load → Hips stay over knees.
  - Locked elbows, shrugged shoulders → wrist and neck strain → Soft elbows, push the floor away.
- **Cues:** "Exhale, round up." · "Inhale, chest softens." · "Stop before the end." · "Move with your breath."
- **YouTube:** `cat camel exercise physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic — Cat-cow stretch](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/cat-cow-stretch); [NHS inform — Exercises for back pain](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/neck-and-back-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-back-pain/); [McGill 1998, Phys Ther — Low back exercises](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9672547/)

### `thread-the-needle` — Thread the needle (quadruped thoracic rotation)
- **Region / targets:** Upper/mid back — thoracic rotation; rear shoulder.
- **Setup:** Mat, all fours: hands under shoulders, hips and knees at 90°, knees padded.
- **Steps:**
  1. Reach your right arm up to the ceiling; eyes follow your hand.
  2. Bring it down and slide the back of the hand under your body to the left.
  3. Let your right shoulder and head lower toward the mat.
  4. Keep your hips square over your knees.
  5. Reverse; finish your reps, then switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 6-8 slow reps per side; or a 20-30 s threaded hold.
- **Breathing:** Inhale to open, long exhale to thread; ~5 breaths per 30 s.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: rotation between the shoulder blades. Stop if: low back pinching, leg symptoms, supporting-shoulder pain, arm pins and needles.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — rotation stays in the mid back. Regression: hips sat back toward the heels, or seated rotation with arms crossed.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s threaded holds, top arm reaching overhead; add 2-3 s active end holds.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Hips shift 5-10 cm sideways or rotate → low-back twist replaces thoracic rotation → Hips square; sit back slightly.
  - Low back sags ~15-20° → lumbar extension load → Light brace, spine long.
  - Supporting elbow locked, shoulder sinking toward the ear → joint strain → Push the floor away.
- **Cues:** "Hips stay still." · "Eyes follow your hand." · "Thread under, exhale." · "Push the floor away."
- **YouTube:** `thread the needle stretch physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [South Tees Hospitals NHS — Threading the needle](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/threading-the-needle/); [Heneghan et al. 2020, BMJ Open SEM — thoracic exercise prescription](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7173996/)

### `open-book` — Open book (side-lying thoracic rotation)
- **Region / targets:** Mid back and chest — thoracic rotation; pectoral stretch.
- **Setup:** Mat, side-lying: head on a pillow, hips and knees ~90°, cushion between stacked knees, arms straight in front.
- **Steps:**
  1. Squeeze the cushion lightly between your knees.
  2. Sweep the top arm up and over, like opening a book.
  3. Let your chest and head turn with it; eyes follow your hand.
  4. Stop before the top knee lifts; rest the arm on a pillow if needed.
  5. Close the book slowly; switch sides after your reps.
- **Dosage:** 6-8 slow reps per side, one-breath pause; or a 20-30 s open hold.
- **Breathing:** Inhale to open, long exhale as the shoulder settles; ~5 breaths per 30 s.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: chest and front-of-shoulder stretch, mid-back rotation. Stop if: shoulder pinching, hand pins and needles, back or leg symptoms.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — stacked, bent knees limit lumbar rotation. Regression: arm to the ceiling only; pillow behind the back.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s open holds with the hand supported on a block; then slow overhead half-circles.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Top knee slides back 5-15 cm, pelvis rolls ~20-30° → low-back twist → Squeeze the cushion; knees stacked.
  - Arm forced to the floor, shoulder 20°+ behind the chest line → front-of-shoulder strain → Stop where the chest stops.
  - Neck cranks ~30° while the chest stays → neck strain → Head and chest turn together.
- **Cues:** "Knees glued together." · "Open the book." · "Chest turns, hips stay." · "Exhale and settle."
- **YouTube:** `open book stretch physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [HSS — Stretches before bed (lying "T" twist)](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/stretches-before-bed); [Heneghan et al. 2020 — sidelying thoracic rotation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7173996/)

### `foam-roller-thoracic-extension` — Foam roller thoracic extension
- **Region / targets:** Upper/mid back — thoracic extension; chest wall.
- **Setup:** Mat; medium-density roller across the lower tips of the shoulder blades, never the low back. Knees bent, buttocks down, hands behind the head, elbows in.
- **Steps:**
  1. Cradle your head in your hands, chin gently tucked.
  2. Exhale and drape your upper back over the roller.
  3. Stop when your ribs start to lift — about 10-20°.
  4. Inhale and curl halfway back.
  5. Shift the roller 2-3 cm higher; repeat at 2-3 spots.
- **Dosage:** 5-6 slow extensions per spot, 2-3 spots (~1 min).
- **Breathing:** Long exhale as you drape back, inhale to return.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: opening in the mid back and chest. Stop if: sharp spine or rib pain, arm pins and needles, dizziness, low back pinching.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — hips stay down, so the lumbar spine is unloaded. Regression: seated mid-back extension, hands behind the neck, elbows lifting. Osteoporosis or past vertebral fracture: use the seated version unless cleared (a precaution; the UK osteoporosis consensus supports extension exercise but does not address rollers).
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 30-60 s draped holds, elbows bent overhead, slow breathing.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Roller under the low back, hips lifted → lumbar hyperextension → Roller at shoulder-blade level, buttocks down.
  - Head hangs back unsupported (~30-40° neck extension) → neck strain, dizziness → Hands carry the head.
  - Ribs flare, low back arches ~15-20° → lumbar substitution → Exhale, ribs down.
- **Cues:** "Hips heavy." · "Hands hold your head." · "Exhale, drape back." · "Ribs down."
- **YouTube:** `foam roller thoracic extension physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [HSS — Foam roller exercises](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/foam-roller-exercises); [Heneghan et al. 2020 — thoracic extension on foam roller](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7173996/); [Brooke-Wavell et al. 2022, BJSM — UK osteoporosis exercise consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35577538/)

## 6. Shoulders / chest / arms

### `doorway-pec-stretch` — Doorway pec stretch
- **Region / targets:** Chest — pectoralis major and minor, front deltoid
- **Setup:** Doorway, staggered stance; forearms on the frame, elbows bent 90° at or just below shoulder height.
- **Steps:**
  1. Step one foot slightly forward in the doorway.
  2. Rest your forearms on the frame, with your elbows no higher than your shoulders.
  3. Draw your ribs down and lengthen your neck.
  4. Lean gently forward until your chest stretches. Hold, then step back.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s.
- **Breathing:** Long, slow exhale as you ease deeper; ~5 breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: an even stretch across the chest. Stop if: a pinch at the front of the shoulder, or tingling into the arm or hand.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — the spine stays neutral; keep the back knee soft if the leg is irritable. Regression: one arm at a time, with a smaller lean.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s holds; vary elbow height (~60°, 90°, ~120°); one-arm version with the chest turned slightly away (lengthened pec minor most in a lab comparison); contract-relax — press the forearms into the frame (20-30% effort, 5 s, keep breathing), relax, ease in.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Hips drift through the door and the low back arches ~15-20° → lumbar load, chest stretch lost → ribs down, lean only 5-10 cm.
  - Elbows raised to ~110-120° with a hard lean → strain at the front of the shoulder, arm tingling → elbows at or below shoulder height.
  - Chin pokes ~3-5 cm forward and shoulders shrug ~2 cm → neck strain → ears over shoulders.
- **Cues:** "Elbows at shoulder height." · "Ribs down." · "Lean from the ankles." · "Exhale, let the chest open."
- **YouTube:** `doorway pec stretch physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic — Posture exercises (doorway stretch)](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/posture-exercises); [J Shoulder Elbow Surg — Three pectoralis minor stretches compared](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16679233/)

### `cross-body-shoulder-stretch` — Cross-body shoulder stretch
- **Region / targets:** Shoulder — posterior deltoid, posterior rotator cuff and capsule
- **Setup:** Stand, or sit tall on a chair.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit or stand tall with your shoulders relaxed.
  2. Lift one arm to just below shoulder height.
  3. Hold it above the elbow with your other hand.
  4. Draw it gently across your chest, keeping your chest facing forward. Hold, then switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you ease the arm 1-2 cm further; ~5 breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: a stretch at the back of the shoulder. Stop if: a pinch at the top or front of the shoulder, or tingling in the hand.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — no load on the spine; sit if standing aggravates. Regression: hold the arm lower (~70°). Diabetes raises the risk of frozen shoulder: if the shoulder is stiff and painful in every direction, get it assessed rather than forcing it.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 5 × 30 s daily on the tighter side (this improved internal rotation in a randomised trial); contract-relax — push the arm into your hand (20-30%, 5 s, keep breathing), relax, ease in.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Pulling on the forearm through a straight elbow → elbow strain → grip above the elbow.
  - Shoulder hikes ~2-3 cm and the arm rises above ~100° → pinch at the front of the shoulder → arm at or below shoulder height.
  - Trunk turns ~20-30° toward the pulled arm → stretch lost → keep the chest square.

### `kneeling-lat-stretch` — Kneeling lat stretch (bench)
- **Region / targets:** Upper back — latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps
- **Setup:** Padded mat, with a bench, chair seat or ball in front; knees hip-width apart, under the hips.
- **Steps:**
  1. Kneel tall, facing the bench.
  2. Hinge forward and place your hands on the bench, arms straight.
  3. Brace gently so your back stays long.
  4. Sit your hips back slowly until you feel the stretch in your armpits.
  5. Stop before your low back rounds. Hold, then come up slowly.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you sit back; ~5 slow breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: a stretch from the armpits down the sides of the back. Stop if: low back pain, leg symptoms, a shoulder pinch, or dizziness on rising.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — the sit-back can end in lumbar flexion; stop as soon as the pelvis starts to tuck. Regression: standing 90° lat stretch — hands on a counter, hip hinge, flat back, soft knees.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s holds; turn the palms up; walk the hands to one side; contract-relax — press the hands into the bench (20-30%, 5 s, keep breathing), relax, sit back 1-2 cm further.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Pelvis tucks and the low back rounds ~20-30° → disc/nerve irritation → stop earlier, keep a neutral curve.
  - Chest sags and the low back arches ~15-20° → pinch in the low back and shoulder → brace, ribs down.
  - Head drops below the arms (~30° neck flexion) → neck strain → keep the ears between the arms.
- **Cues:** "Hips back, back long." · "Stop before the low back rounds." · "Exhale, let the armpits sink."

### `overhead-triceps-stretch` — Overhead triceps stretch
- **Region / targets:** Upper arm — triceps (long head), latissimus dorsi, teres major
- **Setup:** Stand, or sit tall on a chair; towel optional.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit or stand tall, and roll your shoulders down and back.
  2. Reach one arm up, keeping the shoulder away from your ear.
  3. Bend the elbow and drop the hand toward your upper back.
  4. With your other hand, press lightly just above the elbow.
  5. Keep your ribs down and head tall. Hold, then switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you add light pressure; ~5 breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: a stretch along the back of the upper arm. Stop if: a pinch on top of the shoulder, elbow pain, or tingling into the ring and little fingers.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — sit if standing aggravates; keeping the ribs down stops the back arching. Regression: towel version — the top hand holds a towel and the lower hand eases it down behind your back.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s holds; add a slight side-bend away to include the lat; contract-relax — press the elbow into your hand (20-30%, 5 s, keep breathing), relax, ease in.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Ribs flare and the low back arches ~10-15° → lumbar compression → brace, ribs down.
  - Arm pushes the head forward (~20° neck flexion) → neck strain → head tall, arm beside the ear.
  - A hard pull drives the elbow behind the head (shoulder past ~180°) → shoulder pinch → use light pressure or the towel.

### `wrist-flexor-extensor-stretch` — Wrist flexor and extensor stretch
- **Region / targets:** Forearm — wrist/finger flexors (palm side), wrist extensors (back of the forearm)
- **Setup:** Stand or sit; arm straight in front, at or below shoulder height.
- **Steps:**
  1. Reach one arm forward, elbow straight, palm up.
  2. With the other hand, ease the palm and fingers back toward the floor. Hold.
  3. Turn the palm down and relax the hand.
  4. Gently bend the wrist so the fingers point down. Hold, then switch arms.
- **Dosage:** 1-2 × 20-30 s each direction, each arm.
- **Breathing:** Keep your shoulders soft and exhale as you ease the hand back; ~5 breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: a gentle pull along the forearm. Stop if: tingling or numbness in the fingers (bend the elbow slightly and ease off), or sharp wrist or elbow pain.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — no load on the spine. Regression: elbow slightly bent, forearm resting on a table. Diabetes often stiffens the hands (limited joint mobility); daily gentle stretching is advised — go slowly if sensation is reduced.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s holds; table-top version — palms flat, fingers toward you, lean back gently, then repeat with the backs of the hands down; contract-relax at 20-30% for 5 s, keep breathing.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Elbow bent ~30-45° → the stretch is lost → straighten the elbow unless the fingers tingle.
  - Wrist yanked past ~80° or bounced → tendon strain → slow, mild tension.
  - Pulling only on the fingertips (fingers bent back >60°) → finger-joint strain → press on the palm.
- **Cues:** "Elbow straight, shoulder soft." · "Gentle pressure on the palm." · "Exhale, let the forearm lengthen."

### `scapular-wall-slide` — Scapular wall slide
- **Region / targets:** Shoulder blades — serratus anterior, lower and middle trapezius
- **Setup:** Back to a wall, feet ~15-30 cm out, knees soft; head, upper back and pelvis touching the wall.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand against the wall with your ribs down.
  2. Raise your arms into a goalpost, elbows bent 90°.
  3. Set your shoulder blades gently back and down.
  4. Slide up only as far as your low back and shoulders stay quiet.
  5. Slide down, drawing your elbows toward your sides.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 8-10 slow reps (3 s up, 3 s down).
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you slide up; inhale on the way down.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: work around the shoulder blades. Stop if: a shoulder pinch (cap the range below it), tingling in the hands, or low back pain.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — the wall supports the spine. Regression: face the wall with your forearms on it, thumbs pointing away, and slide up (this facing-wall version is the one studied; prefer it for stiff or painful shoulders).
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Progress control, not force: a 3 s pause at the top; lift the forearms 1-2 cm off the wall; facing-wall slide with a light loop band around the wrists.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Low back arches off the wall ~15-20° and the ribs flare → lumbar compression → ribs down, stop the slide there.
  - Shoulders shrug ~2-3 cm at the top → upper trap takes over, shoulder pinch → slide only while the shoulders stay down.
  - Head pokes ~3-5 cm off the wall → neck strain → keep the back of the head on the wall.

### `band-pull-apart` — Band pull-apart
- **Region / targets:** Upper back — middle/lower trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid, infraspinatus

## 7. Torso

### `standing-side-bend` — Standing side bend
- **Region / targets:** Torso — quadratus lumborum, obliques, lats
- **Setup:** Standing, feet hip-width, knees soft; wall or chair within reach.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand tall, weight even on both feet.
  2. Slide one hand down the side of your thigh.
  3. Bend straight sideways, as if between two panes of glass.
  4. Keep your hips level and chest facing forward.
  5. Pause, return tall, switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 6 reps per side with a 3 s pause, then one 20 s hold per side.
- **Breathing:** Exhale to bend, inhale to rise; ~4 breaths per 20 s hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: mild stretch along the opposite waist. Stop if: pain or tingling travels into the buttock or leg.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — side bending can pinch or stretch an irritated nerve root; small range, comfortable side only; skip if your trunk is visibly shifted sideways (lateral shift). Regression: seated side bend, ~10 cm range.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Top arm overhead, 2-3 × 30-45 s per side, sliding 1-2 cm further on each exhale.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Trunk drifts forward ~15-20° → flexion plus side bend, the combination irritated discs tolerate least → shoulders stacked over hips.
  - Hips slide 5-10 cm away from the bend → stretch lost, one segment overloaded → pelvis level and still.
  - Top shoulder rolls forward, trunk rotating ~15° → adds twist → chest square to the front.
- **Cues:** "Slide, don't lean." · "Hips still." · "Chest forward." · "Breathe out, lengthen."

### `supine-twist` — Supine twist (knee rolls)
- **Region / targets:** Torso — lumbar rotators, obliques, thoracolumbar fascia
- **Setup:** Mat; small pillow under the head.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, knees and feet together.
  2. Rest your arms out wide, shoulders heavy.
  3. Slowly lower both knees to one side, only as far as is easy.
  4. Keep both shoulder blades on the floor.
  5. Return to the middle; repeat to the other side.
- **Dosage:** 8-10 rolls each way, 2-3 s pause at the end.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as the knees lower, inhale as they return.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: light stretch across the opposite back and hip. Stop if: symptoms spread down the leg, or a sharp catch.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — small knee rolls are usually well tolerated. The deep crossed-knee twist adds flexion and tensions the top leg's sciatic nerve, so skip it while irritable. Regression: 10-15 cm rolls, pillow between the knees.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** When not irritable: crossed-knee twist, 2-3 × 30-60 s per side (~5 breaths per 30 s).
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Opposite shoulder lifts 5+ cm → forced end-range lumbar rotation → shoulders heavy, smaller range.
  - Knees flop to the floor fast (pelvis rotating ~70-90°) → end-range torsion flares joint or nerve pain → lower slowly, stop near ~45°.
  - Top knee slides 10+ cm behind the bottom knee → uneven pelvic twist, sacroiliac strain → knees together.
- **Cues:** "Knees together, slow and smooth." · "Shoulders heavy." · "Only as far as is easy." · "Breathe out as they lower."

### `childs-pose` — Child's pose (bottom-to-heels stretch)
- **Region / targets:** Lower back — lumbar extensors, thoracolumbar fascia, lats
- **Setup:** Mat, towel under the knees, cushion for the forehead; chair nearby.
- **Steps:**
  1. Kneel on all fours, knees wide, big toes touching.
  2. Sit your hips back toward your heels, only as far as is easy.
  3. Let your chest settle, arms reaching forward.
  4. Rest your forehead on the cushion.
  5. Walk your hands back to rise slowly.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s.
- **Breathing:** Slow nasal inhale into the back ribs, long exhale to settle; ~5 breaths per 30 s.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: broad, gentle low- and mid-back stretch. Stop if: symptoms spread down the leg, knee pain, or light-headedness. - **Deep-stretch progression:** When not irritable: knees wider, 2-3 × 60-90 s; or walk the hands to one side, 30-45 s.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Head hanging below the hips, chin tucked (neck flexed ~45°) → neck strain, dizziness on rising → forehead supported near heart level.
  - Hips forced onto heels, knees together (knees bent ~150°) → knee compression, end-range disc load → knees wider, cushion behind the knees.
  - Shoulders shrugged ~3-4 cm, elbows locked → neck tension → elbows soft.
- **Cues:** "Hips back, only as far as is easy." · "Forehead resting, neck long." · "Come up slowly."

## 8. Lower back

### `pelvic-tilt` — Pelvic tilt (lying)
- **Region / targets:** Lower back — lumbopelvic control; abdominals, lumbar extensors
- **Setup:** Mat or firm bed; small pillow under the head.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat, hands on your hip bones.
  2. Gently roll your pelvis back, flattening your lower back.
  3. Pause, then roll the other way into a small arch.
  4. Keep chest, shoulders and feet relaxed.
  5. Finish halfway — your neutral spine.
- **Dosage:** 10-15 slow tilts, 2 s at each end.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you flatten, inhale as you arch; never hold.

- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — small range and no load; stay symptom-free. Regression: smaller range with a pillow under the knees, or seated on a stool.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Not a stretch. Progress to seated, then standing (back to a wall) tilts, 2 × 15, to build neutral-spine control.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Pelvis lifts into a bridge (sacrum >3 cm off the floor) → becomes a hip lift, control drill lost → tailbone stays down; tilt, don't lift.
  - Big arch on the forward tilt (lumbar extension ~20°, ribs flaring) → can irritate extension-sensitive backs → small arch, ribs soft.
  - Breath held, stomach braced rigid → blood-pressure rise, stiff movement → breathe out as you flatten, stomach soft.

### `knee-to-chest` — Knee-to-chest stretch (single, then double)
- **Region / targets:** Lower back/hips — lumbar extensors, thoracolumbar fascia, gluteus maximus
- **Setup:** Mat or firm bed; thin pillow under the head.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
  2. Bring one knee toward your chest.
  3. Hold behind the thigh, not the shin.
  4. Draw in gently to a mild stretch; the other foot stays flat.
  5. Release slowly; switch legs.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s per leg.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you draw in; ~5 slow breaths per 30 s; no straining.

  - Head and shoulders curl up (neck flexed ~30-40°) → neck strain, extra spinal flexion → head stays down.
  - Tailbone lifts high (pelvis rolling back >20°, worst with both knees) → end-range disc load, may send symptoms down the leg → stop as the tailbone lifts.

### `prone-press-up` — Prone press-up (sphinx regression)
- **Region / targets:** Lower back — lumbar extension range
- **Setup:** Mat or firm bed; pillow under the hips if lying flat hurts.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie face down for a minute; notice where your leg symptoms are.
  2. Prop onto your forearms, elbows under shoulders — the sphinx.
  3. If symptoms stay put or move toward your back, place your hands under your shoulders.
  4. Press your chest up, hips on the floor, buttocks soft.
  5. Breathe out, let your back sag 1-2 s, lower slowly; roll to your side to get up.
- **Dosage:** Prone lying 1 min → sphinx 2 × 30 s → 10 press-ups; stay at the last comfortable level.
- **Breathing:** Exhale as you press and sag, inhale as you lower; ~5 breaths per 30 s sphinx.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: low-back pressure is fine. Leg symptoms retreating toward the back (centralising) is good, even if back pain briefly rises. Stop if: symptoms spread further down the leg (peripheralise) or new tingling appears.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — a symptom-guided tool, not a cure: progress only while leg symptoms centralise or stay the same. Extension-sensitive backs (stenosis, spondylolisthesis, facet pain) often don't tolerate it. Regression: prone lying on a hip pillow → sphinx → half press-up.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** When it reliably eases symptoms: 5 × 30 s top holds, sagging on each exhale; standing back extensions as sitting breaks.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Hips lift 3-5 cm → extension moves to the hips → hips stay down.
  - Buttocks and back clenched → spine compressed, motion blocked → buttocks soft.
  - Shoulders shrugged ~3-4 cm → neck strain → shoulders down, elbows soft.
  - Head thrown back (neck extended ~30-40°) → neck compression, dizziness → eyes on the floor ahead.
- **Cues:** "Hips heavy, back soft." · "Press, breathe out, sag." · "Leg easing — good; spreading — stop."

### `supine-figure-4` — Supine figure-4 stretch
- **Region / targets:** Hips — piriformis, deep hip external rotators, gluteus maximus and medius
- **Setup:** Mat. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. A strap is optional.

  3. Hold behind the left thigh, or loop a strap around it.
  4. Draw the left thigh in until the right buttock stretches. Keep your tailbone and head down.
  5. Lower the foot and switch sides.

  - Crossed ankle sickled ~20°, shin resting on the kneecap → knee twist → flex the foot; rest the ankle just past the knee.

### `knee-to-opposite-shoulder` — Knee-to-opposite-shoulder stretch
- **Region / targets:** Hips — piriformis, gluteus maximus and medius, posterior hip capsule
- **Setup:** Mat. Lie on your back with the other knee bent and foot flat.
- **Steps:**
  1. Bring your right knee toward your chest.
  2. Put your left hand on the outside of the knee and your right hand just above the ankle.
  3. Guide the knee toward your left shoulder, with the shin angled across your body.
  4. Stop at a gentle buttock stretch, hips heavy on the mat.
  5. Release slowly and switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** About 5 slow breaths per hold. Breathe out as the knee drifts across.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: a deep buttock stretch. Stop if: tingling spreads down the leg, sharp buttock pain, or a pinch at the front of the hip. Skip after a hip replacement unless cleared.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Avoid when irritable** — the position is close to the FAIR test (flexion, adduction, internal rotation), which is used to provoke sciatic compression at the piriformis. Regression: supine-figure-4 with the foot on the floor, or cross only to the midline.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Hold 45-90 s. Work toward ~115-120° hip flexion, 25-30° adduction and 40-50° external rotation; a CT study found this lengthened the piriformis most.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Foot flicks out, so the hip rotates inward ~20-30° → FAIR-like nerve compression → hold above the ankle; aim the shin toward the opposite shoulder.
  - Same-side buttock lifts ~5-8 cm and the pelvis rotates ~20° → lumbar twist → keep both hip bones level.
  - Hands crank the kneecap, taking the hip past ~120° → pinch at the front of the hip → hold the thigh; ease off before the pinch.
- **Cues:** "Knee to the opposite shoulder." · "Shin angles across." · "Hips heavy." · "Ease off at the first tingle."
- **YouTube:** `knee to opposite shoulder piriformis stretch physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic — Piriformis syndrome stretches and exercises](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/piriformis-syndrome-stretches-exercises); [Fishman et al. 2002 — FAIR test in piriformis syndrome, Arch Phys Med Rehabil](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11887107/); [Gulledge et al. 2014 — Optimizing piriformis stretch positions, Med Eng Phys](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24262799/)

### `seated-piriformis-stretch` — Seated piriformis stretch
- **Region / targets:** Hips — piriformis, deep external rotators, gluteus maximus
- **Setup:** Sturdy chair. Sit near the front edge with feet flat.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit tall. Lift your right ankle onto your left knee and flex the foot.
  2. Rest one hand on the knee and one on the ankle.
  3. Lengthen your spine, then hinge forward from the hips, chest leading.
  4. Stop at a buttock stretch and let the knee settle by gravity.
  5. Return upright and switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** Breathe in to grow tall, breathe out as you hinge. About 5 breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: a deep buttock stretch. Stop if: tingling or numbness into the leg, groin pinch, or knee pain. Skip after a hip replacement unless cleared.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — a rounded, slumped lean mimics the slump test, which puts tension on the nerve roots. Hinge with a long spine instead. Regression: stay upright and let the knee lower, or use supine-figure-4.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Hold 45-90 s. Add contract-relax: lift the knee into your hand at ~30% effort for 5 s while breathing, then hinge slightly deeper.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Spine rounds ~30-40° and the chin drops ~20° → slump-like nerve tension → chest forward, chin level.
  - Hand forces the knee 10-15 cm down → knee and hip strain → let gravity lower it.
  - Shin resting on the kneecap, foot sickled ~20° → knee and ankle strain → ankle just past the knee, foot flexed.
- **Cues:** "Sit tall first." · "Hinge from the hips." · "Chin level." · "Let the knee drop."
- **YouTube:** `seated piriformis stretch physiotherapy NHS`
- **Sources:** [South Tees Hospitals NHS — Gluteal stretch in sitting](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/gluteal-stretch-in-sitting/); [Cleveland Clinic — Piriformis syndrome stretches and exercises](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/piriformis-syndrome-stretches-exercises); [Majlesi et al. 2008 — Slump and straight-leg-raise tests in disc herniation, J Clin Rheumatol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18391677/)

### `half-kneeling-hip-flexor-stretch` — Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch
- **Region / targets:** Hips — iliopsoas, rectus femoris and front hip capsule of the kneeling leg
- **Setup:** Mat, cushion under the knee, chair for balance.
- **Steps:**
  1. Kneel on your right knee with the left foot forward and both knees at ~90°.
  2. Tuck your tailbone and squeeze your right buttock.
  3. Keep the tuck and shift your hips forward 2-5 cm.
  4. Stay tall with ribs down, and breathe.
  5. Switch sides, then use the chair to stand up slowly.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** Breathe out as you tuck and shift. About 5 breaths per hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: a stretch at the front of the hip. Stop if: pinching in the low back, tingling down the front of the thigh, or kneecap pain.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — fine with the pelvis tucked. Arching loads the lumbar spine in extension, which irritates extension-sensitive backs. Regression: standing split-stance at a counter, back heel up, pelvis tucked.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Hold 45-90 s. Reach the same-side arm up and lean away. Later, rest the back foot on a sofa (couch stretch), but only if the tuck holds.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Pelvis tips forward ~15-20°, low back arches, ribs flare → lumbar extension load, stretch lost → tuck, squeeze the glute, ribs down.
  - Over-lunge: hips ~15 cm forward, front knee ~10 cm past the toes → knee load and back arch → small shift, only after the tuck.
  - Pelvis twisted ~15-20°, back shin angled out → uneven lumbar twist → hips square, shin pointing straight back.
- **Cues:** "Tuck your tail, squeeze the back glute." · "Shift forward just a little." · "Hips square."
- **YouTube:** `half kneeling hip flexor stretch physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [South Tees Hospitals NHS — Hip stretch in half kneeling](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/hip-stretch-in-half-kneeling/); [HSS — Four hip flexor stretches, from a PT](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/hip-flexor-stretch); [Cleveland Clinic — Psoas stretches and exercises](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/psoas-stretch-guide-for-psoas-release)

### `ninety-ninety-hip-switch` — 90/90 hip switch
- **Region / targets:** Hips — hip rotators (piriformis, deep rotators, gluteus medius and minimus), hip capsule
- **Setup:** Mat. Put a cushion or block under the hips if you are stiff.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit with knees bent ~90° and feet flat, wider than your hips.
  2. Sit tall, leaning back slightly onto your hands.
  3. Lower both knees to the right: the front leg rests on its outer side, the back leg on its inner side.
  4. Lift the knees back through the middle and lower them to the left. Keep switching slowly.
- **Dosage:** 6-8 slow switches each way (2-3 s per side), 1-2 sets.
- **Breathing:** Breathe out as the knees lower, breathe in through the middle.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: rotation through both hips. Stop if: groin pinch, inner-knee pain, or buttock or leg tingling. Skip after a hip or knee replacement, or with hip impingement or a meniscus tear, unless cleared.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — internal rotation of the back leg can provoke deep buttock pain, and slumping rounds the lumbar spine. Stay propped on your hands and keep the range small. Regression: block under the hips, half range, or lying windshield-wipers.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Hold each side 30-60 s. Hinge over the front shin with a long spine. Then progress to hands-free switches.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Low back rounds ~20-30° → disc load, and the hips stop rotating → hands behind, block under the hips.
  - Hands force the knees down and the back knee caves ~15° → medial knee strain → let the knees fall naturally.
  - Sit bone of the back leg lifts ~5-10 cm and the trunk leans away ~20° → lumbar side-bend → both sit bones heavy, smaller range.
- **Cues:** "Sit tall." · "Knees sweep like wipers." · "Sit bones heavy."
- **YouTube:** `90 90 hip switch mobility physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic — The 90/90 hip stretch](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/90-90-stretch); [HSS — Four hip flexor stretches, from a PT (90/90 stretch)](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/hip-flexor-stretch)

### `adductor-rock-back` — Adductor rock-back
- **Region / targets:** Inner thigh — adductors (longus, brevis, magnus), gracilis
- **Setup:** Mat with padding under the knees. Start on hands and knees.
- **Steps:**
  1. Place hands under shoulders and knees under hips.
  2. Straighten your right leg out to the side, whole foot flat, toes pointing forward.
  3. Set a neutral spine.
  4. Rock your hips back toward your left heel until the inner thigh stretches.
  5. Stop before your low back rounds. Return, repeat, then switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 8-10 slow rocks per side (3 s back, 2 s forward).
- **Breathing:** Breathe out as you rock back, breathe in as you return.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: an inner-thigh stretch on the straight leg. Stop if: sharp groin pain, hip pinch, inner-knee pain, or back or leg symptoms.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — once hip flexion runs out, the low back rounds, which commonly triggers symptoms. The straight knee also adds nerve tension. Regression: bend the side knee to 90°, or lie on your back and let bent knees fall open.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Hold the end position 30-60 s. Add contract-relax: press the foot down at ~30% effort for 5 s while breathing, then rock deeper. Later, progress to a frog rock-back.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Low back rounds ~20-30° and the pelvis tucks → disc and nerve load → stop before the tuck, belly long.
  - Leg rolled in so the kneecap faces the floor (hip turned in ~30-45°) → medial knee strain → foot flat, kneecap forward.
  - Hips drift ~10 cm sideways or twist ~15° → lumbar twist → rock straight back.
- **Cues:** "Long spine." · "Rock back slowly." · "Stop before the tuck." · "Kneecap forward."
- **YouTube:** `adductor rock back exercise physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic — Hip-opening stretches (frog stretch)](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/hip-opening-stretches); [NHS inform — Exercises for osteoarthritis of the hip (lying inner-thigh stretch)](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-osteoarthritis-of-the-hip/); [Van Dillen et al. 2009 — Modifying symptomatic movement tests incl. quadruped rock-back, Man Ther](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2659590/)

## 10. Hamstrings / nerve

Muscle stretch vs nerve tension (applies to every item in this section): a muscle stretch is a dull pull in the muscle belly that eases as you hold it. Nerve tension is burning, tingling, electric or spreading (below the knee or into the foot) and changes when you move your head or ankle. Nerve signs mean ease off at once.

### `sciatic-nerve-glide-seated` — Seated sciatic nerve glide (slider)
- **Region / targets:** Back of leg / nerve — sciatic-tibial nerve tract (moved, not stretched).
- **Setup:** Firm chair, sitting upright, feet flat, hands on the seat.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit tall on both sit bones.
  2. Straighten one knee, point the toes and look up.
  3. Stop before any pull or tingling.
  4. Bend the knee as your chin drops.
  5. Flow slowly, 2-3 seconds each way.
- **Dosage:** 10 slow reps × 1-2 sets, symptomatic leg first; 5 on sensitive days.
- **Breathing:** Easy breathing, exhale as the knee straightens; never hold.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: faint movement behind the thigh, gone at once. Stop if: tingling, burning or numbness spreads down the leg or lingers for minutes.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — built for sciatica, but small and symptom-free only; sit upright, not slumped, when irritable. Regression: supine glide or seated ankle pumps.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Not a stretch: widen the range, then pull the toes up as the knee straightens; after weeks of calm, a gentle tensioner (chin down, knee straight) × 5, never forced.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Trunk slumps ~20-30°, chin drops as the knee straightens → slump tensioner, nerve flare → sit tall, eyes up.
  - Knee snapped to lock (0°) in under 1 s → jolts the nerve → stop 10-20° short, move slowly.
  - Toes yanked up ~15-20°, held 5-10 s → nerve stretch, not glide → toes pointed, keep moving.
- **Cues:** "Knee up, eyes up." · "Knee down, chin down." · "Smooth, no stretch."
- **YouTube:** `seated sciatic nerve slider physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [South Tees NHS — Sciatic nerve slider](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/sciatic-nerve-slider/); [Ellis et al. 2017, J Man Manip Ther — spinal position and sciatic nerve excursion in seated mobilisation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5430455/); [J Man Manip Ther 2023 — Neural mobilisation in low back and radicular pain (systematic review)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9848316/)

### `sciatic-nerve-glide-supine` — Supine sciatic nerve glide (slider)
- **Region / targets:** Back of leg / nerve — sciatic and tibial nerve; the gentlest glide, spine supported.
- **Setup:** On your back on a mat, head on a thin pillow, other knee bent, foot flat.
- **Steps:**
  1. Hold behind one thigh, hip bent 60-90°.
  2. Straighten the knee as you point your toes away.
  3. Stop before any pull or tingling.
  4. Bend the knee as your toes come back towards you.
  5. Keep the thigh still and the movement flowing.
- **Dosage:** 10 slow reps × 1-2 sets per leg, 2-3 s each way.
- **Breathing:** Soft nasal breathing; exhale as the knee straightens.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: easy movement, at most a faint pull behind the knee. Stop if: symptoms spread below the knee, intensify, or linger after the set.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — in an ultrasound study, sliding moved the sciatic nerve about 5× more than tensioning (17 vs 3 mm), and sliders keep tension low. Regression: hip ~45°, half the knee range, or ankle pumps only.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Over weeks: hip towards 90°, fuller knee range; when calm, toes up at the top or a gentle tensioner × 5, never forced.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Thigh pulled to ~110°, knee locked, toes up → straight-leg-raise tensioner → hip ≤90°, toes pointed.
  - Head and shoulders curl up ~30° → extra neural tension, neck strain → use a strap, head down.
  - Pelvis rolls up, low back rounds ~10-15° → lumbar flexion load → other foot planted, hips heavy.
- **Cues:** "Knee straight, toes away." · "Knee bends, toes back." · "Glide, don't stretch."
- **YouTube:** `supine sciatic nerve glide slider physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Coppieters et al. 2015, JOSPT — sciatic nerve excursion during mobilisation exercises](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26304637/); [Coppieters & Butler 2008, Man Ther — Do sliders slide and tensioners tension?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17398140/); [South Tees NHS — Ten sciatica facts](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/10-sciatica-facts/)

### `supine-hamstring-stretch-strap` — Supine hamstring stretch with strap
- **Region / targets:** Back of thigh — hamstrings; also tensions the sciatic nerve.
- **Setup:** On your back, head on a pillow, strap at the arch near the heel; other leg long or bent.
- **Steps:**
  1. Hold both strap ends, knee bent.
  2. Straighten to a soft knee, toes relaxed.
  3. Raise the leg to a mild pull mid-thigh, hips heavy, head down.
  4. Hold, breathe, lower with the knee bent.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s per leg.
- **Breathing:** About 5 slow breaths per hold; drift up on each exhale.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: dull pull mid-hamstring, 3-4/10. Stop if: burning or tingling behind the knee or into the calf or foot, or symptoms that change with head or ankle movement (nerve).
- **Back & sciatica:** **Avoid when irritable** — on the symptomatic leg this is the straight-leg-raise nerve test. Regression: hip at 90°, knee straightened only to the first pull, or the supine glide.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Symptomatic leg only when calm: 45-60 s × 2-3; contract-relax: heel into the strap at 20-30% for 5 s, breathing, then ease higher.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Strap at the toes, ankle pulled ~15-20° into dorsiflexion → extra nerve tension → strap at the arch.
  - Low back rounds ~10-15°, other thigh lifts → load moves to the lumbar spine → other leg long, hips down.
  - Head curls up ~30° → neural tension, neck strain → head down, arms long.
- **Cues:** "Toes soft." · "Hips heavy, head down." · "Mild pull, mid-thigh."
- **YouTube:** `supine hamstring stretch with strap physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [South Tees NHS — Hamstrings stretch (1)](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/hamstring-stretch-1/); [NHS inform — Exercises for thigh problems](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-thigh-problems/); [Boyd et al. 2009, JOSPT — ankle dorsiflexion sensitises the straight-leg raise](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19881004/)

## 11. Quads / calves

### `side-lying-quad-stretch` — Side-lying quad stretch
- **Region / targets:** Front of thigh — quadriceps, especially rectus femoris; front of hip.
- **Setup:** On your side, head on a pillow, bottom knee bent forward; strap round the top ankle if needed.
- **Steps:**
  1. Bend the top knee and hold the ankle or strap.
  2. Tuck your tailbone, belly gently in.
  3. Keep the knees level, thigh in line with your body.
  4. Ease the knee back to a stretch down the front of the thigh.
  5. Hold, breathe, release, switch sides.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** About 5 slow breaths per hold; exhale as you ease the knee back.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: stretch down the front of the thigh. Stop if: back pinching, knee pain, or front-thigh tingling or burning (femoral nerve).
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — no sciatic nerve load; keep the pelvis tucked if extension irritates. Regression: strap, less knee bend, head and trunk neutral.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s; contract-relax: foot into hand at 20-30% effort for 5 s, breathing, then ease further back.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Low back arches ~15-20° as the heel is pulled → lumbar compression → tuck the tailbone, ribs down.
  - Top thigh drifts forward ~20-30° (hip flexion) → stretch lost → knee in line with or behind the hip.
  - Top knee lifts ~20° away from the bottom knee → twisted, uneven pull → knees stacked.
- **Cues:** "Tail tucked." · "Knees level." · "Knee back, not up."
- **YouTube:** `side lying quad stretch physiotherapy`
- **Sources:** [ACE — Side-lying quadriceps stretch](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/149/side-lying-quadriceps-stretch/); [South Tees NHS — Quads stretch](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/quads-stretch/); [Trainor & Pinnington 2011, Physiotherapy — slump knee bend (femoral nerve) test](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21295239/)

### `wall-calf-stretch` — Wall calf stretch (gastrocnemius)
- **Region / targets:** Upper calf — gastrocnemius, Achilles tendon.
- **Setup:** Facing a wall, hands at shoulder height, one foot a long step back; shoes on with neuropathy or foot deformity.
- **Steps:**
  1. Point both feet straight ahead.
  2. Keep the back knee straight and the heel down.
  3. Bend the front knee and lean your body to the wall.
  4. Stop at a gentle stretch in the upper calf.
  5. Hold, breathe, step the feet together.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** About 5 slow breaths per hold; lean a fraction more on each exhale.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: pull in the calf belly, 3-4/10. Stop if: burning, tingling or numbness into the foot, or symptoms that change with head position (nerve). Neuropathy: stay mild, check feet after; open wound: seated towel stretch instead.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — straight knee plus ankle bend tensions the tibial/sciatic tract, so sciatic calf pain can flare. Regression: shorter step, smaller lean, or the soleus stretch.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s × 2-3; lengthen the step 2-3 cm at a time (diabetic Achilles stiffness: expect slow gains).
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Back heel lifts 1-3 cm → stretch lost → shorter step, heel down.
  - Back foot turns out ~20-30°, arch collapses → foot strain → toes straight ahead.
  - Hips sag, low back arches ~15° → back strain → one line, lean from the ankles.
- **Cues:** "Toes forward." · "Back heel heavy." · "Lean as one piece."
- **YouTube:** `wall calf stretch gastrocnemius physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [NHS — Flexibility exercises (calf stretch)](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/); [South Tees NHS — Upper calf stretch](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/upper-calf-stretch/); [ADA 2016 position statement — Physical activity/exercise and diabetes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)

### `soleus-stretch` — Bent-knee soleus stretch
- **Region / targets:** Lower calf — soleus, Achilles tendon.
- **Setup:** Facing a wall, hands on it, one foot a short step behind the other; shoes on if you have neuropathy.
- **Steps:**
  1. Point both feet straight ahead.
  2. Keep the back heel firmly down.
  3. Bend both knees and sink your hips straight down.
  4. Stop at a gentle stretch low in the calf.
  5. Hold, breathe, then rise slowly.
- **Dosage:** 2 × 20-30 s per side.
- **Breathing:** About 5 slow breaths per hold; sink a little on each exhale.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: deep pull low in the calf or Achilles. Stop if: sharp Achilles or heel pain, foot tingling, or knee pain. With neuropathy, stay mild and check your feet afterwards.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — the bent knee slackens the sciatic-tibial nerve, making this the calf stretch of choice with sciatic calf pain. Regression: seated, sliding the foot back under the chair, heel down.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** 45-60 s holds; then forefoot on a 2-3 cm book, both knees bent.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Back heel lifts 1-2 cm as the knees bend → stretch lost → shorter step, heel down.
  - Back knee barely bends (<15°) → stays a gastrocnemius stretch → bend both knees, hips straight down.
  - Back knee falls inward ~10-15°, arch flattens → knee and foot strain → knee over the second toe.
- **Cues:** "Heel down." · "Sit straight down." · "Knee over toes."
- **YouTube:** `soleus stretch bent knee wall physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [South Tees NHS — Lower calf stretch](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/lower-calf-stretch/); [South Tees NHS — Advanced lower calf stretch](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/advanced-lower-calf-stretch/); [ACE — Standing dorsiflexion calf stretch (bent-knee variation)](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/152/standing-dorsi-flexion-calf-stretch/)

## 12. Breathing / activation

### `diaphragmatic-breathing-90-90` — 90/90 diaphragmatic breathing
- **Region / targets:** Breathing / trunk — diaphragm, deep abdominals, pelvic floor.
- **Setup:** On your back, feet flat on a wall (or calves on a chair), hips and knees ~90°; one hand on the chest, one on the belly.
- **Steps:**
  1. Let your low back rest heavy.
  2. Inhale through the nose for 3-4 s, belly and lower ribs widening.
  3. Keep the chest hand still.
  4. Exhale through pursed lips for 5-8 s as the ribs sink.
  5. Let the next breath arrive by itself.
- **Dosage:** 4-5 breaths × 3 sets (about 2-3 min).
- **Breathing:** ~5-6 breaths per minute, exhale longer than inhale; no holds.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: belly and side ribs rise, neck quiet. Stop if: dizziness, tingling lips or fingers (over-breathing), or back or leg pain. Roll to your side and sit up slowly.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — supported, flexed hips usually ease sensitive backs. Regression: feet on the floor, pillow under the knees.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Exhale to 8-10 s; then heels pull lightly down the wall and the tailbone lifts slightly on each exhale (90/90 hip lift), still no holds.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Ribs flare, low back arches 1-2 cm off the mat → back extension stress → ribs drop, back heavy.
  - Shoulders rise 1-2 cm, chest-only breathing → neck tension → breathe low.
  - Forced exhale, puffed cheeks or a held pause → blood-pressure spike → soft, unforced breath.
- **Cues:** "Breathe low and wide." · "Long, easy exhale." · "Ribs soften down."
- **YouTube:** `90 90 breathing feet on wall physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Boyle et al. 2010, NAJSPT — The value of blowing up a balloon (90/90)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2971640/) (its 2-3 s post-exhale pause is dropped here to keep the no-hold rule); [Cleveland Clinic — Diaphragmatic breathing](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing); [BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 2021 — diaphragm training in chronic low back pain (RCT)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7934526/)

### `crocodile-breathing` — Crocodile breathing (prone)
- **Region / targets:** Breathing / trunk — diaphragm with back and side rib expansion; relaxes the low back.
- **Setup:** Face down on a mat, forehead on stacked hands, legs relaxed; pillow under the hips if lying flat bothers your back.
- **Steps:**
  1. Let your forehead and body sink into the mat.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for 3-4 seconds.
  3. Feel the belly press down and the waist and low back rise.
  4. Breathe out slowly for 5-7 s, everything softening.
  5. Keep shoulders and jaw loose.
- **Dosage:** 8-10 slow breaths × 2 sets (about 2-3 min).
- **Breathing:** ~5-6 breaths per minute, nose in, nose or soft mouth out; no holds.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: back and sides widen. Stop if: back or leg symptoms increase or spread, dizziness, or neck discomfort. Come up via all fours, slowly.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — no load; if lying flat (slight extension) aggravates back or leg, add a hip pillow. Regression: same breathing side-lying or in 90/90.
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Extend to 3-5 min; loop a band or your hands around the lower ribs and breathe into them.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Head lifted, neck extended ~20-30° to look forward → neck strain → forehead on hands, neck long.
  - Low back arches ~10° and buttocks clench on the inhale → extension stress → hips heavy.
  - Shoulders shrug ~2 cm, breath stays in the chest → no back expansion → breathe into the belt line.
- **Cues:** "Forehead heavy." · "Breathe into your back." · "Waist widens."
- **YouTube:** `crocodile breathing physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 2021 — crocodile breathing within diaphragm training for chronic LBP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7934526/); [PLoS One 2026 RCT — diaphragmatic breathing progression including crocodile breathing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12768353/)

### `box-breathing` — Box breathing (no-hold default)
- **Region / targets:** Breathing / nervous system — paced slow breathing to lower arousal.
- **Setup:** Sit tall on a chair, feet flat, or lie on your back with knees bent; hands on the belly.
- **Steps:**
  1. Breathe out slowly to start.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4.
  3. Default: breathe out softly through pursed lips for a count of 6.
  4. Classic box only without hypertension or diabetic eye disease: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4, throat open.
  5. Repeat, breath quiet and low.
- **Dosage:** 1-2 min (about 6-12 breaths); classic box 3-4 rounds.
- **Breathing:** No-hold default ≈ 6 breaths/min; never cue holds with hypertension or severe/unstable diabetic retinopathy.
- **Feel / stop:** Feel: calmer, slower heart, heavier body. Stop if: dizziness, air hunger, tingling lips or fingers, chest discomfort, or headache.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — no spinal load; any comfortable position. Regression: shorter counts (3 in, 4 out).
- **Deep-stretch progression:** Lengthen to 5 in / 7-8 out, 5 min daily. Cyclic sighing (two sniffs in, one long sigh out) gave the largest mood benefit in a trial that also tested box breathing.
- **Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix):**
  - Holding with a closed throat and braced belly (bearing down, face reddening) → Valsalva blood-pressure spike → no-hold version, throat soft.
  - Big fast inhales, shoulders rising 2-3 cm → over-breathing, light-headedness → gentle 70-75% breaths.
  - Counting too fast (>10 breaths/min) → little calming effect → slow the count.
- **Cues:** "In for four." · "Out for six." · "Throat soft, no holding."
- **YouTube:** `box breathing technique physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [Balban et al. 2023, Cell Rep Med — box breathing vs cyclic sighing vs mindfulness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/); [Chaddha et al. 2019, Complement Ther Med — slow breathing lowers blood pressure (meta-analysis)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31331557/); [ADA 2016 — avoid breath-holding with severe or unstable retinopathy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)

## 13. Cardio

### Shared intensity scale and pre-cardio checks

| Level | Talk test | RPE 0-10 | Borg 6-20 | % HRmax | % HRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy (warm-up, cool-down, post-meal) | chat freely | 3-4 | 10-11 | <64% | <40% |
| Moderate (main block) | talk, but not sing | 5-6 | 12-14 | 64-76% | 40-59% |
| Vigorous (interval "fast", capped) | only a few words | 7-8 | 15-17 | 77-95% | 60-89% |

Sources for the scale: [CDC — talk test and 0-10 scale](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html); [Cleveland Clinic — Borg 12-14 = moderate](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/17450-rated-perceived-exertion-rpe-scale); [ACSM 2011 — %HRmax/%HRR bands](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556/).

- **Heart rate.** Estimate HRmax ≈ 208 − 0.7 × age ([Tanaka et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11153730/)). Beta-blockers, and some other heart-rate-slowing drugs, blunt heart rate, so cue by talk test and RPE and show heart rate as information only ([Eston & Connolly 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8776008/)).
- **Blood pressure.** If resting BP is above 160/100, check with a clinician before exercising ([ACSM Exercise is Medicine — hypertension](https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/EIM_Rx-for-Health_Hypertension.pdf)).
  - No breath-holding or white-knuckle gripping.
  - Always finish with the easy 3-minute cool-down.
  - Sit for 30-60 s before standing up from a bike or rower.
- **Glucose (insulin or sulfonylurea users).** Check before the session and carry 15-20 g of fast carbohydrate. These ADA thresholds are written for type 1 diabetes and are applied here cautiously ([ADA 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/)):
  - below 90 mg/dL (5.0 mmol/L): take 15-30 g carbohydrate first
  - above 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L): test ketones and do no intense work
  - moderate or large ketones: no exercise
  - Beta-blockers can mask the warning signs of a low.
- **Feet and eyes.**
  - With neuropathy: wear cushioned, well-fitting shoes and blend socks, and check the feet after every session.
  - With an open foot wound: use non-weight-bearing modes (bike).
  - With severe or unstable diabetic retinopathy: no vigorous intervals, no jarring, no breath-holding.

### `incline-treadmill-walk` — Incline treadmill walk
- **Setup:** Clip on the safety key. Speed 2.5-3.5 mph (4.0-5.6 km/h). Start at 0-3% incline and add about 1% a week toward 5-8%. Treat 10-12% as advanced: popular "12% / 3 mph" plans are hard, so build up from flat or 5%. Wear cushioned, supportive walking shoes.
- **Posture:** Tall spine with a slight whole-body lean from the ankles (~5-10°), eyes forward, arms swinging, hands off the rails. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): gripping the rails with hips flexed ~20-30° and elbows locked → takes load off the legs, strains shoulders and low back, and makes the effort look higher than it is → let go, and lower the incline or speed until you can walk hands-free; bending at the waist (trunk ~20-30° forward of vertical) with long reaching steps → loads the lumbar spine in flexion and stretches the hamstring and sciatic nerve more → lean from the ankles, chest up, shorter quicker steps.
- **Breathing:** Steady rhythm (in for 2-3 steps, out for 2-3). Never hold your breath on the climb.
- **Intensity:** Moderate — talk, not sing; RPE 5-6/10 (Borg 12-14) ≈ 64-76% HRmax or 40-59% HRR. Incline raises effort at the same speed, so re-check after each change. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — Incline and a slight forward lean often ease extension-sensitive (stenosis-type) symptoms; people with stenosis usually walk longer on an incline than on the flat. But uphill adds hip flexion plus ankle dorsiflexion, which tensions the sciatic/tibial nerve and loads calves and hamstrings. If buttock, hamstring or calf nerve symptoms rise, drop to 0-3% and shorten your stride. Stay below 8% while symptoms are irritable.
- **Diabetes:** Moderate incline walking usually lowers glucose. It is weight-bearing: with neuropathy, wear well-fitting cushioned shoes and check your feet afterwards. Use a bike instead if there is an open foot wound. (Insulin/sulfonylurea: run the pre-cardio glucose check.)
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up flat (0-1%), easy pace, RPE 3-4 · 3:00-9:00 main: incline 3-6% (progress toward 8%), pace for RPE 5-6 · 9:00-12:00 cool-down: incline back to 0%, slow to a stroll, step off slowly.
- **Cues:** "Hands off the rails—let your arms swing." · "Lean from your ankles, not your waist." · "Short, quick steps up the hill." · "Talk, but don't sing." · "Tingling down the leg? Lower the incline."
- **YouTube:** `incline treadmill walking form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic — The 12-3-30 workout](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/12-3-30-workout); [Fritz et al. 1997 — Level vs inclined treadmill test in lumbar spinal stenosis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9355058/); [Coppieters et al. 2006 — Sciatic/tibial nerve strain with hip flexion and ankle dorsiflexion](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16838375/)

### `brisk-walk` — Brisk walk
- **Setup:** Flat route, or treadmill at 0-1%. Brisk means 2.5 mph (4 km/h) or faster; most adults settle at 3-4 mph (4.8-6.4 km/h). Aim for 100-120 steps/min (≥100 steps/min ≈ moderate intensity), which the app can count. Wear cushioned, well-fitting shoes and blend (not cotton) socks.
- **Posture:** Tall, eyes on the horizon, shoulders down, elbows ~90° swinging from the shoulder, feet landing under the hips and rolling heel to toe. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): head dropped to watch the feet or phone (neck flexed ~30-45°) → neck and upper-back strain, trips → eyes 10-20 m ahead, phone away; overstriding, with the heel landing well ahead and the knee nearly straight (~0-5°) → braking jolts through knee and back, plus more hamstring/nerve stretch → shorter, quicker steps.
- **Breathing:** Relaxed and rhythmic. In through the nose if comfortable, out through the mouth. No breath-holding on hills.
- **Intensity:** Moderate — talk, not sing; RPE 5-6/10 (Borg 12-14) ≈ 64-76% HRmax (e.g., age 60 → HRmax ~166, target ~106-126 bpm) or 40-59% HRR. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — Walking is usually well tolerated. In one trial, a progressive walking programme nearly doubled the time before the next low-back-pain episode (median 208 vs 112 days). If leg symptoms build, slow down and shorten your steps. Pause for 1-2 min if needed (stenosis-type symptoms often ease when you lean forward or sit), then carry on over flat ground.
- **Diabetes:** Usually lowers glucose. With neuropathy, moderate walking is not thought to raise ulcer risk, but avoid weight-bearing on an unhealed ulcer and check your feet after every walk. (Insulin/sulfonylurea: run the pre-cardio glucose check.)
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up stroll building to ~90-100 steps/min, RPE 3-4 · 3:00-9:00 main brisk at 100-120 steps/min, RPE 5-6 · 9:00-12:00 cool-down easing to a stroll, RPE 2-3.
- **Cues:** "Tall spine, eyes on the horizon." · "Quick, light steps—feet land under you." · "Swing your elbows; keep your shoulders low." · "Talk, but don't sing." · "Leg tingling? Shorten your steps."
- **YouTube:** `brisk walking technique physiotherapist`
- **Sources:** [CDC — Measuring physical activity intensity](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html); [Tudor-Locke et al. 2018 — Walking cadence (≥100 steps/min) as an intensity marker](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29858465/); [Pocovi et al. 2024 — WalkBack trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38908392/)

### `upright-bike` — Upright stationary bike
- **Setup:** Set the saddle so the knee is bent 25-35° with the pedal at the bottom. Quick check: with your heel on the pedal at the bottom, the leg is almost straight; then ride on the ball of the foot. Keep the saddle level, with the knee roughly over the pedal axle at 3 o'clock. Handlebars level with or above the saddle, higher for sore backs. Light resistance, 60-80 rpm.
- **Posture:** Hinge from the hips with a long, neutral spine (trunk ~20-40° forward of vertical), soft elbows, light hands, knees tracking over the feet. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): saddle too high, so the knee locks (<15° bend) and the pelvis rocks ~5-10° side to side → hamstring/sciatic tension and low-back/SI-joint irritation → lower the saddle until the hips stay still; slumped C-curve reaching for low bars (lumbar flexion near end-range, pelvis tucked) → sustained flexion load and back-muscle fatigue → raise the bars and sit taller.
- **Breathing:** Rhythmic. Breathe out through harder pushes, and never hold your breath when adding resistance.
- **Intensity:** Moderate — talk, not sing; RPE 5-6/10 (Borg 12-14) ≈ 64-76% HRmax or 40-59% HRR. Add resistance before cadence. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — Cycling holds the lumbar spine flexed, and lower handlebars mean more lumbosacral flexion. Many stenosis-type backs like the forward lean, but flexion-sensitive disc or sciatica symptoms may flare. Raise the bars, sit more upright, and keep the saddle low enough that the knee never locks (a straight knee with a flexed hip tensions the sciatic nerve). Switch to the recumbent bike or walking if leg symptoms spread.
- **Diabetes:** Non-weight-bearing, so a good option when walking bothers the feet or there is a foot wound. With neuropathy, wear well-fitting shoes (never ride barefoot), use the straps, and check your feet and the skin where you sit afterwards. (Insulin/sulfonylurea: run the pre-cardio glucose check.)
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up easy spin, light resistance, RPE 3-4 · 3:00-9:00 main: add resistance, 70-80 rpm, RPE 5-6 · 9:00-12:00 cool-down: easy spin with resistance off, then dismount slowly.
- **Cues:** "Soft bend in the knee at the bottom." · "Hinge from the hips, long spine." · "Light hands, low shoulders." · "Smooth circles—hips stay quiet." · "Breathe out as you push."
- **YouTube:** `exercise bike seat height setup physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Peveler 2011 — 25-35° knee angle for saddle height](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20581695/); [Streisfeld et al. 2017 — Cyclists' spinal kinematics and low back pain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27784817/); [Cleveland Clinic — How to deal with cycling pain](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-to-deal-with-cycling-pain)

### `recumbent-bike` — Recumbent bike
- **Setup:** Slide the seat so the knee keeps a 25-35° bend at the farthest pedal; it should never lock. Set the backrest upright to slightly reclined so it fully supports the low back, and sit with the hips all the way back. Straps over the forefoot, light resistance, 60-80 rpm, hands resting lightly on the side handles.
- **Posture:** Back and pelvis against the backrest, ribs stacked over the pelvis, shoulders and jaw relaxed, knees in line with the feet. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): seat too far, so the knee snaps straight (~0-10°) with the hip flexed ~80-90° and the ankle pulled up → this is a straight-leg-raise position that tensions the sciatic nerve, and the hips slide forward → move the seat closer to keep a soft knee; seat too close, so the hip flexes >110° with the thighs near the belly and the pelvis tucked under (~15-20° posterior tilt) → lumbar rounding and kneecap pressure → move the seat back and keep the pelvis against the rest.
- **Breathing:** Slow belly breathing comes easily in this position. Breathe out through the effort, with no breath-holding when you add resistance.
- **Intensity:** Moderate — talk, not sing; RPE 5-6/10 (Borg 12-14) ≈ 64-76% HRmax or 40-59% HRR. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — Supported back, stable seat and no impact make this a good choice when walking or the upright bike flares symptoms, or when balance is limited. Seat distance is the main thing to get right. If leg tingling appears, bring the seat closer and lower the resistance.
- **Diabetes:** Non-weight-bearing, so preferred with an unhealed foot ulcer or altered gait. Straps can rub, so check your feet afterwards. With autonomic neuropathy or BP medicines, sit for 30-60 s before standing to avoid dizziness. (Insulin/sulfonylurea: run the pre-cardio glucose check.)
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up easy pedalling, RPE 3-4 · 3:00-9:00 main: resistance up, 70-80 rpm, RPE 5-6 · 9:00-12:00 cool-down: easy with resistance off, then pause before standing.
- **Cues:** "Back against the seat, hips all the way back." · "Soft knee at the far pedal." · "Push through the whole foot." · "Breathe low and easy." · "Pause before you stand."
- **YouTube:** `recumbent bike seat adjustment physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [ADA 2016 — Physical activity/exercise and diabetes position statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/); [Coppieters et al. 2006 — Nerve strain during straight-leg raising](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16838375/); [Peveler 2011 — 25-35° knee angle](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20581695/) (no recumbent-specific technique source found; the knee angle is carried over from upright saddle research)

### `elliptical` — Elliptical cross-trainer
- **Setup:** Start with low resistance and a low ramp (0-5 on most machines), moving forward. Feet centred and flat in the pedals, hands light on the moving or fixed handles. Size check: if you push off your toes or heels a lot, the stride is too long; if you find yourself hunching, the machine is too small.
- **Posture:** Upright trunk (lean ≤10°), hips under shoulders, eyes forward, knees tracking over the second toe, light grip. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): hanging on the handles or console with the trunk flexed ~20-30° and elbows locked → takes work off the legs, rounds the low back, and tight gripping raises BP → stand tall with light hands; up on the toes, heels lifted with ankles plantarflexed ~20-30° → forefoot pressure, calf overload, numb toes → press through the heels and whole foot, and lower the resistance or ramp.
- **Breathing:** Rhythmic and matched to your strides. Breathe out fully, with no breath-holding as resistance rises.
- **Intensity:** Moderate — talk, not sing; RPE 5-6/10 (Borg 12-14) ≈ 64-76% HRmax or 40-59% HRR. Raise resistance or ramp before speed. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — Low impact, but ellipticals produce more trunk, pelvic, hip and knee flexion than walking, so stay upright. If leg symptoms rise, shorten the stride (lower the ramp), reduce resistance, or switch to walking or the recumbent bike.
- **Diabetes:** Peak foot pressures are lower than walking, but because the feet never leave the pedals, total pressure over time is similar. So the elliptical is not automatically safer for numb feet: start with short sessions, wear cushioned shoes and check your feet afterwards. Foot tingling on ellipticals is common; reposition your feet or pedal backward briefly, and stop if it persists. Not ideal if balance is poor. (Insulin/sulfonylurea: run the pre-cardio glucose check.)
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up easy strides, low resistance, RPE 3-4 · 3:00-9:00 main: resistance or ramp up 1-2 levels, RPE 5-6 · 9:00-12:00 cool-down: resistance down, slow strides, hold the fixed handles to step off.
- **Cues:** "Stand tall, hips under shoulders." · "Light hands—let your legs do the work." · "Heels down, whole foot on the pedal." · "Smooth and quiet." · "Breathe out fully."
- **YouTube:** `elliptical proper form physical therapist`
- **Sources:** [Burnfield et al. 2010 — Elliptical vs walking kinematics and muscle demands](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20022994/); [Burnfield et al. 2020 — Plantar pressures on ellipticals in older adults](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32791378/); [Cleveland Clinic — Treadmill or elliptical?](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/treadmill-or-elliptical-how-to-decide-whats-your-best-workout)

### `rowing-machine` — Rowing machine (ergometer)
- **Setup:** Damper 3-5. Higher settings feel heavier but tire the muscles before you get the cardio benefit. Foot straps across the widest part of the foot, 18-24 strokes/min, relaxed grip with flat wrists.
- **Posture:** At the catch: shins vertical (never past vertical), arms straight, trunk leaning ~20-30° forward from the hips with a long spine. On the drive: legs, then swing the trunk through vertical, then arms. At the finish: lean back ~10-15°, handle to the lower ribs, shoulders low. On the recovery: arms, then body, then knees. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): rounded low back at the catch, pelvis tucked (posterior tilt ~10-20°) and shoulders reaching past the shins → lumbar flexion under load, which gets worse with fatigue and more so on the erg than in a boat → sit tall on your sit-bones, hinge from the hips, shorten the slide; big lay-back at the finish (>30° behind vertical, ribs flared, low back arched) → lumbar extension load and shoulder strain → push with the legs first and finish only slightly behind vertical.
- **Breathing:** Breathe out through the drive and in on the recovery (two breaths per stroke at higher rates). Never hold your breath or white-knuckle the handle.
- **Intensity:** Moderate — talk, not sing; RPE 5-6/10 (Borg 12-14) ≈ 64-76% HRmax or 40-59% HRR. Intensity comes from how hard you push, not from the damper. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Avoid when irritable** — Every stroke repeats loaded lumbar flexion at the catch. The finish is a straight-leg sit (hip flexed, knees straight), which tensions the sciatic nerve if the trunk slumps. Rowers with back pain show more posterior pelvic rotation at the catch. When symptoms are quiet, use short bouts, a low damper and limited forward reach, and stop at any leg symptoms.
- **Diabetes:** Non-weight-bearing for the feet, but straps can rub, so check your feet afterwards and protect your hands from blisters. Hard pieces can raise glucose briefly. (Insulin/sulfonylurea: run the pre-cardio glucose check.)
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up: legs-only, then legs-and-body strokes, rate 18-20, RPE 3-4 · 3:00-9:00 main: full strokes, rate 20-24, RPE 5-6 · 9:00-12:00 cool-down: easy strokes, rate 18, light pressure.
- **Cues:** "Legs, body, arms—then arms, body, legs." · "Tall spine, hinge from the hips." · "Breathe out as you push." · "Relaxed grip, low shoulders." · "Shins stop at vertical."
- **YouTube:** `concept2 rowing technique beginners`
- **Sources:** [Concept2 — Indoor rowing technique](https://www.concept2.com/training/rowing-technique); [Concept2 — Damper setting 101](https://www.concept2.com/training/articles/damper-setting); [Nugent et al. 2021 — Rowing-related low back pain and biomechanics (systematic review)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33397675/)

### `interval-protocols` — Interval protocols (walk, bike, elliptical)
- **Setup:** Use a mode you already tolerate: walking, upright or recumbent bike, or elliptical. Not rowing while back or leg symptoms are irritable. Start only after about 2 weeks of comfortable 10-20 min continuous moderate sessions. Skip intervals today if resting BP is >160/100, glucose is out of range, or symptoms are flaring. Formats: beginner 30 s faster / 90 s easy; standard 1 min faster / 1 min easy; interval walking 3 min fast / 3 min slow (in longer sessions).
- **Posture:** Keep the base mode's form at the higher speed. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): form collapsing in the fast segment, with the head jutting forward and trunk flexing ~20-30°, or grabbing the rails or handles → neck and back strain, and BP spikes from gripping → slow to the speed at which your posture holds; overstriding to go faster (heel far ahead, knee nearly straight) → jolts and hamstring/nerve tension → take quicker steps, not longer ones.
- **Breathing:** In fast segments, breathe out fully and keep a rhythm. Let breathing settle in the easy segments. Never hold your breath.
- **Intensity:** Fast = RPE 6-7/10 (Borg 14-16), a few words at a time, ≈ 70-80% HRmax. Easy = RPE 3-4. With hypertension, keep fast segments at RPE 7 or below and never sprint all-out. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **Modify** — Intervals magnify whatever the base mode does to the back. Start only when continuous moderate work causes no flare. Choose the bike, recumbent or elliptical if faster walking provokes leg symptoms, and skip intervals on flare days.
- **Diabetes:** In type 2 diabetes, 3-min fast/slow interval walking improved fitness and glucose control more than continuous walking with the same energy cost. Short, hard efforts can briefly raise glucose; moderate work lowers it. On insulin or a sulfonylurea, check before starting: below ~90 mg/dL (5.0 mmol/L), take 15-30 g carbs first; above 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L), test ketones and skip hard efforts. Beta-blockers can mask the warning signs of a low. Avoid vigorous intervals with severe or unstable diabetic retinopathy.
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up easy building to moderate, RPE 3-5 · 3:00-9:00 main: 3 × (1:00 fast at RPE 6-7 / 1:00 easy at RPE 3-4) · 9:00-12:00 cool-down easy, RPE 2-3.
- **Cues:** "Pick it up—strong, not all-out." · "Quicker steps, same tall posture." · "Ease off now; let your breathing settle." · "Breathe out fully—never hold your breath." · "Three easy minutes to finish."
- **YouTube:** `interval walking training 3 minutes fast 3 minutes slow`
- **Sources:** [Karstoft et al. 2013 — Interval-walking training in type 2 diabetes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23002086/); [ADA 2016 position statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908414/); [ACSM Exercise is Medicine — Being active when you have hypertension (PDF)](https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/EIM_Rx-for-Health_Hypertension.pdf)

### `post-meal-walk` — Post-meal walk
- **Setup:** Flat route, hallway, or treadmill at 0-1%, at your normal comfortable pace. Start any time from about 10 to 60 min after finishing a main meal; glucose usually peaks 30-90 min after eating. Walk 10-15 min after each main meal, especially the largest or evening meal. Even 2-5 min helps.
- **Posture:** Relaxed, upright walk, arms loose, eyes forward. Mistakes (Mistake → Risk → Fix): a slumped shuffle after a big meal, with the upper back rounded and head forward ~20-30° → squeezes the stomach (reflux) and strains the neck and back → stand tall, loosen the waistband, slow down; phone-gazing with the neck flexed ~45° → trips and neck strain → keep your eyes on the path ahead.
- **Breathing:** Easy, conversational breathing, in through the nose if comfortable. If reflux or a stitch appears, slow down and lengthen the out-breath.
- **Intensity:** Light to moderate: you can chat easily. RPE 3-5/10 (Borg 10-13). No need to push; timing and duration matter more than speed. Beta-blockers: go by talk test/RPE.
- **Back & sciatica:** **OK** — Gentle walking breaks up sitting after meals. If leg symptoms rise, shorten the walk or your stride, pause, then continue.
- **Diabetes:** Ten minutes after each main meal lowered post-meal glucose ~12% compared with one 30-min walk a day, and ~22% after the evening meal. On mealtime insulin or a sulfonylurea, a post-meal walk can push glucose lower: check it, carry 15-20 g fast carbs, and ask your care team about dose timing. A short easy walk rarely causes lows; hard workouts can. With neuropathy, wear shoes (not slippers) and check your feet afterwards.
- **12-min session:** 0:00-3:00 warm-up easy stroll · 3:00-9:00 main: comfortable pace, RPE 4-5 (talking easily) · 9:00-12:00 cool-down: easy stroll home.
- **Cues:** "Easy pace—you could chat the whole way." · "Tall and relaxed after your meal." · "Ten minutes after each main meal is plenty." · "Shoes on, and check your feet afterwards."
- **YouTube:** `walking after meals blood sugar diabetes educator`
- **Sources:** [Reynolds et al. 2016 — Walking after meals in type 2 diabetes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27747394/); [Kang et al. 2023 — Post-meal exercise meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10610082/); [Cleveland Clinic — Walking after eating](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/walking-after-eating)
