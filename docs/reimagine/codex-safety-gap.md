# Readiness engine reconciliation with the clinical safety contract

Audited on 8 October 2026 against [Clinical tracking protocols, Safety contract](../research/clinical-tracking-protocols.md#safety-contract). The existing engine is **not ready to be the sole permission gate for Stretch, Walk and Guided Session**. The outcome merger correctly preserves the most restrictive contribution it receives, but some emergency contributions never reach it, some required observations cannot be captured, and several hold conditions permit modified or recovery exercise.

This audit creates no implementation or test files. It evaluates the current working tree at Git HEAD `f21db7a`; the readiness source SHA-256 is `81e8a3e5a72decb61ec7e38b19535b37bc92b0b39d4c5300922693f01aebe0a0`. The contract SHA-256 is `bf2c0133b77f19e25cb675d0bfdc0f022b6472ef30132dd6e485c185c3a2119e`. These identify the evaluated versions rather than claiming that HEAD alone includes every working tree file.

Final citations include the concurrent additions of food preferences to the profile and a custom Start button label to the sheet. Neither changes clinical capture or rule evaluation. The final profile SHA-256 is `2032c7abc2aa967fb9e558db8cd3ac0350befecc9f64801b82da43032757809e`; the sheet SHA-256 is `93e63e5b746b9c7e953c3830ef459ce53f45e5538fa5cd95121e5bc2fe6f8d5c`.

I read the requested files and the only matching `readiness*.test.ts`, plus their direct dependencies and consumers. I executed the actual readiness, health, profile default and form helper functions by transpiling the existing TypeScript in memory. Engine outcomes, reasons and recheck flags below were observed in those probes. Component capture and rendering paths were inspected statically; the BP averaging arithmetic was reproduced separately. The existing Vitest assertions were reviewed, not run as a suite. No browser interaction or production certification is claimed.

## Reading the verdicts

Each contract row receives one overall verdict. **DIVERGES** includes a rule that works for some captured conditions but misses another condition, action or release requirement. **MISSING** means the particular clinical condition has neither an adequate input nor its decision rule; a generic nearby question is not treated as adequate capture. **MATCH** is scoped to that row's responsibility, with higher priority escalation assessed in its own rows. **MORE CONSERVATIVE** is used for stricter isolated boundary behaviour and additional restrictions.

The contract's actions are distinct from existing colours:

| Contract action | Required permission | Current representation |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | No session, emergency help immediately | `urgent` generally provides this |
| Today | No session, explicit clinical help today | Some `red` reasons provide this; `red` alone means “Rest today” |
| Hold | No relevant session until the stated recovery/check/plan condition | Sometimes `red`; sometimes a recheck timer; some corresponding conditions incorrectly use runnable `amber` or `recovery` |
| Adjust | Only the permitted mode, movements, load or intensity | Modifiers, nerve/back conditions and exercise filtering can implement this |
| Reassure | Result in the recorded target, with no unresolved safety condition | `green` alone cannot prove this because it can carry restrictions or a recheck timer |

`CheckInSheet` permits `green`, `amber` and `recovery` **only if there is no `recheckMinutes`**, at `src/components/checkin/CheckInSheet.tsx:131`. `buildSessionPlan` returns no session for `urgent` or `red` at `src/engine/session.ts:117`, but builds recovery activity at line 124. Consequently, “recovery” is exercise permission, not a hold. A new mode that checks only whether the outcome is red/urgent would also miss existing timer holds.

Unless specified otherwise, concrete engine inputs below use a known type 2 profile, active with vigorous clearance, no insulin/secretagogue/SGLT2, no other restrictions; a check-in has `urgentSymptoms=false`, `news=[]`, `sleep='gt7'` and `energy=4`. “Insulin profile” adds `insulin='injections_or_pump'`; “SGLT2 profile” adds `sglt2i=true`. These are test fixtures, not assumptions about the owner.

## Outcome ordering and masking

`OUTCOME_ORDER`, `src/types/checkin.ts:8`, is `green < amber < recovery < red < urgent`. `merge`, `src/engine/readiness.ts:388`, takes the maximum, and line 398 sorts reasons by that order. **There is no observed case in which an emitted urgent contribution is downgraded by a later amber, recovery or red contribution.** The merger itself should be retained.

That ordering does **not** implement the complete `emergency > today > hold > adjust > reassure` contract:

* Today and hold do not have distinct disposition/release state. Red with “rest” is not necessarily help today.
* Recovery remains runnable; it cannot represent the contract's untested-ketone or neurological hold.
* A green outcome with `recheckMinutes` is an effective hold in the sheet, outside the colour ordering. That check must be shared by every mode.
* Green with `FOOT`, severe-eye restrictions or nerve conditions is a restricted permission, not unrestricted reassurance.

There are concrete cases where higher priority information is lost **before** the merger or its presentation:

| Case | What happens | Contract result |
|---|---|---|
| SGLT2, glucose 60, blood ketones 3.0 | `glucoseRules:243` returns only the low contribution, skipping `ketoneRules`; result amber/recheck 15 | Emergency for ketones, alongside appropriate hypo rescue |
| SGLT2, glucose 50, blood ketones 3.0 | `glucoseRules:236` returns only severe-low red | Emergency, not rest today |
| Checked chest/stroke/cauda emergency plus suspect-unit or implausible glucose | `form.ts:26` blocks submission; `CheckInSheet.tsx:249` disables the button and emergency guidance is only rendered after a result | Show emergency guidance immediately and block exercise without requiring glucose correction first |
| Two severe raw BP readings whose components average below the red gates | Sheet destroys the raw observations before `bpRules` runs | Evaluate each observation and repeat sequence before any diagnostic average |
| A saved cauda flag after profile back areas are removed | Form submission at `CheckInSheet.tsx:116` drops the hidden back answers | Keep the emergency evidence until explicitly addressed |
| Saved runnable plan followed by a new urgent check-in | `SessionPage.tsx:52` selects the saved plan; line 79 checks that plan rather than current readiness, and line 93 exempts a resume from the current recheck guard | Reevaluate current acute flags before resuming; preservation of training progress cannot override an emergency |

There is also conflicting secondary presentation without colour downgrade: insulin/SGLT2, glucose 80 and blood ketones 3.0 produces urgent **plus** `recheckMinutes=15` and carbohydrate actions. `CheckInSheet.tsx:257` shows the timer even for urgent results, and `OutcomeBanner.tsx:46` renders all actions under “Before you start”. The session is blocked, but emergency help must not appear contingent on waiting for that timer. Oral rescue instructions also require the absent swallowing/alertness state.

Recommended reconciliation is to retain combinable treatment components, add explicit reviewed/unknown knowledge and insulin regimen, and record the applicable clinician monitoring/exercise plan. Do not replace combinations with a single exclusive medicine bucket. Add metformin exposure/duration for the B12 cadence and prior crisis/insulin deficiency where clinically relevant. Unknown should prevent risk-dependent personalised clearance; **confirmed** diet/metformin-only treatment must not acquire an invented daily fingerstick requirement.

`deriveHealth` can infer SGLT2 risk even without a diabetes diagnosis, but `glucoseRules`, `readiness.ts:199`, exits when diabetes is `none`. Thus it also discards entered high ketones in that profile. Risk and existing observations should not disappear because the diagnosis field changes.

## Glucose units and exact comparisons

The conversion factor **18.0 is acceptable** under the requested convention. The defect is `Math.round` in `toMgdl`, `src/engine/readiness.ts:22`, before clinical comparisons, while mg/dL input is kept unrounded. The same physical value can take a different branch.

The ordinary glucose thresholds, trend comparisons and UI ketone prompt use the resulting `mg`: `glucoseRules:230`, 236, 243, 253, 264, 267, 283 and 285; `ketoneRules:191`; `CheckInSheet.tsx:97`, 212 and 216. Raw-unit plausibility checks occur first at `glucoseSanity:30`. The clinician start target is a bare number compared as mg/dL at line 233; `profile.ts:51` does not document a unit. Make that target's unit explicit rather than accepting a mmol/L number there.

|---|---|---|---|
| 54 mg/dL | `54/18 = 3` | Both use level 1 amber/recheck | 53.9 mg/dL is red; `53.9/18` mmol/L becomes 54 and amber |
| 70 mg/dL | `70/18` | Both avoid biochemical hypo; insulin profile is held below default start minimum | 69.9 mg/dL is `low`, amber; `69.9/18` becomes 70 and `belowStart`, green with a timer and different carbohydrate instructions |

| 250 mg/dL | `250/18` | SGLT2/no ketones: both recovery, already wrong permission under the contract | 249.9 mg/dL is green; `249.9/18` becomes 250 and recovery |

The exact boundary arithmetic is defined by the selected factor. If the implementation chooses 18.016 instead, derive each mmol/L counterpart from that factor and keep it consistent. Do not use independently rounded published display equivalents as mathematically identical comparisons.

Accepted numerical input is not restricted by the engine to integer mg/dL or one-decimal mmol/L. The sheet's `step='0.1'` at `CheckInSheet.tsx:178` does not validate the engine interface or all submission precision. These decimal examples therefore expose actual accepted-input defects; they are not claims about every meter's display resolution.

Additional unit/validity gaps:

* A runtime missing unit falls into the mmol/L `else` branch in both `glucoseSanity` and `toMgdl`; TypeScript's union is not runtime validation. Reject unknown units instead of inferring them.
* A reliable 0.9 mmol/L value is rejected by `glucoseSanity:42` and produces green for a low-risk profile if passed directly to the engine, despite representing a very low glucose. The sheet stops submission, but does not replace it with the required safe low-reading assessment.
* Exactly 600 is accepted, above 600 is rejected in mg/dL, while up to 34 mmol/L is accepted at `glucoseSanity:41`. Plausibility boundaries differ and contain no extreme-high escalation.
* Urine ketones need categorical interpretation, not the synthetic interpolation at `ketonesMmol:152`. Urine acetoacetate concentrations/categories and blood ketone readings are not interchangeable glucose units.

## Release, freshness and mode permission

`DailyCheckIn`, `src/types/checkin.ts:41`, has a calendar date but no measurement time, rescue/recheck sequence or event resolution. `planFor`, `src/hooks/useGuided.ts:26`, reuses that date's check-in. `saveCheckIn`, line 52, replaces the date's record; same-day prior observations are not passed as history. `recent` in `evaluateCheckIn` affects sleep only.

The existing timer offers some useful protection: merely letting it expire does not expose Start while `recheckMinutes` remains. However, `CheckInSheet.tsx:267` allows “Enter a new reading” before expiry, leaves the entered value intact, and `submitBlocked`, `form.ts:27`, checks only presence, not a newly measured value/time. It cannot prove the specified wait, fresh measurement, symptom recovery or clinician release. A recovered level 2 episode can be erased by “Change answers” and a normal new number.

Fresh starts in `SessionPage.tsx:93` also honour the recheck flag. Resumes have a separate, source-proven gap: a still-resumable same-day saved green plan plus a subsequently saved `urgentSymptoms=true` check-in selects the old plan at `SessionPage.tsx:52`. The no-session check at line 79 tests the saved plan, and the recheck guard explicitly requires no `resumeState`. `Player` at line 132 passes that plan to `useGuidedSession`, whose options at `src/hooks/useGuidedSession.ts:23` have no current readiness input. The person can therefore reach resumed-session controls despite the new emergency. This route was inspected statically, not exercised in a browser. Preserving the original plan and workout date is useful; current safety permission must be reevaluated separately on resume and when reported conditions change.

The contract does not support inventing a universal expiry of a certain number of minutes for every reading. Add measurement time/source and enforce the treatment plan's event-based pre-session/recheck requirements. A morning check-in alone cannot prove that a required check occurred before a later session. Keep emergency flags independent of freshness and profile edits.

Mode permission needs to preserve both global blocks and existing filtering. A foot flag can allow safe reviewed seated activity while refusing Walk. Severe-eye flags can exclude head-down/straining movements without refusing all movement. `evaluateFlags` and `pickModality` already embody parts of that distinction. Conversely, no “safe” floor clip or walk should be offered for emergency, today or unresolved required-data holds. General `clearance='vigorous'` is not evidence of permission to exercise at a particular new glucose, BP or neurological event.

## Where the contract needs clarification

These are proposed reconciliations, not edits to the existing research file.

1. **The 600 equivalence is rounded.** My contract says 600 mg/dL is equivalent to 33.3 mmol/L. With the engine's factor 18, 33.3 equals 599.4, and the exact counterpart is `600/18`. Use the canonical threshold with unrounded conversion; the display approximation must not become an independently coded exact threshold.
2. **Reported severity and assistance need separate states.** Historical level 3, a currently alert person who needs assistance, and unconsciousness/unsafe swallowing must not share an unqualified no-oral rule. The contract's stop/review remains; emergency rescue and oral permission follow present ability. Likewise, historical bilateral sciatica is not new bilateral neurological deterioration.
3. **A first still-low 15 minute recheck is a conservative session policy.** ADA supports repeating rescue treatment and reevaluating treatment after level 2/3; it does not mandate same-day clinician contact after every single level 1 rescue loop. The contract deliberately ends that session at this point. Agree that product rule explicitly before asserting that the existing repeat loop is a guideline violation.
4. **The >160/>100 hold is also conservative app policy.** The cited ACSM handout advises a provider check before exercise above 160/100. It is not a universal emergency or proof that every ordinary gentle activity is unsafe. A recorded applicable plan can permit an option; generic clearance is insufficient.
5. **Do not make H-DATA a glucose mandate for all diabetes.** Missing optional glucose on confirmed low-hypo-risk treatment can appropriately remain nonblocking. Unknown treatment or a missing required safety check is different. The current low-risk no-reading branch is correct when the low-risk classification is actually known.
6. **Keep useful additional restrictions.** Lower intensity at exactly 160/100, no intervals/heavy lifts at 140/90, caution with asymptomatic low BP, and stronger eye/foot filtering can safely remain as explicit conservative policies. The engine's red branch at diastolic ≥110 is an exercise hold, not an emergency diagnosis. Existing local research associates the 180/110 restriction with resistance training; do not silently broaden that claim to every mode.
7. **Absolute pain bands do not prove clinical safety.** The older [back research](../research/back-sciatica-mobility.md) at line 82 explicitly calls its tendon pain-monitoring model an extrapolation to low back pain. Conservative comfort adjustments can be useful, but neither those bands nor the loading ladder may defer new weakness, acute systemic red flags or deterioration review. Fixed clinical pain permission cutoffs remain **Unsourced — do not ship**.
8. **Stricter ketone emergency boundaries are deliberate.** The contract's blood ≥3.0 and urine ≥2+ emergency policy is more conservative than the NHS public thresholds quoted alongside it. The ordinary blood engine `>=3` matches that policy; it is not a bug to be changed to strict `>3`. Preserve urine's actual scale before making the same comparison.

## Existing tests and their limits

The existing tests establish several real strengths: 5.5 mmol/L and 99 mg/dL equivalence at `readiness.test.ts:24`; timer holds at line 84; clinician start targets at line 89; independent urgent flags at line 254; cauda flags surviving **direct engine** profile edits at line 331; and elevated ketones at normal/no glucose at lines 316/321.

Some assertions intentionally preserve behaviour that conflicts with the newer contract: missing insulin reading is amber at `readiness.test.ts:109`; absent high-glucose ketones permits recovery at line 135; standing dizziness is amber at line 151; and new neurological symptoms permit recovery at line 204. Passing those assertions would not establish compliance with the new contract.

There are no assertions in that file for all requested exact/near unit boundaries, low glucose combined with emergency ketones, 600 emergency escalation, raw BP averaging loss, current unsafe swallowing, emergency submission blocked by invalid glucose, or sticky post-low release. The form tests retain saved BP at `src/components/checkin/form.test.ts:24` and check presence of a recheck reading at line 38, but do not validate those emergency/form-history paths. This is a coverage gap, not a claim that the unrun suite failed.
